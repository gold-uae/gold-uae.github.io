Who Sets the Gold Rate in Dubai? From World Spot Price to the Khaleej Times Gold Rate

Type "khaleej times gold rate" or "gulf news gold price" and you will find a table of per-gram figures, which leads many readers to assume a newspaper sets the price. It does not. The gold rate in Dubai starts life as the international spot price of gold in US dollars per troy ounce, is converted into dirhams at the fixed peg, and is published as a per-gram retail rate by the Dubai Gold & Jewellery Group, the trade body for the emirate's jewellers. Newspapers, apps and sites like this one then display that figure or a live equivalent.

This page follows the chain from the world market to the board outside a shop in Deira, and explains why two sources can show slightly different numbers on the same afternoon.

Quick answer

  • No single person sets the Dubai gold rate; it is derived from the international spot price.
  • The Dubai Gold & Jewellery Group publishes the daily retail per-gram rate that shops display.
  • Khaleej Times and Gulf News do not set a rate; they republish the same daily figure.
  • Small differences between sources come from timing, rounding and buy versus sell quotes.
  • The same rate applies in all seven emirates; shops add making charges on top.

Step one: the international spot price

Gold is one of the most heavily traded assets in the world, and its benchmark price is set by trading in the over-the-counter market centred on London, in futures on exchanges in the United States and Asia, and in regional hubs including Dubai's own commodities exchange. Banks, refiners, miners, central banks, funds and jewellery manufacturers all transact against that price. The result is a continuously quoted dollar price for a troy ounce of pure gold, known as the spot price.

No Dubai institution controls that figure. A jeweller in the Gold Souk is a price taker, exactly like a refinery in Switzerland or a bank in Singapore. When the spot price moves because of interest-rate expectations, the strength of the dollar, geopolitical tension or central-bank buying, the Dubai rate moves with it within minutes. The spot price in USD page tracks the ounce figure, and the price analysis page covers what tends to move it.

Step two: ounces to grams, dollars to dirhams

The conversion from the spot price to a Dubai shop rate is arithmetic, not judgement. There are two fixed constants involved.

Divide the dollar price per ounce by 31.1035, multiply by 3.6725, and you have the dirham value of one gram of pure gold. Scale by purity for the lower karats. Because the dirham is pegged, the AED rate rises and falls one-for-one with the dollar price; residents paid in rupees or pesos feel currency moves on top, which the rate in Indian rupees page illustrates.

Step three: the Dubai Gold &amp; Jewellery Group rate

The Dubai Gold & Jewellery Group is the trade association that brings together the emirate's retailers, wholesalers and manufacturers. Each day it publishes a retail per-gram rate for the main karats, derived from the prevailing spot price with a small margin for the trade, and updates it during the day as the market moves. That published figure is what shops across the UAE write on their boards and what the Dubai gold and jewellery group rate searches are looking for.

Why have a published rate at all, if it is just arithmetic on the spot price? Consistency. A common reference means a customer can walk between a hundred shops in Deira and see the same metal rate, which focuses competition on making charges and design rather than on confusing per-gram claims. It also gives wholesalers and retailers a shared basis for settling trades among themselves.

The group's rate is adopted well beyond Dubai. Jewellers in Abu Dhabi, Sharjah, Ajman and the northern emirates buy from the same wholesalers and use the same board figure, which is why our pages for Abu Dhabi and Sharjah show the same table.

Where Khaleej Times and Gulf News fit in

The UAE's English-language newspapers have run gold rate tables for decades, first in print and now online, and for many residents the Khaleej Times gold rate or the Gulf News gold price is the habitual morning check. Both publications report the rate; neither sets it. Their tables reproduce the trade-group figure, sometimes at a fixed time of day and sometimes with live feeds, and their news desks add context on why the market moved.

The same is true of the Arabic press. A search for "al khaleej gold rate" leads to the Arabic daily Al Khaleej and its own rate table, again republished rather than determined. Treat newspaper tables as a snapshot of the published rate at the moment they were updated, which is exactly how the shops treat them.

Why the numbers can differ slightly

  • Timing: one source updated at 09:00, another at 14:00, and the market moved in between.
  • Rounding: some tables round to the nearest quarter dirham, others to two decimals.
  • Buy versus sell: a few sources show what shops pay when buying back, which sits below the selling rate.
  • Live versus published: a live widget like ours tracks spot continuously; the board rate is updated in steps.

How to read any gold rate table

Whichever source you use, a rate table answers four questions. Which karat? Per gram or per ounce or per tola? Selling rate or buying rate? And when was it last updated? If a table does not say, assume it shows the shop selling rate per gram and look for the timestamp before trusting it for a purchase.

  1. Find the 24K line; everything else is a fraction of it.
  2. Check the unit. A per-tola figure is the gram rate multiplied by 11.6638.
  3. Note the update time and compare with the live spot movement if the market is busy.
  4. Remember the table shows metal only; add the shop's making charge and 5% VAT for a real bill.

For a worked example of turning a rate into a bill, the how to buy gold in Dubai page walks through a purchase line by line, and the making charges page explains the one part of the price that the rate table never shows.

Screenshot the board rate when you enter a shop. If the shop's quote per gram is above it, the difference is making charge and should be shown as such on the receipt.

Can a shop charge more or less than the published rate?

In principle a jeweller is free to price as it likes; in practice the published rate is a strong anchor. Selling the metal below it would mean a loss against the wholesaler's price, and selling above it is quickly noticed by customers who have just walked past a dozen other boards. What varies is everything around the rate: making charges, discounts offered in the Dubai Shopping Festival or before Diwali, buy-back terms and whether old gold is accepted at a fair rate in part exchange.

Some chains advertise a lower per-gram figure on specific days as a promotion. Read those carefully: the discount is usually on making charges or is tied to a minimum purchase, not a lower price for the gold itself. The jewellers and brands page describes how the larger retailers structure their offers, and the hallmark and purity page covers the other way a cheap-looking rate can mislead: a lower karat than stated.

Why this matters for the reader

Understanding where the rate comes from changes how you shop. You stop looking for a cheaper emirate or a cheaper newspaper and start comparing the only things that actually vary. You know that a rate quoted in the morning may not hold by evening, and that a live figure on your phone is a fair reference in any shop. You also know that no one in Dubai can tell you where the rate is going, because it is made by a global market that the city follows rather than leads.

Use the live board on this page or the live chart as your reference, treat newspaper tables as a daily snapshot, and spend your bargaining energy on the making charge. That is how the trade itself reads the numbers.

Questions people ask

Does Khaleej Times set the gold rate in Dubai?

No. Khaleej Times, Gulf News and other newspapers publish the daily rate for their readers but do not determine it. The figure in their tables is the retail per-gram rate published by the Dubai Gold & Jewellery Group, which in turn follows the international spot price converted to dirhams. Differences between papers come from update times and rounding.

What is the Dubai Gold & Jewellery Group rate?

It is the daily retail per-gram rate for 24K, 22K, 21K and 18K gold published by the trade association of Dubai's jewellers. It is derived from the prevailing world spot price and updated during the day. Shops across the UAE display it on their boards and use it as the metal component of every bill, adding making charges and VAT on top.

Why does the gold rate change several times a day?

Because the world spot price trades almost continuously from Monday morning in Asia to Friday evening in New York, and the Dubai rate is a direct conversion of that price. When the market moves enough, the published rate is revised and shop boards follow. A live widget tracks the movement continuously, while printed and newspaper tables show a snapshot.

Is the gold rate the same in every emirate?

Yes. Jewellers in Abu Dhabi, Sharjah, Ajman, Al Ain, Fujairah and Ras Al Khaimah source from the same wholesalers and use the same published per-gram rate as Dubai. The only differences a shopper will find between emirates are in making charges, design range and the bargaining culture of individual shops.

Why is the buying rate lower than the selling rate?

When a shop buys gold from the public it offers slightly less than the board selling rate to cover testing, refining and its own margin. Some rate tables show both figures. The gap is normal and modest for pure gold, but it widens for jewellery with stones, where the stone weight is deducted, and the making charge you originally paid is never refunded.
