Gold Rate in Abu Dhabi Today: Live 22K and 21K Price per Gram

People in the capital often assume they are paying a premium compared with Dubai. For the metal, they are not: today's gold rate in Abu Dhabi is the national per-gram figure shown on the live board below, derived from the dollar spot price and the fixed dirham peg. What Abu Dhabi does have is its own geography, a different mix of buyers and a slightly different rhythm to how shops price making charges.

This page gives you the live rate for every karat, then walks through the Madinat Zayed Gold Centre, Hamdan Street and the malls, with a worked example for a gold ring so you can judge any quote.

Shops in Madinat Zayed, on Hamdan Street and in the malls display these same figures, usually on a screen by the door. The number may trail ours by an hour or two on a busy day, because most counters refresh their board a few times daily rather than continuously. That lag is harmless in a calm market and worth noticing in a fast one.

The capital's boards put 21K and 22K side by side with equal prominence, which tells you something about who shops here: a large Emirati and wider Gulf clientele asking for 21K, and an equally large South Asian community asking for 22K. The 24K row serves the bar and coin buyers, and 18K covers the diamond and Italian pieces in the mall boutiques. If you want a single karat explained in depth, the 22K page and the 21K page each carry their own live card.

Abu Dhabi versus Dubai: is the drive worth it?

The honest answer is: not for the rate, sometimes for the range. The per-gram price is identical, because the Dubai reference rate is adopted by the trade nationwide and the dirham peg leaves no room for a local variation. A 10-gram 22K bangle has the same metal value on Hamdan Street as in Deira.

Where Dubai wins is sheer volume. The Deira souk and its covered extension hold more designs in one square kilometre than the whole of Abu Dhabi, and that density pushes making charges on plain 22K work a little lower. Where Abu Dhabi wins is calm: no tourist crush, shopkeepers who expect repeat customers rather than one-off visitors, and a strong stock of Gulf-style 21K sets that Dubai's Indian-focused counters carry less of.

A sensible rule many residents use: buy everyday pieces and repairs locally, and make the trip to Dubai only for a large bridal order where comparing a dozen counters in an afternoon can shave the making charge meaningfully. Gold shops in Abu Dhabi: Madinat Zayed, Hamdan Street and the malls

The capital's gold trade is organised around one anchor and several satellites.

Madinat Zayed Shopping Centre and Gold Centre

This is Abu Dhabi's answer to the Deira souk: a purpose-built centre in the heart of the old city where dozens of jewellers trade under one roof, air-conditioned, with a dedicated gold section. It is the place for 21K bridal sets, 22K Indian designs, bars and coins, and for getting three quotes on the same piece without walking outside in August. Parking is tight at weekends; weekday mornings are easiest.

Hamdan Street and the surrounding blocks

Street-front jewellers, many of them long-established family businesses, strong on 21K Arab designs and repairs. Prices on making charges are negotiable and the owners are often on the premises.

The malls

Al Wahda, Mushrif, Dalma, Yas and Marina malls host the big chain branches and brand boutiques: Malabar Gold, Joyalukkas, Damas and similar names. Fixed-price, promotion-driven, excellent for gifting and for 18K diamond work, and the obvious choice if you want a receipt from a brand you recognise.

For residents in Mussafah and Khalifa City, the neighbourhood jewellers in Shabiya and the community malls cover plain 22K and repairs at honest margins. The gold shops near me page helps you find the nearest counter, and best jewellers and brands explains what each chain is known for.

Gold ring price in Abu Dhabi: a worked example

Rings are the most searched piece in the capital, usually for an engagement or an Eid gift, so here is how a quote is built without quoting a price that will be stale tomorrow.

  1. Weight. A plain ladies' band in 21K typically weighs three to five grams; a men's signet or a stone-set ring can be eight grams or more. The shop weighs it in front of you.
  2. Metal value. Multiply the weight by the karat row on the board. Our calculator does it instantly for any weight and karat.
  3. Making charge. Rings carry a higher charge per gram than chains because the work is fiddly; stone-set designs higher still. Expect a per-gram fee for plain bands and a percentage for designer pieces.
  4. Stones. Diamonds or coloured stones are priced separately and are not part of the gold weight. Ask for the stone weight and grade in writing.
  5. VAT at 5% on the total.
For an engagement ring you plan to resize, choose a plain band in 21K or 22K rather than a heavily patterned one. Any Hamdan Street jeweller can resize it in an afternoon, and plain gold holds its resale value because there is no making charge to lose.

The capital is one of the few places where this is a real question for everyday buyers, because both karats are stocked deeply.

Neither is "better". If you plan to sell or exchange in India one day, buy 22K, because that is the standard there. If the piece will be worn daily in the Gulf and possibly exchanged in a Gulf shop, 21K is the natural choice and the making charges on Arab designs are often friendlier. Malabar Gold, Joyalukkas and the chains in Abu Dhabi

Searches for "Malabar Gold Abu Dhabi" are among the most common gold queries in the capital, and the reason is trust: Indian families know the brand from home and like the fixed, printed pricing. The large chains have branches in the main malls and on Hamdan Street, and they publish the day's rate on their own boards, which matches the national figure.

What the chains offer that a small souk counter may not: a printed invoice with weight, karat and making charge itemised, buy-back and exchange schemes at the full board rate for their own pieces, certified hallmarks, and the ability to exchange or repair at a branch in Kerala or Karachi. What they charge for it: a higher making charge on plain pieces than an independent in Madinat Zayed, offset by frequent promotions during Eid, Diwali, Onam and the summer sales.

Independents win on negotiation and on unusual Gulf designs; chains win on paperwork and peace of mind. Many Abu Dhabi residents use both: a chain for a gift that needs a certificate, an independent for a heavy plain bangle bought mainly as savings. The making charges page shows what to expect from each.

Selling and exchanging gold in Abu Dhabi

When it is time to sell, the live board again sets the number: shops in Madinat Zayed and on Hamdan Street buy 21K and 22K at the day's gram rate minus a margin, for the metal only. Take the piece to two or three counters, let each test and weigh it, and accept the best figure; the spread between offers is usually small but not nothing.

Exchange is often the better deal. Most jewellers, chains especially, will value your old gold at or near the full board rate when you put the proceeds towards a new piece, which effectively refunds the margin you would lose on a cash sale. Keep the original invoice; it speeds up the test and improves the offer.

If you need cash but want the gold back later, a gold loan from a licensed finance company is the alternative, with the jewellery held as security. The selling guide applies equally in Abu Dhabi, and the gold loan page explains how the loan-to-value and fees typically work.

Questions people ask

What is the gold rate in Abu Dhabi today for 22K?

The live 22K gram rate is in the board at the top of the page and refreshes as the international price moves. It equals 91.6% of the 24K figure, because 22K is 916 parts per thousand gold. Every shop in Abu Dhabi, from Madinat Zayed to the Yas Mall boutiques, works from this same number; only the making charge differs.

Is gold cheaper in Abu Dhabi or Dubai?

The metal rate is identical in both cities. Dubai's Deira souk tends to have slightly lower making charges on plain 22K pieces because of the density of competing counters, while Abu Dhabi's Madinat Zayed is strong on 21K Gulf designs and is far less crowded. For most everyday purchases the saving from driving to Dubai is smaller than the fuel and time; for a large bridal order it may be worth it.

Where is the best place to buy gold in Abu Dhabi?

Madinat Zayed Gold Centre is the obvious first stop: many jewellers under one roof, all karats, bars and coins, and easy comparison. Hamdan Street suits buyers who want a family jeweller and Arab designs. The malls host the chain brands for certified, fixed-price shopping. Visit on a weekday morning for parking and patience.

How much is a gold ring in Abu Dhabi?

It depends on weight and karat, which is why we do not print a figure. A plain 21K band of around four grams costs four times the 21K gram rate in metal, plus a making charge for the work, plus 5% VAT. Stone-set rings add the stone price separately. Use the calculator with the weight the shop shows you, then compare its quote with that number.

Does Malabar Gold in Abu Dhabi use a different rate?

No. Malabar Gold, Joyalukkas, Damas and the other chains display the national per-gram rate on their own boards and it matches the figure on this page. Their prices differ from independents only in the making charge and in promotional discounts, so compare the itemised invoice rather than the headline rate.

Can tourists in Abu Dhabi claim VAT back on gold?

Yes, on jewellery, through the same Planet refund scheme used in Dubai. Ask the shop for a tax-free tag at the time of purchase, keep the invoice, and present the goods at the refund desk at Abu Dhabi airport before departure. Investment bars and coins are zero-rated already, so there is no VAT to reclaim on them.
