Gold Rate in UAE Today by Emirate: One Price, Seven Markets

Searching for the gold rate in Abu Dhabi, Sharjah or Ajman separately is a habit from countries where every city has its own bullion association. The UAE does not work that way: a single per-gram rate, derived from the international spot price, is displayed on shop boards from Al Ras in Deira to the Al Ain Souk. The live board below is that rate.

What this section adds is the local layer. Each emirate has its own gold district, its own mix of buyers and its own quirks on making charges, and knowing them is worth more than any imaginary price gap.

Quick answer

  • The gold rate in the UAE is one national figure per gram; Dubai, Abu Dhabi and Sharjah do not quote different metal prices.
  • The figure comes from the US dollar spot price converted at the fixed dirham peg, updated through the day.
  • What changes from emirate to emirate is making charges, selection and how hard shops compete.
  • This hub routes you to each emirate's page, to the pages that explain the rate, and to rates in rupees.

Why there is no cheaper emirate for the metal itself

Three facts remove the price difference between cities. The dirham is pegged to the dollar at a fixed rate, so no emirate gets a better exchange rate. Gold enters the country duty-free, so there is no regional customs layer. And the reference rate published in Dubai is adopted by the trade everywhere, because a wholesaler in Deira supplies retailers in Fujairah and Ras Al Khaimah from the same stock at the same price.

So if someone tells you gold is cheaper in Sharjah, what they usually mean is one of three things:

  • Making charges in Sharjah's Rolla area are lower on plain 21K and 22K pieces, because rents are lower and the clientele is more price-sensitive.
  • A specific shop was running a promotion the day they visited.
  • They compared a plain bangle in one city with a designer piece in another.

Compare like with like: the same karat, a similar weight, and the making charge quoted as a separate line. Our making charges guide gives you the vocabulary, and the gold calculator gives you the metal value to measure every quote against.

The big three: Dubai, Abu Dhabi and Sharjah

Most of the country's gold changes hands in three cities, and each has a distinct character.

Dubai

The deepest market in the Gulf: the Deira Gold Souk and its covered extension, Meena Bazaar in Bur Dubai, the Karama shops favoured by South Asian families, and the Gold & Diamond Park on Sheikh Zayed Road for made-to-order work. Best selection, hardest bargaining, and the base for every brand's flagship. Start with gold rate in Dubai today.

Abu Dhabi

Calmer and more Emirati in taste. Madinat Zayed Shopping Centre and its Gold Centre, plus the Hamdan Street shops, carry a heavier share of 21K Gulf designs and bridal sets. Prices on making charges are fair but there is less walk-in competition than in Deira. See gold rate in Abu Dhabi today.

Sharjah

The value hunter's city. The Central (Blue) Souk is the tourist face; the Rolla and Al Shuwaihean streets are where residents buy weight at thin margins. Open gold rate in Sharjah today.

The other four: Ajman, Al Ain, Fujairah and Ras Al Khaimah

Smaller markets, but each has a reason to exist, and residents of these emirates should not assume they must drive to Dubai.

  • Ajman. The souk area near Al Bustan has a compact gold row that serves Ajman and northern Sharjah; many Rolla traders have a second counter here. Low rents keep making charges competitive. Read gold rate in Ajman.
  • Al Ain. The garden city's gold trade sits around the Al Ain Souk and the older town centre, serving a largely Emirati and Omani clientele that favours 21K and traditional pieces. Read gold rate in Al Ain.
  • Fujairah. A handful of jewellers in the main souk and the malls; good for repairs and plain 22K, thin on designer stock. Read gold rate in Fujairah.
  • Ras Al Khaimah. Gold shops cluster in the old town souk and Al Nakheel area, with Dubai wholesalers supplying them weekly. Read gold rate in Ras Al Khaimah.

In all four, the rate is the national board; what you gain is convenience and, often, a jeweller who remembers your name.

Reading an emirate's gold board like a local

Boards look alike from Deira to Fujairah, which makes them easy to read once you know the layout. The left column lists karats, usually 24, 22, 21 and 18, sometimes 14. The next column is the selling rate per gram; many boards add a buying rate, which is what the shop pays you for scrap and is a little lower. A third line sometimes shows the ounce in dollars or the kilo bar price for traders.

Two habits save money. First, check the board's timestamp or ask when it was last changed, especially after a volatile US session. Second, remember that the board never includes making charges, so a 25-gram 21K set is not board rate times twenty-five; it is that figure plus the workmanship plus 5%. The karat explained page covers the fineness stamps you will see on the piece itself, and the hallmark guide tells you how to confirm the karat matches the board row you were charged for.

And if the shop is in a mall rather than a souk, expect the same board but a higher making charge; you are paying for the air-conditioning and the parking, which on a July afternoon may be worth it.

Questions people ask

Which emirate has the lowest gold rate today?

None of them. The per-gram metal rate is a single national figure derived from the dollar spot price, so Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Sharjah and the rest display the same number. Differences you notice in final prices come from making charges, promotions and the type of piece, which is why comparing two quotes line by line matters more than choosing a city.

Is the UAE gold rate per gram or per tola?

Shops quote per gram, and the board on the wall is in dirhams per gram for each karat. Buyers from Pakistan and India often think in tolas, and most jewellers will happily convert: one tola is 11.6638 grams, so multiply the gram rate by that. Our calculator handles both units so you never have to do the sum at the counter.

Why does the newspaper gold rate differ from the live rate?

Newspapers and their websites publish the rate at a fixed time, typically the morning opening figure from the Dubai Gold & Jewellery Group. The live board here updates continuously from the spot price, so by afternoon the two can differ, sometimes noticeably on a volatile day. Neither is wrong; they are snapshots taken at different moments.

Do gold shops in the UAE negotiate on the rate?

Not on the metal rate, which is fixed by the board, but almost always on the making charge, and sometimes on the wastage or stone charges attached to a piece. In souk areas such as Deira, Rolla and Madinat Zayed, a polite request for a better making charge is expected rather than rude. Mall branches of big chains have less flexibility but run frequent promotions instead.

Does the gold rate differ on Friday or at the weekend in the UAE?

International trading winds down on Friday evening New York time and reopens on Monday morning in Asia, so the UAE rate is effectively frozen from Saturday until Monday. Shops stay open at the weekend and simply use the last published figure. If major news breaks over the weekend, Monday's board can open with a visible gap.
