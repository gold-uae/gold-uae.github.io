Gold Rate in Ras Al Khaimah Today: RAK Gold Rate Per Gram and Where to Buy

Ras Al Khaimah is the UAE's northernmost emirate, a mix of an old creek-side town, new resorts on Al Marjan Island and a large industrial workforce around Al Hamra and the cement and ceramics plants. The gold rate in Ras Al Khaimah today, whichever part of the emirate you are in, is the same per-gram dirham rate used in Dubai, because the whole country prices from one daily reference that follows the international spot price. The live board below shows it for every karat.

RAK's gold trade is modest in size but busy, with strong demand from Emirati families, a sizeable Pakistani and Indian community and visitors heading north to the mountains and beaches. This page covers where to buy, how local shops price and what to watch on the receipt.

Quick answer

  • The RAK gold rate is the national UAE per-gram rate, updated live from the world spot price.
  • Ras Al Khaimah's gold shops sit in the old town and Al Nakheel markets and in the big malls.
  • 22K and 21K dominate; 18K is in the malls; 24K is mostly small bars.
  • Making charges and VAT, not the metal rate, decide the final bill.
  • The tola is still a common unit among RAK's Pakistani community: 1 tola = 11.6638 g.

Shops in Ras Al Khaimah copy this rate onto their boards and refresh it during the day. In a market with a limited number of jewellers, a few boards update less frequently than in Deira, so you may notice a small gap between the live table and a shop's figure during a volatile session. The shop bills at its current board rate, so if the market is moving quickly it is worth glancing at the live number on your phone before you agree a price.

For the Pakistani and Indian residents who think in tolas, the gold calculator converts weight in grams or tolas into a dirham value for any karat, and the rate in PKR page shows the same gold in rupees.

Where the Ras Al Khaimah gold rate comes from

There is no committee in RAK setting a gold price. The Dubai Gold & Jewellery Group publishes a per-gram rate each day that traders and retailers throughout the UAE adopt as their benchmark, and RAK's jewellers, who source their stock from Dubai and Sharjah wholesalers, use it like everyone else. The benchmark itself is simply the global spot price translated into dirhams, which is why it changes several times a day.

Because the UAE dirham is pegged to the US dollar, the conversion carries no exchange-rate noise. A move of one percent in the dollar price of gold is a move of one percent in the RAK gold rate. The page on who sets the Dubai gold rate explains the mechanics, and the spot price in USD page lets you watch the dollar figure that sits behind the dirham one.

The point for shoppers is clear: you are not going to find a cheaper gram of gold by driving to another emirate, and anyone quoting a rate noticeably below the live figure is either adding the difference elsewhere or not telling you the karat honestly.

Ras Al Khaimah gold souk and shops: a map in words

Searches for "ras al khaimah gold souk" expect a single market, but the emirate's gold trade is spread across three kinds of location.

Old town and Al Nakheel

The traditional markets on either side of the creek, in the old RAK town and in Al Nakheel, have rows of independent jewellers. These shops carry 21K Gulf sets, 22K South Asian pieces and bridal collections, and they are the places where making charges can be discussed. Many have been in the same family for decades.

The malls

The national chains have counters in the larger malls of the emirate, offering fixed making charges, catalogue designs, more 18K and diamond work, and a printed invoice with an exchange policy. They are where resort visitors and residents of the Al Hamra area tend to shop.

Neighbourhood shops

Smaller jewellers in residential and industrial districts serve the workforce, often trading in 22K pieces bought a few grams at a time at month end. They are convenient but carry limited stock, so compare their making charges with a trip to the old town. For a broader view of markets across the country, the gold souks hub is the place to start.

Reading a RAK jeweller's bill

Consider a 21K bridal set weighing 48 grams bought in the old town. The metal value is 48 multiplied by the live 21K rate. On top comes the making charge, which for a worked set with multiple pieces is usually quoted as a percentage of the metal value rather than a flat per-gram sum. VAT at 5% is then applied to metal plus making charge. Any stones are listed separately, since they are not sold by gold weight.

The pattern to check is that the receipt lists weight, karat, rate, making charge and VAT on separate lines. A single blended per-gram figure is not wrong, but it hides how much of the price is labour and makes it impossible to compare the shop next door. Ask for the breakdown; the making charges page shows what a fair percentage for a set looks like against a flat charge for a plain bangle.

Ask the shop to weigh the set with and without its detachable stones or clasp. The difference tells you how much of what you are paying for is gold, and the honest shops in RAK will do it without being asked twice.

Much of Ras Al Khaimah's gold is bought by people who grew up pricing in tolas. A tola is 11.6638 grams, so a shop quoting per gram is simply using a smaller unit; the value of the metal does not change.

When RAK gets busy, and when it does not

Demand in Ras Al Khaimah peaks in the days before Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha, during the winter wedding months, and around the month-end salary cycle in the industrial areas. Diwali brings a surge in the Indian community, and the mall counters see tourists on weekends during the cooler season when the resorts are full. In those windows, with relatively few shops to go round, making charges stay firm and queues form at the busiest counters.

The gold rate itself is unaffected by any of this; it moves with world markets. If you are buying for a wedding months away, some families prefer to buy a little at a time rather than wait for a single perfect day, since no one can predict the rate. The price history page shows how the dirham rate has behaved over recent months and years, which is a better guide than any forecast. For those who want gold as savings without a making charge, the gold bars page explains sizes and premiums.

Tourists, Oman visitors and VAT refunds

Ras Al Khaimah's resorts bring in holidaymakers who sometimes buy gold before flying home from Dubai or Sharjah. Jewellery carries 5% VAT, which tourists can reclaim at the airport under the refund scheme if the shop issued a tax-free form, the purchase met the minimum and the goods leave within ninety days. The mall chains are reliably registered; some old town shops are not, so ask first. The process is covered on the VAT and tax refund page.

RAK also borders Oman's Musandam governorate, and visitors from across that border shop in the emirate for the duty-free pricing. Whether you are heading to Oman, Pakistan or India, keep the itemised receipt for customs, and check the allowance for your destination before buying a large quantity.

Selling gold in Ras Al Khaimah

The old town jewellers buy gold from the public, as do some mall chains for their own pieces. The offer is based on pure gold content, tested and weighed in front of you, at a figure a little below the live buying rate. Making charges are not returned, and stone weight is deducted. With a limited number of buyers in the emirate it is worth collecting two or three quotes and, for a large sale, adding a quote from a Dubai buyer, since the drive can pay for itself on a big lot.

If you need cash but want to keep your jewellery, a gold-backed loan is an alternative offered by several banks and finance houses; the gold loan page explains valuation and repayment. For the outright sale route, the selling guide applies in RAK exactly as it does in Dubai.

Questions people ask

Is the gold rate in RAK the same as in Dubai?

Yes. Ras Al Khaimah's jewellers use the national per-gram rate that follows the international spot price converted into dirhams at the fixed peg, the same rate shown on boards in Deira. What differs between shops is the making charge, the range of designs and the willingness to negotiate, never the price of the metal itself.

Where are the gold shops in Ras Al Khaimah?

The traditional jewellers are in the old town market and the Al Nakheel area on either side of the creek. The national chains have counters in the larger malls, and small neighbourhood shops serve the residential and industrial districts. For choice and negotiable making charges, start in the old town; for branded 18K and warranties, try the malls.

Do RAK shops price in tolas or grams?

Boards show the rate per gram, as everywhere in the UAE, but shops serving Pakistani and Indian customers will happily quote per tola on request. One tola equals 11.6638 grams, so a tola price is just the gram rate multiplied by that figure. The metal value is identical; only the unit changes.

Which karat should I buy in Ras Al Khaimah?

It depends on who will wear it and whether you plan to resell. 22K holds more pure gold per gram and is the South Asian standard; 21K is the Gulf preference and slightly harder for daily wear; 18K suits stone-set and lightweight pieces. For pure savings, a small 24K bar avoids making charges and is zero-rated for VAT.

Can I get a tourist VAT refund on gold bought in RAK?

Yes, provided the shop is registered with the refund scheme and issues a tax-free form when you pay. You validate it at the departure airport and the goods must leave the UAE within ninety days. Mall chains are generally registered; confirm with smaller old town shops before buying.
