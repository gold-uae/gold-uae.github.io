Dubai Gold Rate in Pakistani Rupees, Nepali Rupees and Sri Lankan Rupees Today

Walk through the gold rows of Rolla in Sharjah or the shops around Al Bustan in Ajman and you will hear prices discussed in tolas as often as grams. Pakistani, Nepali, Sri Lankan, Bangladeshi and Filipino residents make up a large share of the UAE's gold buyers, and each community measures the Dubai rate against a home price in its own currency and its own unit. This page converts the live Dubai rate into Pakistani rupees, Nepali rupees, Sri Lankan rupees, taka and pesos, explains how each home market builds its price, and sets out the unit conversions so a tola in Lahore and a gram in Deira can finally be compared on equal terms.

Quick answer

  • The converters below show Dubai's per-gram rate in PKR, NPR, LKR, BDT and PHP using live exchange rates.
  • Pakistan, Nepal and Bangladesh think in tolas or bhori, about 11.66 grams; multiply the gram rate by 11.6638.
  • Sri Lanka quotes 22K gold per pawn of 8 grams, so divide the home figure by eight before comparing.
  • Home-country rates include import duty and local taxes that Dubai does not charge, which is the whole saving.
  • A fall in your home currency lifts the home gold price even when the dollar price of gold is unchanged.

To get a per-tola figure, multiply the per-gram number by 11.6638. A reader comparing with a Karachi or Lahore sarafa board should check two things first: that the home quote is for 24K and not 22K or 21K, and whether it is per tola or per 10 grams, since both appear side by side in Pakistani listings.

Pakistani buyers in Dubai lean towards 21K and 22K, and plain bangle sets and chains are the typical purchase at the Sharjah souk and in Deira. The Dubai advantage over Pakistan comes from the absence of import duty and from a currency that does not slide, which is why many families time their purchases to visits by relatives.

Gold price in Nepal today compared with Dubai

Nepal has one of the most tola-centred gold cultures anywhere. The daily rate is announced by the national jewellers' federation in Kathmandu, quoted per tola for fine gold and for a lower-purity standard used in traditional jewellery. The Nepali rupee is itself fixed to the Indian rupee, so the Nepal gold price follows the Indian one closely and carries Nepal's own import duty on top.

Gold price in Sri Lanka: the pawn and the Dubai gram

Sri Lanka quotes 22K gold per pawn, which is eight grams, and sometimes per sovereign, an old coin weight of about the same size. The rate is published by the jewellers' trade in Colombo and reflects the rupee's exchange rate and Sri Lanka's import regime. Because the Sri Lankan rupee has had volatile years, the home price in rupees can swing far more than the dollar price of gold.

Why home-country rates drift away from the Dubai rate

If the metal costs the same everywhere, why is the gap between Dubai and Islamabad or Colombo different every month? Three forces move independently of gold itself.

  1. Exchange rates. Every home price is the dollar price multiplied by the local currency rate. When the rupee, taka or peso weakens, the home price rises with no change in gold. The UAE never feels this because the dirham is fixed.
  2. Import duty and taxes. Each country sets its own duty on gold imports and its own sales tax on jewellery, and governments adjust them, sometimes sharply, to manage trade deficits.
  3. Local supply and smuggling premiums. When official imports are restricted, the street price can carry an extra premium that does not exist in a market like Dubai where supply is open.

A useful habit is to check the spot price in dollars first. If it is flat and your home rate still jumped, the move is about your currency or your government, not about gold. That tells you whether to hurry or to wait, and it is the same logic behind the Indian rupee comparison.

Carrying gold home: limits, receipts and sensible weights

The conversion only matters if the gold reaches home legally and cheaply. Rules differ by country and change without much notice, so this section gives principles rather than figures.

  • Each of Pakistan, Nepal, Sri Lanka, Bangladesh and the Philippines sets its own allowance for jewellery carried by returning residents, often distinguishing worn jewellery from new items in boxes, and treating bars and coins more strictly. Check the customs website of your country before the trip.
  • Keep the Dubai invoice with weight, karat and price. It is your proof of value for customs and your proof of purity for any jeweller at home.
  • Carry gold in hand luggage and declare it where required. The penalty for an undeclared excess is always worse than the duty.
  • Prefer weights that sit comfortably within the allowance: a few bangles or one chain rather than a heavy set, and buy over several trips if the family needs more.
If you shop at the end of a Dubai Shopping Festival or Eid promotion, ask for the zero-making-charge price on plain 21K or 22K pieces. For a buyer who will measure value in tolas back home, a piece with no making charge is almost pure metal and keeps the best resale value.

How those making charges work, and when they really are zero, is covered on the making charges page.

Questions people ask

What is the Dubai gold rate in Pakistani rupees today?

The PKR converter near the top of this page shows the live Dubai per-gram rate in Pakistani rupees. Multiply it by 11.6638 for a per-tola figure. The result is what you would pay to buy in Dubai, converted at today's exchange rate; it excludes any Pakistani duty on arrival and is lower than the Karachi rate mainly because Dubai charges no import duty.

How much is 1 tola of gold in Dubai?

One tola is 11.6638 grams, so the tola price is the Dubai per-gram rate for your chosen karat multiplied by 11.6638. Shops in the UAE sell by the gram, but any jeweller in Deira, Rolla or Ajman will quote a tola if you ask, and 24K tola bars are a common savings purchase among South Asian residents.

Why is the gold price in Nepal higher than in Dubai?

Nepal's price includes import duty and reflects the Nepali rupee, which is tied to the Indian rupee and has weakened against the dollar over the years. Dubai charges no duty, has a fixed currency and a very competitive market. The metal itself costs the same in both places on the world market.

How do I compare the gold price in Sri Lanka with Dubai?

Sri Lanka quotes 22K gold per pawn of eight grams. Divide the Colombo figure by eight to get a per-gram 22K price in Sri Lankan rupees, then compare it with the converted Dubai 22K rate shown on this page. The gap you see is duty, tax and local margin plus the effect of the rupee's exchange rate.

Is it worth buying gold in Dubai to take to Bangladesh or the Philippines?

Often yes for jewellery that fits within your country's customs allowance, because Dubai's price carries no import duty and the purity is reliably stamped. The saving shrinks if you must pay duty on arrival or if the piece has heavy making charges. Compare the full bill, not only the metal rate, and check the current customs rule before you buy.
