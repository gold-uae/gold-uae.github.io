Gold Rate in Al Ain Today: Per-Gram Price, Al Ain Gold Souk and Where to Buy

Al Ain is the UAE's garden city, a two-hour drive inland from both Abu Dhabi and Dubai, and it has its own long-standing gold trade. The gold rate in Al Ain, though, is not a local number. Jewellers here price from the same daily UAE reference rate as every other emirate, which follows the international spot price converted into dirhams. The board below shows that live rate per gram for 24K, 22K, 21K and 18K.

What makes Al Ain distinctive is its customer base. The city is home to a large Emirati population and sits on the border with Oman, so the gold souk leans heavily towards 21K Gulf-style pieces and does brisk trade on weekends with shoppers crossing from Buraimi.

Quick answer

  • Today's gold rate in Al Ain is the UAE-wide per-gram rate: identical to Abu Dhabi city and Dubai.
  • The Al Ain gold souk is in the old town centre near the main souk and bus station.
  • Al Ain shops stock mostly 21K Gulf designs, with 22K and 18K alongside.
  • The rate updates through the day; your bill adds making charges and 5% VAT on top.
  • Buyers from Buraimi across the Omani border also shop here, so weekends are busy.

Shops in Al Ain display this rate on a board, usually by the entrance, and most update it a few times during trading hours. If the market jumps mid-afternoon, a board last changed at noon will be a little off, so treat the live table above as the reference and the shop board as a check.

Remember that the per-gram rate is for the metal alone. A finished piece adds a making charge, and the whole sum carries 5% VAT. Our gold calculator lets you enter a weight and karat to estimate the metal value before you go shopping.

One UAE rate: why Al Ain follows Abu Dhabi and Dubai

Al Ain belongs to the Emirate of Abu Dhabi, and residents sometimes ask whether the Abu Dhabi gold rate and the Dubai gold rate differ. They do not. The Dubai Gold & Jewellery Group publishes a daily rate that functions as the national benchmark, and wholesalers who supply Al Ain's shops from Dubai and Abu Dhabi city use it for every transaction. A jeweller on the Al Ain souk cannot sell below it without losing money and cannot sell above it for long without losing customers.

Underneath that benchmark is the global market. Gold trades around the clock in London, New York, Zurich and Shanghai, and the dollar price feeds straight into the dirham price because the two currencies are pegged. A full account is on the who sets the Dubai gold rate page, and the spot price in USD page shows the ounce figure that drives everything.

For Al Ain shoppers the takeaway is that there is nothing to gain by checking the rate in a different city. The useful comparison is between shops, on making charge and design, not between emirates.

Al Ain gold souk: layout and character

The Al Ain gold souk occupies a stretch of the old town centre, within walking distance of the main Al Ain Souk, the central bus station and the Al Ain Oasis. The shops line a few streets and a covered arcade, and most are small, family-run counters that have served the same Emirati families for a generation. It is quieter than any Dubai gold area and the browsing culture is relaxed: you can look for ten minutes without pressure.

What is on the shelves

The stock reflects demand. Heavy 21K sets for weddings, bangles sold in matching pairs, Emirati-style head pieces and coin necklaces, and a good range of 21K and 22K children's gifts for births and Eid. 18K is present for lighter daily wear and for rings with stones. Pure 24K is mostly small bars and coins kept in a drawer rather than on display.

Beyond the souk

Gold shops in Al Ain are not limited to the souk. The larger national chains have counters inside the city's malls, with fixed prices, air conditioning and a warranty card, and a few independent jewellers trade from the Sanaiya and Town Centre districts. For how the souk compares with mall counters, see the gold souks hub.

Souk shop or mall chain: which suits you?

The gold rate is identical in both, so the decision rests on four things: making charge, design range, after-sales service and how much you enjoy negotiating.

Working out a price before you walk in

Suppose you want a pair of 21K bangles weighing 28 grams together. Multiply 28 by the live 21K rate on the board above for the metal value. Add the making charge, which for plain bangles is quoted per gram and for engraved or filigree work is higher. Add 5% VAT on that subtotal. That total is what a fair Al Ain shop should quote, give or take the making charge.

Where people get confused is when a shop quotes a single all-in per-gram figure. That figure is the metal rate plus the making charge blended together, and it makes comparison between shops harder. Politely ask for the two to be separated on the receipt. Any reputable shop will do it, and the making charges page explains what a reasonable split looks like.

Al Ain shops are at their quietest on weekday mornings. That is when a shop owner has time to open the back drawer of unmounted pieces and to discuss a custom order at a fair making charge.

Buraimi, Oman and the border effect

Al Ain and the Omani town of Buraimi were once a single oasis settlement, and the two still share a daily flow of people. Shoppers from Buraimi and from further inside Oman come to the Al Ain gold souk on weekends because the UAE has no import duty on gold and because the selection is wider. That cross-border demand keeps the souk busier than its size would suggest and keeps shop owners competitive on making charges.

If you are travelling onward with gold, keep the itemised receipt. Customs officers on either side may ask for proof of purchase and karat, and an invoice showing weight, purity and the price paid answers the question in seconds. Travellers heading further afield, particularly to India, should read the customs allowance guide before they buy more than a modest quantity.

Tourists staying in Al Ain hotels can also use the UAE's VAT refund scheme for jewellery on departure, provided the shop issues a tax-free form and the purchase meets the minimum. Details are on the VAT and refund page.

Seasons and timing in Al Ain

Al Ain's gold calendar is shaped by Emirati family life more than by Diwali or the Dubai Shopping Festival. The busiest weeks are the run-up to both Eids, the national wedding season from October through March when the weather cools, and the days after salaries land at month end. Shop owners know this and have the least room on making charges when their counters are crowded.

The gold rate itself does not care about the calendar; it moves with the world market. Many Al Ain families handle that uncertainty by buying a little at a time, treating each purchase as savings in a form that can be worn. Our price history page shows how the dirham rate has behaved over months and years so you can see the pattern for yourself, and the investing hub covers bars and coins for those who want gold without the making charge at all.

Selling or pledging gold in Al Ain

Most souk jewellers buy gold as well as sell it. The offer is based on pure gold content at a rate just below the live buying figure, after a quick test and weighing. Expect the shop to deduct stone weight and to ignore the making charge you originally paid. Having the original receipt from an Al Ain shop makes the process smoother, and shops often give a slightly better price on pieces they sold themselves.

If you would rather keep the jewellery, several banks and finance companies in the UAE offer loans secured against gold, and a few Al Ain branches handle them. The gold loan page explains how the valuation and the loan-to-value work. For the straightforward sale route, start with the selling guide, which applies equally in Al Ain.

Questions people ask

Is the gold rate in Al Ain different from Abu Dhabi city?

No. Al Ain is part of the Emirate of Abu Dhabi and both cities use the same national per-gram rate, which follows the international spot price converted into dirhams. Making charges can differ between individual shops, but the metal price is the same whether you buy in Al Ain, Abu Dhabi city, Dubai or Sharjah.

Where is the Al Ain gold souk?

It is in the old town centre, close to the main Al Ain Souk, the central bus station and the Al Ain Oasis. The gold shops occupy a few streets and a covered arcade within easy walking distance of each other. Parking is available nearby and the area is simple to reach from any part of the city.

Which karat do Al Ain shops mainly sell?

21K dominates because the city's Emirati and wider Gulf clientele prefer it for sets, bangles and gifts. 22K is stocked for South Asian customers and 18K for lighter pieces and stone-set rings. Pure 24K is available as small bars and coins. Ask for the karat to be written on the receipt whatever you buy.

Can I pay less in Al Ain than in Dubai?

Not on the gold itself, which costs the same per gram everywhere in the UAE. You may pay a lower making charge in Al Ain on plain Gulf-style pieces because competition for the Omani cross-border trade is keen and overheads are modest. For branded or intricate designs the difference is small.

Do gold shops in Al Ain give tourist VAT refunds?

Shops registered with the refund scheme can issue a tax-free form at purchase, which tourists validate at the airport when leaving the UAE within ninety days. Ask before paying whether the shop is registered, since not every small souk counter is. The refund applies to jewellery; investment bars are already zero-rated.
