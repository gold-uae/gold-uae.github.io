Gold Rate Today in Fujairah: Live Per-Gram Price and the Fujairah Gold Souk

Fujairah is the only emirate entirely on the Gulf of Oman coast, separated from Dubai by the Hajar mountains and a ninety-minute drive. Its gold trade is small, but it serves a loyal local market of Emirati families, port and shipping workers and the South Asian community, plus weekend visitors heading to the beaches. The gold rate today in Fujairah is not a local figure: shops price from the same UAE-wide per-gram rate that Deira uses, derived from the global spot price in dollars and converted at the pegged dirham. That live rate is on the board below.

What this page adds is the local picture: where the Fujairah gold souk actually is, what the shops stock, how to read their bills and when a trip over the mountains is worth it.

Quick answer

  • The gold rate in Fujairah today is the same per-gram AED rate used across the UAE.
  • Fujairah's gold shops are a small cluster in the city's old souk area and a few malls.
  • The rate follows the world spot price minute by minute; shop boards update during the day.
  • Bills add a making charge and 5% VAT; that is where shops differ, not on the metal.
  • East-coast residents often compare Fujairah shops with Sharjah and Dubai for range.

A Fujairah jeweller writes a version of this on the board at the counter and adjusts it when the market moves enough to matter. Because the east coast is a quieter market, some smaller shops update less often than Deira does, which is why the board inside a shop can sit a little above or below the live number for part of the day. Use the live rate as your anchor and expect the shop to bill at the figure current at the time of sale.

Why there is no separate Fujairah gold price

Fujairah's jewellers do not buy gold locally. Their stock arrives from wholesalers and manufacturers in Dubai and Sharjah, who in turn work off the daily reference rate published by the Dubai Gold & Jewellery Group. A shop on the east coast therefore pays the same for its metal as a shop in Bur Dubai and sells it at the same per-gram rate. The only way a Fujairah shop could undercut the rate would be by selling at a loss, and the only way it could sit above it for long would be if customers never compared.

That reference rate is itself a mirror of the global market: gold trading in London, New York and Asia sets a dollar price for the ounce, and the dirham peg turns it into a dirham price without any currency effect. The full chain from world market to shop board is set out on the who sets the Dubai gold rate page.

For a resident of Fujairah the useful lesson is that searching for a cheaper rate in another emirate is wasted effort. The rates by emirate hub spells out what actually varies: making charges, design range, service and bargaining culture.

Fujairah gold souk and gold shops: where to look

Fujairah does not have a dedicated gold souk on the scale of Deira or even Sharjah. Instead, there is a modest row of gold and jewellery shops in the old market area of Fujairah city, a short distance from the fort and the central roundabouts, together with counters from the national chains inside the city's shopping malls. Together they make up what locals call the Fujairah gold souk.

The old market shops

These are small independent counters, often run by the same family for decades. Stock is weighted towards 21K Gulf sets, 22K South Asian designs and a modest range of 18K. Prices are quoted by weight at the board rate with a negotiable making charge, and you can order a specific design to be brought from Dubai within a week or two.

Mall counters

The chains in the malls carry fixed making charges, catalogue designs and more 18K and diamond work. They suit buyers who want a receipt with a warranty and a straightforward exchange policy.

Nearby alternatives

Khor Fakkan and Kalba, Sharjah enclaves on the same coast, have a few jewellers each, and Dibba at the northern end serves the border with Oman. For the big-city alternative, see the Sharjah gold souk page, which is the nearest large market by road.

Following one purchase through the bill

Take a practical case: a 22K chain weighing 4.5 grams bought for a child's birthday from a shop in the old market.

  1. The chain is weighed in front of you; ask to see the scale read zero before it goes on.
  2. 4.5 g is multiplied by the live 22K rate to give the metal value.
  3. A making charge is added. For a machine-made chain this is usually a modest per-gram sum; for a handmade rope or a designer link it climbs.
  4. VAT at 5% is calculated on metal plus making charge.
  5. The receipt should show weight, karat, rate, making charge and VAT as separate lines.

Two shops in Fujairah will quote the same metal line for that chain. If their totals differ, the gap is making charge, and that is the number to negotiate. For a sense of what is reasonable for chains, bangles and sets, read the making charges guide; for chain styles and weights specifically, the gold chains page is a good companion.

In a small market like Fujairah the shop owner is usually at the counter. Asking directly for the making charge per gram, before you look at any design, sets the tone for an honest conversation.

Who buys gold in Fujairah and when

The emirate's demand has a rhythm of its own. Port and shipping workers paid at month end, Emirati families in the wedding season, and visitors from Dubai who come for the coast and stop at a jeweller on the way home. Eid brings the biggest surge, with Diwali a clear second among the South Asian community. During those weeks the small number of shops means counters get crowded and making charges hold firm.

The quieter months of high summer are when a local shop is most willing to talk about the labour component, and when a special order from Dubai gets made faster. Keep an eye on the live gold chart if you are trying to buy during a dip, but remember that the rate is the same whether you buy in July or December; the calendar only changes the making charge and the crowd.

Tourists, VAT and leaving the country

Fujairah hosts many visitors who fly out of Dubai or Sharjah. Jewellery bought anywhere in the UAE carries 5% VAT, and tourists can reclaim it through the refund scheme at the departure airport if the shop issued a tax-free form at the time of purchase, the goods leave within ninety days and the purchase met the minimum. Not every small Fujairah counter is registered, so ask before you pay. The VAT and tax refund page walks through the process.

Investment-grade bars and coins of 99% purity or higher are zero-rated, which is one reason some residents who want gold purely as savings choose a small bar over a bangle. Whatever you buy, keep the itemised receipt: customs at your destination may ask for proof of karat and value, and residents returning to India face a specific duty-free allowance that is explained on the Dubai to India customs page.

Selling gold on the east coast

Fujairah jewellers buy as well as sell, pricing on the pure gold content after a test and a weigh-in, at a figure a little under the live buying rate. The small size of the market means you should always get more than one quote, and for a large quantity it is sensible to add a quote from a dedicated buyer in Dubai before accepting. Stones, enamel and non-gold clasps are deducted from the weight, and the making charge you paid at purchase is not returned.

Bring the original receipt if you have it, particularly if the piece was bought locally; shops tend to offer a better price on their own work. The selling gold guide describes the process and the questions to ask, and the selling and loans hub explains gold-backed loans for those who need cash but want to keep their jewellery.

Questions people ask

Does Fujairah have its own gold rate?

No. Jewellers in Fujairah use the UAE-wide per-gram rate that follows the international spot price converted into dirhams at the fixed peg. The metal costs the same per gram as in Dubai, Sharjah or Abu Dhabi. Only the making charge and the design range vary from one shop to another.

Where is the Fujairah gold souk?

The main group of gold shops is in the old market area of Fujairah city, not far from the fort and the central roundabouts, with further counters from the national chains inside the city's malls. It is a small cluster rather than a dedicated souk, so allow for a short walk between shops and a mall visit if you want to compare.

Which karat is easiest to find in Fujairah?

21K is the most widely stocked because Emirati and Gulf customers prefer it, followed by 22K for the South Asian community. 18K is available in the malls and in a few souk shops, mostly as lighter chains and stone-set rings. Investment-grade 24K is limited to small bars and coins, so serious bar buyers usually go to Dubai.

Are making charges lower in Fujairah than in Dubai?

On plain, common designs they are often similar or slightly lower because rents are modest, but with only a handful of shops competing there is less downward pressure than in Deira. Negotiate on the making charge and compare it with what a Dubai shop would ask for the same weight before deciding whether a trip is worth it.

Can tourists reclaim VAT on gold bought in Fujairah?

Yes, if the shop is registered with the tourist refund scheme and issues a tax-free form at purchase. The refund is claimed at the departure airport, and the jewellery must leave the country within ninety days. Ask about registration before paying, because some small independent counters are not part of the scheme.
