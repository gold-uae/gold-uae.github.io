Gold Spot Price in USD: the Live Ounce Price and What It Means in Dubai

Every gold board in the UAE, from the Deira souk to a Madinat Zayed showroom in Abu Dhabi, starts from one number: the international spot price of gold in US dollars per troy ounce. That figure is set by continuous trading between banks, refiners, funds and futures exchanges, and it never sleeps for long. This page shows the live ounce price, walks through the arithmetic that turns it into a per-gram rate in dirhams, and explains what actually pushes it up or down so you can read the board like a trader rather than a tourist.

Quick answer

  • The gold spot price is the world benchmark, quoted in US dollars per troy ounce (31.1035 g).
  • Dubai's per-gram rate is that ounce price divided by 31.1035 and multiplied by 3.6725 dirhams.
  • Because the dirham is pegged to the dollar, the UAE rate moves exactly as the spot price moves.
  • Spot trades almost 24 hours a day on weekdays and pauses from Friday evening to Sunday evening New York time.
  • What you pay in a Deira shop is spot plus a small premium, making charges and 5% VAT on jewellery.

What the gold spot price actually is

"Spot" means the price for gold delivered now, as opposed to a futures contract for delivery in a later month. The benchmark is quoted for one troy ounce of 999 fine gold in US dollars, and it is the number you see labelled XAU/USD on trading screens.

There is no single building where the spot price is decided. It emerges from over-the-counter dealing centred on London, the COMEX futures market in New York, and electronic platforms that run through Asia. A reference fix is also published twice on London business days, but shops in the Emirates follow the live quote, not the fix.

Two details matter when you compare quotes. First, every quote has a bid and an ask; the published figure is usually the mid or the last trade. Second, a troy ounce is heavier than the kitchen ounce: 31.1035 grams, not 28.35. Mixing the two is the most common mistake people make when they check the gold calculator against a souk receipt.

From dollars per ounce to dirhams per gram

The conversion is pure arithmetic, and it is worth doing once by hand so the shop board stops looking mysterious. Take the spot price per ounce, divide by 31.1035 to get the price of one gram of pure gold in dollars, then multiply by 3.6725 to express it in dirhams. That gives the 24K per-gram rate.

Lower karats are a slice of that figure according to their fineness:

24K999100%
22K91691.6%
21K87587.5%

14K58558.5%

So if spot is S dollars, a gram of 22K metal in Dubai is worth roughly S × 3.6725 × 0.916 ÷ 31.1035 dirhams before any charges. The daily board published for the trade rounds this and adds a thin margin, which is why the posted rate sits a touch above the raw calculation. The karat guide goes deeper into what those fineness numbers mean on a hallmark.

COMEX, London and the 24-hour gold clock

Gold trading follows the sun. The week opens in Asia on Sunday evening New York time, which is early Monday morning in the UAE. Tokyo, Shanghai and Singapore set the tone, then London takes over from about mid-morning Gulf time, and New York joins for the busiest overlap in the UAE afternoon and evening.

The COMEX gold price you see quoted in US headlines is a futures price for the most active contract month. It normally sits a few dollars above spot because it includes financing and storage until delivery; the gap is small and moves with interest rates. Traders in Dubai also watch DGCX, the local exchange, which lists gold futures priced to track the same international market.

The quiet window runs from the New York close on Friday afternoon until Sunday evening, which is Saturday morning to Monday morning in Dubai. During those hours the shop boards in Deira stay fixed at the last published rate, which is why Saturday shoppers at the Gold Souk see no movement until Monday.

What moves the world gold price

Gold has no earnings and pays no coupon, so its price is driven by what investors expect from the alternatives. The clearest relationships are these:

  • The US dollar. Gold is priced in dollars, so a weaker dollar usually lifts the ounce price and a stronger dollar weighs on it.
  • Real interest rates. When inflation-adjusted bond yields rise, holding gold costs more in missed income and the price tends to soften; falling real yields do the opposite.
  • Central banks. Reserve managers have been large net buyers in recent years, and their purchases provide steady underlying demand.
  • Risk events. Wars, banking scares and elections send money into gold as a hedge, often quickly and sometimes briefly.
  • Physical demand seasons. Indian wedding months, Diwali, Chinese New Year and Eid add jewellery demand that supports prices at the margin.

None of these works in isolation, and the mix changes. For a reader of the price analysis page, the practical lesson is that a headline about US inflation data can move your 22K rate in Sharjah within seconds.

Why the UAE rate copies the dollar price exactly

The dirham has been fixed at 3.6725 to the US dollar since 1997. That peg removes an entire layer of uncertainty that buyers in India, Pakistan or Egypt live with: in those countries the local gold rate is the dollar spot price multiplied by an exchange rate that itself changes every day. In the Emirates only one variable moves.

For expatriates this has a useful consequence. If you earn in dirhams and send money home, the gold rate in your home currency can rise even on a day when the dollar price of gold is flat, simply because the rupee or the Pakistani rupee weakened. Our pages on the Dubai rate in Indian rupees and the rate in PKR, NPR and LKR show both pieces side by side.

The peg also explains why the same per-gram figure applies from Dubai to Fujairah. There is no regional spot price for Abu Dhabi or Ajman; the rates by emirate differ only in local making charges and the mix of shops.

Spot versus the price you actually pay in Deira

Spot is a wholesale benchmark for large bars between professionals. A retail customer in Bur Dubai or Karama pays a chain of small additions on top of it.

  1. The published retail rate. The trade body's daily board rounds the raw conversion and includes a modest margin for the metal itself.
  2. Making charges. A per-gram fee or a percentage for the craftsmanship, highest on intricate bridal sets and lowest on plain bangles and coins. See the making charges guide.
  3. VAT. Jewellery carries 5% VAT on the full invoice; investment bars of 99%+ purity are zero-rated. Tourists can reclaim the VAT on departure.
  4. The buy-back spread. When you sell, shops pay slightly below the board for the metal and nothing for the making charge, which is the real cost of owning jewellery rather than bars.

Before you negotiate, convert the live ounce price to a 22K gram figure yourself and write it down. A seller who quotes a metal rate noticeably above that number is padding the price, whatever the story about design or brand.

Reading quotes like a trader: units, markets and common confusions

Search for the gold price in dollars and you will meet several different numbers that are all technically correct. Keep these distinctions in mind:

Ounce, gram, tola, kilo

International quotes use the troy ounce; Gulf shops use the gram; South Asian families still think in tolas (11.6638 g); refiners and vaults talk in kilos. One ounce is about 2.67 tolas, so a tola rate in Dubai is simply the gram rate times 11.6638.

Spot, futures and the US retail price

"Gold price in USA" usually refers to the same spot benchmark, but American retail coins and bars carry dealer premiums that are typically larger than the premiums in Dubai. If you are comparing a US dealer's one-ounce coin with a kilo bar in Dubai, compare the premium over spot, not the sticker.

Live versus delayed

Some free quotes update every few minutes and some lag by a quarter of an hour. On a volatile day the difference can be meaningful for a large purchase, so for anything above a few hundred grams confirm the rate at the counter at the moment of payment. Questions people ask

What is the gold spot price right now in US dollars?

The live figure is shown in the widget near the top of this page and refreshes automatically in your browser. It is quoted per troy ounce of pure gold. To see it as a Dubai per-gram rate, divide by 31.1035 and multiply by 3.6725, then apply the karat share you are buying, for example 0.916 for 22K.

Is the Dubai gold rate the same as the international spot price?

It is derived from it directly, but it is not identical. The retail board published daily in Dubai converts the spot price to dirhams per gram and adds a small margin; the shop then adds making charges and VAT on jewellery. The underlying metal value, however, tracks spot tick for tick because the dirham is pegged to the dollar.

Why does the gold price in dollars change when the Dubai shops are closed?

Gold trades around the clock across Asian, European and American sessions, while shop boards only update during business hours. Overnight moves show up as a jump when the board is refreshed in the morning. The only period when the spot market itself is quiet is from the Friday close in New York to the Sunday evening reopening.

What is the difference between the COMEX gold price and spot gold?

COMEX quotes are futures contracts for delivery in a set month, traded in New York. They usually sit slightly above spot because they include the cost of carrying gold until delivery. Spot is the price for immediate settlement. For a shopper in the UAE the two move together so closely that the distinction rarely matters at the counter.

Does a stronger US dollar make gold cheaper in the UAE?

Usually yes, indirectly. A stronger dollar tends to push the ounce price down, and since the dirham is fixed to the dollar, the per-gram rate in AED falls with it. For an Indian or Pakistani expat the picture is mixed, because the same dollar strength often weakens the home currency and lifts the rate back home.
