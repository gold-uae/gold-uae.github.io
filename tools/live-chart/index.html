Live Gold Chart: Gold Price Chart in USD and AED, 24 Hours to 3 Days and Beyond

A gold chart answers a question the rate board cannot: not just what the price is, but what it has been doing. The live gold chart on this page shows the spot price in dollars per troy ounce, updated while the market is open, with the dirham-per-gram equivalent alongside so you can relate it to the Dubai board. Zoom to 24 hours for today's trend, three days for the week's shape, or further for context.

Below the chart you will find how to read it, when during the UAE day the price actually moves, how the Dubai board follows the chart, and what the chart terms used in trading forums mean.

How to read the gold price graph: lines, candles and timeframes

Most people only need the line view, but it helps to know what the other views mean when you see them elsewhere:

  • Line chart. One point per interval, usually the closing price. Clean and easy to read; the best choice for deciding whether to visit the souk this afternoon or tomorrow.
  • Candlestick chart. Each candle shows the open, high, low and close for its interval. A long candle means a volatile interval; a long wick means the price tried to move and was pushed back. Traders like candles; shoppers rarely need them.
  • Timeframe. The width of each interval: one minute, one hour, one day. A 24-hour chart with one-minute candles is noisy; the same day on hourly candles shows the real shape.

When the gold price moves during a UAE day

Gold is traded somewhere in the world almost continuously from Sunday evening to Friday evening, New York time. From the point of view of someone in Dubai or Abu Dhabi, the day unfolds like this:

  1. Early morning (UAE). Asian centres are active: Tokyo, Shanghai, Singapore, Hong Kong. Moves are usually modest unless there is Chinese demand news.
  2. Late morning to early afternoon. London opens. Liquidity rises and the day's direction often starts to form. The London morning and afternoon reference prices are set during this window.
  3. Mid-afternoon to evening. New York opens while London is still trading. This overlap is the busiest part of the day, and US economic data releases in this window cause the sharpest intraday swings.
  4. Late evening to night. London closes, New York winds down; the price drifts until Asia reopens.

Practically, this means a shop board updated at noon can be stale by six in the evening after a US data release. If you are buying a large weight, checking the chart on the way to Deira or Madinat Zayed is worthwhile. The page on who sets the Dubai rate explains how and how often the board is refreshed.

The weekend gap: why Saturday shoppers see Friday's price

The international market closes on Friday evening New York time and does not reopen until Sunday evening, which is early Monday morning in the UAE. During that gap the chart is flat and the Dubai board stays at Friday's close. UAE souks, however, are at their busiest on Saturday and Sunday, so a large share of retail gold in the country is bought at a price fixed on Friday.

This matters in two ways. If big news breaks over the weekend, Monday's open can jump or drop sharply, and anyone who bought on Saturday either got a bargain or paid over the odds in hindsight. And if you are selling, a Friday-evening check of the chart tells you exactly the rate you will be quoted against all weekend, with no further movement to wait for.

Some chart providers show a short gap at the weekend; others draw a flat line across it. Either way, the first candles on Monday morning UAE time are the ones to watch for the reaction to weekend events.

Gold market trend today: reading the short-term direction honestly

People search for the gold trend today hoping for a verdict, and the honest answer from a 24-hour chart is usually "slightly up" or "slightly down" with no information about tomorrow. A few habits keep the short view useful without over-interpreting it:

  • Compare with the previous close, not with the day's low. "Up since 9am" can simply be a recovery from an early dip; the change on the day is the fairer measure.
  • Notice the size of the move in dirhams per gram. A dollar move that looks dramatic on a zoomed-in chart often translates to a tiny change per gram. Run it through the calculator for the weight you plan to buy and see if it even matters.
  • Expect scheduled events. US inflation and employment data, central-bank rate decisions and the monthly jobs report are known in advance and routinely move the chart. The price analysis page lists what historically drives the price.
  • Ignore anyone with a firm forecast. Trend-following works until it stops, and the stop is not visible on the chart in advance.
If the chart shows a sharp spike in the last hour, wait before entering a shop: boards tend to be updated upward quickly and downward slowly, so buying right after a jump often means paying the new high while sellers are still quoting the old one.

Gold trading graph versus the shop board: why they differ

The chart shows a wholesale price for large bars delivered in London or traded as futures in New York. The Dubai shop board shows a retail per-gram rate for the karats sold locally. They are tightly linked but not identical:

  • The board is updated a few times a day, not every second, so it lags the chart.
  • The board is rounded to whole dirhams per gram.
  • The board's lower-karat figures apply the purity fractions, which is simple arithmetic, but some boards round each karat separately.
  • Neither the board nor the chart includes making charges, VAT on jewellery or the buy-back deduction when selling.

For most buyers, a gap of about a dirham per gram between chart and board is normal. A much larger gap, especially in the shop's favour, is worth a question. Questions people ask

Is the gold chart live, and how often does it update?

Yes. The chart draws from a live spot price feed and refreshes while the page is open, so during trading hours you see the current price and the recent path. The market itself pauses from Friday evening to Sunday evening New York time, so over the UAE weekend the line is flat at Friday's close and resumes early Monday morning UAE time.

Why is the gold chart in dollars when I buy in dirhams?

Gold is priced globally in US dollars per troy ounce, and every local rate is derived from that number. Because the dirham is fixed at 3.6725 to the dollar, the AED chart is the dollar chart scaled by a constant, with no extra currency movement. The page shows both so you can read the global reference and relate it instantly to the per-gram board in Dubai.

What does a 24-hour gold chart tell me about buying today?

It tells you whether the price has risen, fallen or held since the previous close and how volatile the day has been, which helps decide whether to go to the souk now or wait until evening. It does not predict tomorrow. For a big purchase, run the day's move through the calculator for your weight; often the change per gram is smaller than the making charge difference between two shops.

Can I use this chart for gold trading?

You can read it, but it is designed for buyers and sellers of physical gold, not for leveraged trading. Trading platforms provide tick-by-tick data, order books and indicators that a simple reference chart does not. If you are considering trading rather than buying metal, read the online trading page first; leverage magnifies losses as much as gains, and this site does not give trading advice.

What is the difference between the gold spot price chart and the Dubai gold rate?

The spot chart is the wholesale international price per ounce in dollars, moving continuously. The Dubai gold rate is that price converted to dirhams per gram, split by karat, rounded and updated a few times a day for retail boards. They move together, with the board lagging by minutes to hours. Making charges and VAT are added at the shop on top of the board rate.
