Gold Calculator UAE: 1 Gram, 5 Gram, 10 Gram, 1 Tola and 100 Gram Gold Price in AED

Whether you are pricing a 1 gram gold ring for a newborn, a 10 gram chain for a wedding, a 1 tola coin to take back to Lahore or a 100 gram bar as savings, the question is the same: weight multiplied by the per-gram rate for that karat. The calculator below does that with the live rate in dirhams, so you never have to multiply by hand or trust a stale number from a screenshot.

The rest of this page explains how to read the result, how weight units differ, why your price in a shop will not match the calculator exactly, and the handful of situations where people get the maths wrong.

  • Type a weight, pick a karat and the calculator shows the live metal value in AED instantly.
  • It handles grams, tolas (11.6638 g) and troy ounces (31.1035 g), and converts between them.

How the calculation works, step by step

There is no magic in the calculator; it follows the arithmetic a jeweller does in their head:

  1. Convert the spot price to a gram rate. The market quotes gold in US dollars per troy ounce. Divide by 31.1035 to get dollars per gram, then multiply by 3.6725 to get dirhams per gram of pure (24K) gold.
  2. Apply the karat. 22K is 91.6% gold, so its rate is 0.916 times the 24K rate. 21K uses 0.875, 18K uses 0.75, 14K uses 0.585.
  3. Multiply by the weight. A 10 gram piece at 22K is 10 times the 22K gram rate. A 1 tola piece is 11.6638 grams, so multiply by that.

This gives you the metal value: what the gold itself is worth today. Everything a shop charges above it is making charge, VAT on that charge, and the retailer's margin. Everything a buyer offers below it is their refining and resale margin. The karat explainer goes deeper into why 22K and 21K have different rates for the same weight.

Grams, tolas and ounces: getting the unit right

Three weight systems meet in the UAE gold market, and mixing them up is the most common calculator mistake.

UnitGramsWho uses it
Gram1UAE shop boards, invoices, hallmarked jewellery everywhere
Tola11.6638Pakistani, Indian and Nepali buyers; some coins and old bars
Troy ounce31.1035International spot price, large bars, Western coins
Kilogram1,000Wholesale and investment bars

Two traps to watch. First, the tola used in South Asian trade is the 11.6638 g version, but some old family pieces were weighed on a slightly different traditional tola; if you are reselling an heirloom, trust the shop scale, not the family story. Second, the troy ounce is heavier than the ordinary (avoirdupois) ounce of about 28.35 g that kitchen scales use, so never weigh gold on a kitchen scale set to ounces.

Readers comparing Dubai with Karachi or Kathmandu will find the tola rates in PKR, NPR and LKR page useful; it does the currency conversion the calculator does not.

Common weights people search for, and what they usually are

The searches that bring people here cluster around a few weights, each tied to a real purchase in the UAE:

  • 1 gram. The smallest coin or bar, or a tiny baby ring or nose pin. At this size the making charge and packaging can equal the metal value, so a 1 gram bar from a bank or bullion shop is almost always better value than 1 gram of jewellery.
  • 5 gram. A light chain, a pair of studs, or the most popular small bar for gifting at Eid or Diwali.
  • 10 gram. A substantial ring or a slim bangle, and the standard "biscuit" bar size. Many 22K rate searches are really 10 gram questions because Indian shops quote per 10 g.
  • 20 gram. A men's chain or a single heavy bangle; also the duty-free allowance for men returning to India after a year abroad.
  • 50 gram. A bridal set in 21K or 22K, or a mid-size bar.
  • 100 gram. The serious savings bar, and the size at which bar premiums over spot become small.
  • 1 kg. Wholesale territory, bought through bullion dealers rather than souk shops; see the bar guide for how that market works.

Enter any of these into the calculator and switch between karats to see the trade-off between purity and weight for a fixed budget.

Why the shop price will not match the calculator exactly

A price tag in Meena Bazaar or at a mall jeweller is built from four parts, and the calculator only produces the first:

  • Metal value at the board rate for the karat, which should be within a dirham or two per gram of the calculator.
  • Making charge, quoted either per gram or as a percentage of the metal value. Plain bangles and chains carry low charges; intricate necklaces and branded designs carry high ones. The making charges page gives the typical ranges by style.
  • VAT at 5% on jewellery. Investment-grade bars and coins of 99% purity or more are zero-rated, which is one reason bars are cheaper per gram than ornaments. Tourists can reclaim VAT on departure; details on the VAT and refund page.
  • Stones and extras, priced separately from the gold weight.

When selling, the arithmetic runs backwards: metal value minus a deduction for refining, minus any stone weight. The calculator still gives you the anchor; the selling guide explains what deduction is normal.

Ask the shop to weigh the piece in front of you and read the grams aloud, then type that figure into the calculator before you discuss the making charge. Negotiating from the metal value upwards is far easier than negotiating from the tag downwards.

Stone-set pieces, hollow pieces and other calculation traps

A few situations trip up even careful buyers:

Gross versus net weight

A ring with a zircon or a necklace with pearls weighs more on the scale than the gold it contains. Reputable shops print the net gold weight on the invoice and price the stones separately. If a shop quotes the gold rate on gross weight, you are paying gold price for glass. Always ask which figure is on the tag.

Hollow and lightweight jewellery

Modern machine-made bangles and chains can be hollow, giving a large look for a low weight. That is not a trick in itself, but the making charge per gram on hollow work is often higher, so the calculator result will look small next to the tag. Judge such pieces on the total price for the look you want, not on gram value alone.

Mixed karats in one set

Some sets combine a 22K chain with an 18K pendant that holds stones. Each part must be calculated at its own karat rate; a single blended figure hides which part is expensive.

Old gold exchange

When trading in old jewellery against a new purchase, the shop values your old gold at its tested karat, not the karat on a worn stamp. The hallmark and testing page explains how that test is done and why you should watch it.

Using the calculator to plan a budget

Running the arithmetic in reverse is just as useful. If you have a fixed sum from a salary or a bonus, divide it by the per-gram rate for the karat you want and you get the maximum weight before making charges; knock off roughly the typical making charge percentage and you have a realistic gram target to take into the souk. Doing this at home keeps the decision calm; doing it at the counter with a salesperson waiting does not.

For regular savers, the same approach shows how many grams a monthly amount buys at today's rate. Many UAE residents buy small bars or coins each month in this way rather than trying to time the market. The investing hub covers that habit, the choice between bars and coins, and the storage question.

Finally, remember the calculator is a tool, not a valuation. It cannot see craftsmanship, brand or condition, all of which matter on the second-hand market. Use it to know what the gold is worth, and let the rest of this site help you judge the rest.

Questions people ask

How do I find the 10 gram gold price in Dubai today?

Enter 10 in the calculator, select grams and the karat you want (22K for Indian-style jewellery, 21K for Gulf designs, 24K for bars) and the value appears in AED at the live rate. Ten grams is simply ten times the per-gram board rate for that karat, which is why a quick multiplication at the shop counter should give the same figure.

What is 1 tola in grams and how do I price it?

One tola is 11.6638 grams. Select tola as the unit in the calculator, enter 1, and the tool multiplies the per-gram rate by 11.6638 for the karat chosen. Pakistani and Indian coins and some older bars are sold by the tola, while UAE shop boards quote per gram, so the conversion is the step most people need.

Does the calculator include making charges and VAT?

No. It shows the metal value only, which is the part of the price that is the same in every shop. Making charges vary by design and retailer, and 5% VAT applies to jewellery but not to investment-grade bars and coins, so adding them would make the figure wrong for most readers. Treat the result as the base and add the shop's quoted charge on top.

Why is the 1 gram gold price in the UAE different for 22K and 24K?

Because 22K contains 91.6% gold and 24K contains 99.9%. The gram of 22K has less gold in it, so its rate is about 91.6% of the 24K rate; 21K is 87.5% and 18K is 75%. The alloy metals that make up the difference are worth almost nothing by comparison, so the per-gram price tracks the gold content almost exactly.

Can I use the calculator to check the 100 gram or 1 kg gold price?

Yes, enter 100 or 1000 grams at 24K. Note that bars of this size are sold by bullion dealers at a small premium over the calculated spot value, which covers refining and packaging, and that the premium per gram shrinks as the bar gets larger. The calculator gives you the spot value to compare that premium against.
