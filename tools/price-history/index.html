Gold Price History UAE: Gold Rate Chart for the Last Month, 6 Months and 10 Years

Before a big purchase, almost everyone in the UAE asks the same thing: is the gold rate high right now, or is this normal? The honest answer needs a chart with history behind it, not a single day's figure. The interactive graph on this page shows the gold rate in Dubai over the last month, six months, year and ten years, in dirhams per gram and dollars per ounce.

Below the chart we explain how to read it, why the UAE line is the global line in different units, what drove the big moves of the past, and how buyers and sellers in Deira and Sharjah actually use this information.

How to read a gold rate history without fooling yourself

Price charts invite over-confidence, so a few reading habits help:

  • Check the range before judging the slope. A one-month view can look like a crash or a boom that is barely visible on the ten-year view. Start wide, then narrow.
  • Look at where today sits in the band. Is the current rate near the top of the last six months, the middle or the bottom? That is the honest measure of "expensive".
  • Ignore the exact number on old days. What matters is the shape and the level relative to now; the figure for a random Tuesday two years ago is trivia.
  • Do not draw lines into the future. A rising trend contains no promise. Gold has had multi-year climbs followed by multi-year slides, and the turn is never visible on the day it happens.

Used this way, the history is a calming tool: it turns "the rate is high" from a feeling into a measurable statement, which is usually the first step to deciding whether to buy a smaller piece now, wait, or buy in instalments.

Why the UAE gold history is just the world history in dirhams

In many countries a gold chart in local currency tells two stories at once: the metal and the currency. In India the rupee has weakened against the dollar for decades, so gold in rupees has risen even in years when gold in dollars fell. The same is true for the Pakistani rupee, the Egyptian pound and the Turkish lira.

The UAE is different. The dirham has been fixed at 3.6725 to the dollar since 1997, so the AED gold chart is the USD chart multiplied by a constant. There is no currency effect to disentangle. This has two practical consequences for a resident:

  1. Gold held in the UAE does not protect you against dirham weakness, because there is none to protect against; it behaves exactly like dollar gold.
  2. If your savings goal is in rupees or another home currency, the UAE chart understates your return in home-currency terms whenever that currency weakens. The rupee page and the PKR, NPR and LKR page show the converted figures.

The spot price in USD page goes deeper into how the international price is formed and where the dollar figure comes from.

What has moved the gold price over the last ten years

Without quoting figures that will date, the broad forces behind the last decade of the chart are well understood:

  • Real interest rates. Gold pays no interest, so when inflation-adjusted yields on dollar bonds are low or negative, holding gold costs little and the price tends to rise; when real yields climb, gold usually struggles.
  • The US dollar. A weaker dollar makes gold cheaper for buyers in other currencies and typically lifts the dollar price; a stronger dollar does the reverse.
  • Central-bank buying. Several large central banks, notably in Asia and the Middle East, have added gold to their reserves over the period, providing steady demand under the market.
  • Crisis demand. Pandemics, wars, banking scares and trade disputes have each produced sharp upward moves as investors sought a store of value, often followed by partial retracements once calm returned.
  • Physical demand seasons. Indian wedding and festival months, Chinese New Year and, locally, Eid and the Dubai Shopping Festival, add demand that is visible in jewellery sales more than in the global price.

None of these is a timing signal on its own. The price analysis page discusses how they interact and why forecasts are so unreliable.

Gold rate in the last six months and last month: how buyers use the short view

The six-month and one-month ranges are the ones residents use most, because they match the horizon of a real decision: a wedding in spring, a Diwali gift, a bonus arriving next quarter. Three practical patterns:

Buying for an occasion

Look at the six-month range and note the band. If today is near the bottom of the band, many buyers simply buy. If it is near the top, a common approach is to buy part of the planned weight now and the rest closer to the date, accepting that the rate might move either way. This is not market timing, it is spreading risk across two dates.

Selling old jewellery

Scrap sellers in Deira and Karama often check the one-month chart and wait for a day when the rate is at the upper end of the recent range before visiting a buyer. The gain from doing so is modest, but the cost of checking is nothing. The selling guide covers the rest of that process.

Monthly saving

Regular buyers of small bars or coins mostly ignore the short view on purpose. Buying the same dirham amount each month averages the price across highs and lows, which the ten-year chart shows has historically smoothed out the entry point.

Set a reminder to glance at the six-month chart a few weeks before any planned purchase. Knowing in advance whether the rate is near a high stops you being surprised at the counter, and gives you time to decide on a smaller weight or a lower karat if the number is uncomfortable.

Gold price over the last ten years: the long view in the UAE

Zoom the chart to its full range and the picture changes. The month-to-month noise disappears and you see a series of long moves: multi-year rises, long sideways stretches and occasional sharp corrections. Measured in dirhams per gram, the rate at the far left of the ten-year view is well below the levels of recent years, which is why older residents remember buying 22K bangles for what now seems like a small sum.

Two lessons from the long chart matter for UAE households. First, the rate has spent long periods going nowhere; anyone who bought at a peak has sometimes waited years to see that level again. Second, over the full span the direction has been up, which is why gold has remained the default form of family savings across the Gulf and South Asia. Both facts are true at once, and the sensible conclusion for most people is to treat gold as a long-term holding rather than a short-term bet.

For the choice between bars, coins and jewellery as a way of holding that long-term position, and the cost of each, start at the investing hub.

What this history page cannot do

It cannot show the price of a specific piece of jewellery over time, because making charges and designs change independently of the metal. It cannot show the buy-back prices that shops actually paid on old days, only the metal rate. And it cannot forecast: a chart that has risen for a year is as likely to pause as to continue, and anyone who tells you otherwise is guessing.

What it can do is replace a vague feeling about whether gold is dear with a clear view of where today's rate sits against its own past. Pair it with the calculator to turn that view into a dirham figure for the weight you have in mind, and with the tools hub if you are not sure which view you need.

Questions people ask

Where can I see the gold rate in the UAE for the last six months?

Use the chart at the top of this page and select the six-month range. It shows the 24K rate in AED per gram, from which the 22K, 21K and 18K rates follow at fixed percentages. The data is the international price converted at the fixed dirham-dollar rate, so it matches what Dubai shop boards displayed on those days to within rounding.

Is the gold price history in Dubai different from the global history?

Only in units. The dirham has been pegged to the dollar at the same rate since 1997, so the Dubai gold chart in AED per gram is the global chart in USD per ounce multiplied by a constant. The shape, the peaks and the troughs are identical. This is unlike India or Pakistan, where currency moves add a second trend to the local gold chart.

Can the chart show the gold rate in Dubai last month day by day?

Yes, choose the one-month range and hover or tap each point to see the daily value. Daily points reflect the closing international price for that day converted into dirhams. The Dubai board is updated several times during trading hours, so an individual day's board may have shown slightly different figures in the morning and afternoon.

Does gold always go up over ten years?

No. Over the full ten-year view the overall direction has been upward, but the chart also contains long flat stretches and sharp falls, and there have been periods in gold's longer history where the price took many years to regain an earlier peak. Many investors treat it as a long-term store of value rather than a guaranteed gainer, and size their holdings accordingly.

Should I wait for the price to fall before buying gold?

Nobody can tell you when it will fall. What the history can tell you is where today sits in the recent range. If that makes you uncomfortable, common approaches are to buy a smaller weight, choose a lower karat, or split the purchase across two or three dates. Consider your own timeline and budget rather than a prediction; this site does not give financial advice.
