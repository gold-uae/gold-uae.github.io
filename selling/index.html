Selling Gold in the UAE and Gold Loans: Sell, Exchange or Borrow Against Your Gold

Gold in a UAE household is savings you can wear, and sooner or later a bangle set, an old chain or a few coins are asked to turn back into dirhams. The good news is that the Emirates has one of the most liquid gold markets anywhere: the same counters that sold you the metal will buy it back, licensed buyers compete openly, and banks and finance companies lend against it. The less good news is that the making charge you paid is gone, and buyers price the metal, not the design.

This section hub explains how old gold is valued, lays out the three routes, and points you to our detailed guides on selling in Dubai and gold loans across the UAE.

Quick answer

  • Old gold in the UAE is bought by weight and purity at a small discount to the day's rate; making charges are never refunded.
  • You have three routes: sell for cash, exchange for new jewellery, or borrow against the gold and keep it.
  • Sell when you no longer want the gold; exchange when you want new pieces; borrow when the need for cash is temporary.
  • The Deira souk, branded chains and licensed buyers all quote from the same rate, so compare three offers.
  • Bring the original invoice and your Emirates ID; honest buyers ask for both.

Before you walk into any shop, decide what problem you are solving, because each route goes to a different counter:

  • You need cash and do not want the gold back. Sell it. Read how to sell gold in Dubai for where to go, how the offer is built and what paperwork to bring.

How buyers value old gold in the UAE

Whatever counter you choose, the valuation follows the same four steps, and knowing them lets you check the arithmetic on the spot.

  1. Purity. The buyer reads the hallmark and usually confirms it with an acid or electronic test. 22K is treated as 916 fineness, 21K as 875, 18K as 750. Pieces without a hallmark are tested and often priced one step lower.
  2. Weight. The item is weighed on a calibrated scale. Stones, enamel and non-gold clasps are deducted, which is why a heavy stone-set necklace sells for less than its gross weight suggests.
  3. Rate. The buyer applies the day's per-gram rate for that karat, then subtracts a buying margin. Souk buyers compete hard on this margin; a branded chain's cash offer is typically less generous than its exchange credit.
  4. Deductions. Melting loss, testing fees or a flat handling charge may appear. Ask for them to be listed.

You can estimate the metal value yourself before you leave home with the gold calculator: enter the weight and karat and you have the ceiling any honest offer will sit just below. The 22K and 21K pages explain the fineness behind the numbers.

Gold loans in the UAE: keep the gold, borrow the cash

A gold loan is a secured advance: a bank, a licensed finance company or a jeweller running a lending desk holds your gold as collateral and pays you a percentage of its metal value, which you repay with profit or interest over an agreed term. When you settle, the same pieces come back to you. It suits a school-fee month, a medical bill or a business cash gap when selling would lose the design value and the making charge for ever.

Our gold loan in the UAE guide covers the practicalities: how the loan-to-value is set, how pricing is quoted, Sharia-compliant structures offered by Islamic lenders, what happens if the rate falls while your gold is pledged, and the questions to ask before signing. It also explains why only licensed lenders should ever hold your gold, and how to read a pledge receipt.

Compare the two routes with a simple test: if the total cost of borrowing for the months you need is less than the making charge and buying margin you would lose by selling and later re-buying, the loan is the cheaper path. If you are unlikely to want the gold back, selling is simpler.

Paperwork, identity and what honest buyers ask for

UAE rules on precious-metal dealers require buyers to know who they are dealing with, so a legitimate counter will ask for your Emirates ID or passport and will record the transaction. Treat a buyer who wants no ID and offers cash in an envelope as a red flag rather than a convenience; you may be helping someone launder metal, and you will have no receipt if a dispute arises.

Bring the original purchase invoice if you have it. It proves the karat and weight you bought, speeds up the test, and at a branded jeweller unlocks full exchange credit on their own pieces. For gold bought abroad or inherited, a hallmark still carries weight, and a quick test settles the rest. Our buying guide explains why the itemised invoice you get today is the document that protects you when you sell years later.

Expect to receive a buying receipt with the weight, purity, rate and amount. Payment is usually cash for small sums and bank transfer for larger ones; many buyers cap cash under anti-money-laundering rules.

Timing, seasons and the investor's angle

Buyers quote from the day's rate, so the question of when to sell is really the question of where the rate is. Nobody can predict it, but a few patterns are worth knowing. Demand for new jewellery peaks before Eid, Diwali and the wedding season, which means buyers are keen to replenish stock and margins on old gold are often tightest then. Quiet summer months and Ramadan afternoons are slow at the counters, giving you time to negotiate but fewer competing buyers.

For those who hold bars and coins rather than jewellery, selling is simpler because there is no design to lose: a recognised bar from a known refiner sells close to spot. Our gold bars page and the price analysis page discuss how investors think about exit timing without pretending to forecast.

Photograph each piece on the buyer's scale with the display visible before you accept an offer. If you decide to walk to the next counter, you have the weight on record and the second buyer cannot quietly read a lighter number.

Common mistakes when selling or pledging gold

  • Expecting the making charge back. It is a labour cost paid once; buyers price only the metal.
  • Accepting the first offer in a souk. The next counter is ten steps away and expects you to come back.
  • Selling stone-set pieces for cash. Stones are deducted; exchanging such pieces at a jeweller who values the setting often does better.
  • Pledging gold with an unlicensed lender. If the lender disappears, so does your gold, and no regulator will chase it for you.
  • Not reading the loan terms for a rate fall. Some agreements require extra collateral or partial repayment if the gold price drops during the term.
  • Forgetting VAT. Selling personal jewellery is not a taxable supply for you, but a buyer's margin may be quoted gross or net of its own VAT treatment; ask for the amount you will actually receive.

With those avoided, selling or borrowing against gold in the UAE is a same-day, well-regulated transaction, which is exactly why so many families keep part of their savings in 21K and 22K in the first place.

Questions people ask

What is the gold selling rate in Dubai today?

Buyers start from the same daily per-gram rate for each karat that shops use for selling, then subtract a buying margin that varies by counter and by whether the piece is hallmarked and plain. The live rate on this page is the ceiling. Three offers from different buyers on the same day show you the real margin, which is usually smallest on plain hallmarked 22K and 21K.

Is it better to sell gold or take a gold loan in the UAE?

Sell when you no longer want the gold or the pieces have no design value worth preserving. Borrow when the cash need is temporary and the total cost of the loan over the months you need it is lower than the making charge and buying margin you would lose by selling and later buying again. Exchange when the real goal is new jewellery.

Do I need the original invoice to sell gold in Dubai?

No, but it helps. A hallmark and a quick purity test are enough for most buyers, who will weigh and price the metal regardless. The invoice speeds the process, supports the stated karat, and at a branded jeweller can unlock full exchange credit on pieces they sold. You will need your Emirates ID or passport in every case.

Where do I get the best price for old gold in the UAE?

Plain hallmarked gold usually fetches the tightest margin at competing souk counters in Deira, Meena Bazaar, Rolla or Madinat Zayed, or at licensed buyers who publish their margin. Branded chains give their best value through exchange rather than cash. Whichever you choose, compare three offers for the same piece on the same day and photograph the scale.

Can I sell gold coins and bars as easily as jewellery?

Usually more easily. A bar or coin from a recognised refiner with intact packaging sells close to the spot price because there is no design or stone to deduct and purity is certified. Bullion dealers, exchange houses and many souk counters buy them. Unknown brands or opened packaging may be tested and priced with a wider margin.
