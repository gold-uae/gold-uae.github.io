Gold Loan in the UAE: How a Loan Against Gold Works, Costs and Risks

A gold loan in the UAE turns jewellery sitting in a drawer into cash without selling it. You hand the pieces to a licensed lender, they value the metal at the day's rate, advance you a share of that value, and keep the gold in a vault until you repay. Residents reach for this when a school fee, a medical bill or a family emergency back home lands before the next salary. This page explains who actually lends against gold here, how the sum is calculated, what it costs and when borrowing beats simply selling.

Quick answer

  • A gold loan is cash advanced against jewellery, coins or bars that you pledge and later reclaim.
  • Lenders advance a percentage of the metal value, commonly well below 100%, so the loan is always secured.
  • You pay interest or a profit rate for the months the loan runs; miss payments and the gold can be sold.
  • Loans against gold in the UAE come from licensed finance companies and some jewellers, not from the souk floor.

What a loan against gold in the UAE actually is

Strip away the paperwork and a gold loan is a pawn arrangement with modern regulation. Three things define it.

  • Collateral. Your gold is valued by weight and karat, then stored by the lender. You keep ownership; they keep possession.
  • Loan-to-value. The lender advances a fixed percentage of the metal value, never the whole amount, so that if the rate falls they are still covered.
  • Term and charge. You agree a period, often measured in months, and pay interest or, with an Islamic lender, a fixed fee or profit rate on the arrangement. At the end you repay the principal and collect the gold.

Because the security is tangible and liquid, approval is fast and your salary certificate matters far less than it would for a personal loan. That is the whole appeal: no credit score lecture, no month-long wait. The live metal value every lender starts from is shown here:

Who offers gold loans in Dubai and the other emirates

This is where many residents get confused, because the big retail banks do not advertise gold loans the way banks in Kerala or Lahore do. In the UAE the lending comes from a narrower set of players.

Licensed finance companies

A handful of regulated non-bank finance houses, several of them Sharia-compliant, run gold-backed financing products. They operate from offices rather than shop counters and are the most structured option, with written terms and a clear redemption process.

Jewellers offering buyback-with-return

Some established jewellers, particularly in Deira, Karama and Sharjah's Rolla area, will advance money against pieces you bought from them or that they have assessed, with the understanding that you return within an agreed window to redeem. Terms vary from shop to shop and are not always written as formally as a finance company's, so insist on a signed document.

What does not exist

There is no network of informal pawnbrokers, and nobody standing outside the Gold Souk should be lending you anything. If a stranger offers cash against your gold on the street, walk on.

How much you can borrow: the gold loan calculation

Every lender runs the same three-step sum, which is why the gold calculator on this site doubles as a gold loan calculator.

  1. Net weight. Stones, threads and clasps made of other metals are deducted. A 24-gram bangle pair might net 23.4 grams.
  2. Metal value. Net weight × purity factor × today's 24K rate. For 22K that factor is 0.916; for 21K it is 0.875; for 18K it is 0.750.
  3. Loan-to-value. The lender applies its percentage. If a company lends 70% of metal value and your bangles are worth a certain sum, the advance is seven-tenths of that sum.

Two points follow from this. First, higher karat means more cash per gram, so 22K bangles raise more than an 18K bracelet of the same weight. Second, the loan-to-value percentage is the number to negotiate, not the rate, because the rate is public and fixed for the day. Ask each lender for its percentage before you discuss anything else.

With Islamic products the structure is different in form: you may see a rahn (pledge) with a safekeeping fee rather than interest. The cash effect is similar, so compare the total you repay over the full term rather than the headline rate.

Ask for the total repayment amount in dirhams for your exact term, written on the agreement. It is the only figure that lets you compare two lenders honestly.

The process from valuation to redemption

A gold loan in Dubai usually completes in a single visit.

  1. Bring the gold, your Emirates ID and, if you have them, the original purchase invoices. Tourists are generally not eligible; residency is expected.
  2. The lender tests each piece. Expect an XRF reading or an acid test, and the net weight recorded on a form you sign. Check the hallmark guide first so you know what your pieces should read.
  3. You receive a valuation, the loan-to-value percentage and the proposed advance. Nothing stops you taking this on paper to a competitor.
  4. Sign the agreement, receive the cash or a transfer, and get a receipt listing every item by weight, karat and description, ideally with a photograph.
  5. Pay the instalments as agreed. Most lenders let you repay early.
  6. On final settlement the gold is returned in the condition recorded. Compare it against the receipt before you leave.

Keep that receipt as carefully as the jewellery itself; it is the only proof of what you deposited.

Risks: what happens if you cannot repay

The security that makes approval easy is also the thing you stand to lose. If instalments stop, the lender follows the default clause in the agreement: a penalty, a notice period, and then the right to sell the gold to recover the debt. Any surplus after the debt and costs should be returned to you, but a sale in a hurry rarely fetches the best price.

Two quieter risks are worth weighing. If the gold rate falls sharply during the term, some lenders can ask for a top-up payment or extra collateral to restore their margin. And if the rate rises, you still only owe the cash you borrowed, which is good for you, but you cannot sell the gold to capture the gain until it is back in your hands. Borrow a smaller percentage than the maximum offered if you want a cushion against both situations. The price analysis page explains what tends to move the rate.

Gold loan or outright sale: choosing between them

The honest comparison depends on how long you need the money and how much you value the pieces.

JewelleryReturned when you repayGone; making charges never recovered

A worked way to think about it: if you expect to need the cash for only two or three months and the gold is a wedding set you will wear for decades, borrowing is almost always cheaper than selling and buying back later with fresh making charges. If the need stretches past a year, the accumulated cost of the loan starts to approach what you would lose by selling, and selling the gold becomes the cleaner option. Families who want cash flexibility in future often shift part of their holdings into coins, which are the easiest form to pledge or sell quickly.

Questions to settle before you sign

Before you pledge anything, get clear answers to these from the person across the desk and make sure the agreement says the same.

  • What loan-to-value percentage is applied, and to which karat rate?
  • Is the rate flat or reducing, and what is the total repayment for my term?
  • Where is the gold stored and is it insured at full value?
  • Can I repay early, and is there a fee for doing so?
  • How many missed payments trigger a sale, and will I receive any surplus?
  • Will the exact pieces, not equivalent weight, be returned?

That last point matters more than people expect. Some arrangements return 'equivalent gold' rather than your own items; for inherited or wedding jewellery that is not acceptable. Choosing well here, and reading the karat guide so you understand the valuation, keeps a useful financial tool from becoming a regret.

Questions people ask

Which banks give gold loans in the UAE?

Mainstream UAE banks rarely offer a retail gold loan product. Loans against gold come mostly from licensed finance companies, several of them Sharia-compliant, and from some jewellers who advance cash against pieces they have assessed. Always confirm the lender's licence and get the terms in writing.

How much gold loan can I get per gram in Dubai?

It depends on the karat, the day's rate and the lender's loan-to-value percentage. Multiply net weight by the purity factor and the 24K rate to get the metal value, then apply the lender's percentage. The calculator on this page gives the metal value instantly; the percentage is the figure to ask each lender.

What is the gold loan interest rate in the UAE?

Rates vary by lender and move with the market, so any fixed figure quickly goes stale. Ask whether the rate is flat or reducing, monthly or annual, and request the total repayment amount for your term in dirhams. Islamic lenders may charge a safekeeping fee under a rahn structure instead of interest.

Can I get a gold loan on a tourist visa?

Generally no. Lenders expect UAE residency with a valid Emirates ID, because the agreement runs for months and they need a verifiable address. Visitors who need cash from their gold usually sell it instead, which only requires a passport.

Is a gold loan cheaper than a personal loan?

Often, because the loan is fully secured and approval does not depend on your salary. The trade-off is that you must have enough gold to pledge, and your jewellery is out of reach until you repay. Compare the total cost over your actual term rather than the headline rate.

Do I get the same jewellery back after repaying?

You should, and the agreement must say so. The receipt should list every piece by weight, karat and description, ideally with a photo. Avoid any arrangement that promises only equivalent gold by weight, especially for wedding or inherited pieces.
