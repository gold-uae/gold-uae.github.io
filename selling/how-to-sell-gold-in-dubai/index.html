How to Sell Gold in Dubai: Where to Go and What You Will Be Paid

Selling gold in Dubai is quick, legal and usually fair, because every buyer works from the same published per-gram rate that you can see on the boards in every shop. What separates a good deal from a poor one is the deduction the buyer takes from that rate, and how honestly your piece is weighed and tested. This page walks you through the whole process: who buys, how the selling rate is calculated, what to carry with you and how to get the strongest offer for anything from a single ring to a wedding set.

Quick answer

  • You are paid for the metal only: weight multiplied by purity multiplied by today's rate, minus a small margin.
  • Making charges you paid when buying are gone; coins and bars lose far less than ornate jewellery.
  • The Deira Gold Souk, Meena Bazaar and brand buyback counters are the usual places to sell for cash.
  • Bring your Emirates ID or passport and the original invoice if you still have it.
  • Get two or three quotes on the same day; the rate is identical everywhere, the deduction is not.

How the gold selling rate in Dubai is calculated

Think of selling as the reverse of buying, minus the extras. When you bought a 22K bangle you paid for the metal, the making charge and 5% VAT on the whole amount. When you sell it, only the metal comes back. The buyer multiplies the net weight by the purity factor (0.916 for 22K, 0.875 for 21K, 0.750 for 18K), multiplies that by the day's 24K rate, then subtracts a margin to cover refining and profit.

Say you hand over a 21K chain weighing 14.2 grams. The buyer takes 14.2 × 0.875 to get the pure-gold content, applies the 24K per-gram rate, and offers you that figure less roughly one to four percent depending on the shop and the state of the piece. Stones, enamel and solder joints are weighed out first, which is why a heavy-looking necklace can come back lighter on the scale than you expected.

You can rehearse the arithmetic before you leave home with the gold calculator: enter the weight and karat and you have the metal value the quote should sit just below.

Where to sell gold in Dubai for cash

There is no shortage of gold buyers near you in this city, but they fall into distinct groups with different habits.

The Deira Gold Souk

The lanes around Al Ras hold the highest density of buyers anywhere in the Emirates. Many retail counters buy as well as sell, and a cluster of dedicated buyback shops sits on the side streets off the main covered walkway. Competition keeps deductions thin, and you can walk ten metres to a rival for a second opinion. The Dubai Gold Souk guide covers the layout.

Meena Bazaar and Karama

The Bur Dubai side is quieter and the shops are used to residents selling a few pieces to raise money before a holiday or a visa renewal. Prices are close to the souk, and parking is easier.

Brand buyback counters

The large chains run exchange and buyback desks inside their mall branches. They are the best choice if you bought from the same brand and still have the invoice, because the purity is already on record and the deduction is often reduced or waived for exchanges.

Gold refiners and bullion dealers

If you are selling bars or coins rather than ornaments, bullion dealers in Deira and the DMCC area pay closer to spot than a jewellery shop will.

Step by step: selling a piece on the day

  1. Check the rate first. Look at the board or your phone so you know the 24K and 22K numbers before anyone quotes you.
  2. Clean the piece. A quick wipe removes grime that adds nothing to the weight but makes the buyer suspicious.
  3. Watch the test. The shop will rub the piece on a touchstone, use an acid kit or run it through an XRF machine. Stay at the counter while it happens.
  4. Watch the scale. Ask to see the display, confirm it reads in grams, and note the weight yourself.
  5. Ask for the working. A good buyer tells you the weight, the purity factor, the rate and the deduction. If they give only a lump sum, ask them to break it down.
  6. Compare. Take the written quote to a second shop. Nobody is offended; it is how the souk works.
  7. Show ID and sign. You will give a copy of your Emirates ID or passport and sign a purchase note. Cash is normal for small sums; larger amounts are often paid by transfer.
Sell in the morning. Rates update during the day and buyers tend to be more generous when the shop is quiet and the day's rate has just been posted.

Getting the best price from gold buyers near you

A few habits lift your offer without any drama.

  • Know your karat. Look for the stamp: 916 means 22K, 875 means 21K, 750 means 18K. Read the hallmark guide if the mark is worn.
  • Keep the invoice. A receipt showing the karat and weight shortens the test and removes the buyer's excuse to assume a lower purity.
  • Sell by weight, not by piece. Group everything of the same karat together so it is weighed once and quoted as a single lot.
  • Do not accept a flat per-gram offer below the 24K rate without a stated deduction. Ask what percentage is being taken.
  • Consider an exchange. If you are going to buy new jewellery anyway, exchanging old for new at the same shop usually gives you full metal value with no deduction at all, and only the making charge on the new piece to pay.

For Indian and Pakistani residents, it also helps to know what the sale is worth back home; the rupee rate page converts the Dubai rate live.

Tricks to avoid when you sell gold

Dubai is tightly regulated and outright fraud is rare, but a few practices quietly reduce what you are paid.

  • The slow scale. A scale that is not on a level surface or has not been zeroed will read light. Ask to see it zeroed with nothing on the pan.
  • Downgrading the karat. Claiming a 22K piece is 'only 21K' because the mark is faint is the commonest way to shave three or four percent. Insist on an XRF reading if you disagree.
  • Weighing stones as waste. Small stones are legitimately deducted, but the deduction should be the actual stone weight, not a round number.
  • Street approaches. Anyone outside a shop offering to buy 'at a better rate' is to be ignored. Sell only inside a licensed shop with a trade licence on the wall.
  • Quotes that expire in minutes. Pressure to decide on the spot is a sign to walk away and compare.

If your gold was bought abroad and you are unsure of its purity, have it tested by a neutral assay office before you sell; it costs little and removes the guesswork from the quote.

Selling versus borrowing against your gold

Not everyone who walks into a buyer actually wants to part with their jewellery. If you need cash for a short period, a gold loan lets you pledge the pieces, receive a percentage of their metal value, and reclaim them when you repay. You keep the ornaments and avoid the deduction entirely, but you pay a profit rate or interest for as long as the loan runs.

Selling makes more sense when the pieces are old-fashioned and you would not wear them again, when the rate is historically high and you are happy to lock in the gain, or when you need the money for longer than a few months. Borrowing makes more sense for a wedding set you will certainly want back or a rate you believe is temporarily low. Neither choice is wrong; only the timing and your attachment to the pieces decide it. The selling and loans hub compares both routes side by side.

After the sale: what the paperwork means

Every licensed buyer keeps a record of who sold what, which is why you are asked for identification. The purchase note you sign lists the weight, karat and amount paid; keep your copy, because it is proof of a legitimate sale if a question ever arises about the origin of the gold. There is no capital-gains tax on a personal sale in the UAE, and nothing is deducted at source. If you are sending the proceeds home as a remittance, the exchange house may ask for the note when the sum is large, so take a photograph of it before you leave the counter. Finally, remember that the amount you were offered is tied to the rate at that moment; if you walk out to think about it, tomorrow's offer will follow tomorrow's board, which you can check on the Dubai rate page before heading back.

Questions people ask

What is the gold selling rate today in Dubai?

There is no separate selling rate. Buyers start from the same per-gram rate shown on every shop board and on this site, then deduct a margin of a few percent. The live strip above gives the 24K, 22K, 21K and 18K figures; your offer should sit just below the figure for your karat multiplied by the weight.

Can I sell gold in Dubai without a receipt?

Yes. A receipt speeds things up and supports the karat you claim, but it is not required. The shop will test the piece and weigh it, and you only need to show Emirates ID or a passport. Gold bought years ago or inherited is sold every day without any invoice.

Do I need an Emirates ID to sell gold?

You need valid identification. Residents use their Emirates ID; tourists use their passport. The buyer records your details on the purchase note because licensed dealers must be able to show who sold them each lot. Without ID a reputable shop will refuse the transaction.

Is it better to sell gold in Dubai or in India?

The metal is worth the same everywhere, so the question is who deducts less. Dubai buyers are competitive and there is no tax on a personal sale, while in India a buyer may deduct more and the rate includes import duty. Many residents prefer to sell here and remit the cash rather than carry gold home.

Will I get the full price for gold jewellery I bought last year?

You get the metal value, not the price you paid. Making charges and VAT are not refunded, so a piece bought with a 12% making charge will come back with roughly that much missing, unless the rate has risen enough to cover the gap. Exchanging old for new at the same shop avoids most of the loss.
