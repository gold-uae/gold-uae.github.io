Gold Bar Price in Dubai: 1 g to 1 kg Bars, PAMP and Local Refineries, What You Really Pay

A gold bar is the plainest way to own gold in the UAE: no making charge, no design to argue about, no stones, just 24K metal with a refinery stamp. The price you pay is the day's 24K rate multiplied by the weight, plus a premium that covers refining, minting, packaging and the dealer's margin. That premium is the whole game. It can be a few percent on a 1 g gift bar or a fraction of a percent on a 1 kg cast bar, and it varies between a souk bullion desk, a mall jeweller and a bank. The live prices below update through the day; the rest of this page explains how to read them.

Quick answer

  • A gold bar in Dubai is priced at the live 24K rate per gram plus a small premium for the refinery and size.
  • The premium per gram shrinks as the bar grows: 1 g bars cost far more per gram than 100 g bars.
  • Bars of 99% purity or higher that are tradable carry zero VAT in the UAE.
  • PAMP, Valcambi and Dubai-based refineries all sell back easily; sealed packaging matters more than the brand.
  • A 1 kg bar is a serious purchase; most residents start at 10 g or 20 g.

Standard sizes sold in Dubai run from 1 g wafers to 1 kg cast bars, with 2.5 g, 5 g, 10 g, 20 g, 50 g, 100 g, 250 g and 500 g in between. South Asian buyers also ask for the tola bar (11.6638 g), which Deira dealers stock in 1, 5 and 10 tola weights. The kilobar is the trade unit; everything smaller is a retail product with retail packaging.

Because the AED is fixed to the dollar, these numbers move exactly with the international ounce price divided by 31.1035 and converted at 3.6725. The 24K page shows the per-gram rate on its own, and the calculator handles odd weights like a 2 tola biscuit.

What decides the premium on a gold bar

Two bars of the same weight from two counters a few doors apart in the Gold Souk can differ in price. The metal is identical, so the gap is entirely premium, and the premium depends on five things.

  • Size: minting a 1 g wafer costs almost as much as minting a 10 g one, so the small bar carries that cost over far fewer grams. The curve flattens sharply from 50 g upward.
  • Minted versus cast: minted bars are stamped from rolled sheet, bright and precise, and sealed in a card; cast bars are poured, rougher and cheaper per gram. At 100 g and above many buyers choose cast.
  • Refinery brand: internationally accredited Swiss brands command a slightly higher premium than UAE refineries, though both are accepted for buyback in Dubai.
  • Packaging and assay card: a tamper-evident pack with a serial number and assayer signature is worth paying for because it sells back without a test.
  • Seller type: bullion specialists price tightest; mall jewellers add margin for the showroom; banks sit in between but give you a bank receipt.

A fair rule: pay a premium that you can recover in a modest price rise. On a 1 g bar that may take a long move; on a 100 g bar, a small one. Premiums also widen briefly when demand spikes before Diwali and the Eids.

For a UAE resident the practical choice is between a Swiss minted pack and a local minted pack. The Swiss pack travels better; the local pack is a little cheaper and just as liquid in Deira, Sharjah or Abu Dhabi. Either way, buy sealed. Where to buy a gold bar in Dubai and the other emirates

The bar market is concentrated. You will not find bullion at every jewellery counter, and prices tighten the closer you get to the trade.

  1. Bullion dealers around the Deira Gold Souk and the Gold Souk Extension: the deepest stock from 10 g to 1 kg, tola bars, and the thinnest premiums. Many sit on the upper floors of the buildings near the Al Ras metro exit rather than in the ground-floor windows. See the souk guide.
  2. Large jewellery chains: every branch sells 1 g to 50 g minted bars in gift packs; 100 g and above may need ordering. Fixed, published premiums and easy buyback at any branch.
  3. Banks and exchange houses: a few sell bars over the counter or through an app, delivered to a branch or held in storage.
  4. Gold & Diamond Park on Sheikh Zayed Road: several bullion and refinery offices alongside the jewellers, convenient if you live in the Marina, Jumeirah or Al Barsha side of town.
  5. Abu Dhabi and Sharjah: Madinat Zayed Gold Centre and the Rolla area have bullion desks at the same rate as Dubai with less queueing.

Call ahead for anything above 100 g; many dealers hold large bars at a vault rather than in the shop and need a few hours' notice. The wholesalers page explains which trade counters serve walk-in buyers.

Ask the dealer for both prices before you buy: the price to sell you the bar and the price they would pay to buy the same bar back today. The gap between them is your real cost. A tight gap on a bar you can resell anywhere beats a cheap headline price on a brand only one shop will take.

VAT on gold bars: when it is zero and when it is not

The UAE charges 5% VAT on jewellery but zero-rates investment-grade gold, which is why a bar is cheaper to own than a bangle of the same weight. The rule has conditions, and a few bars fall outside it.

  • The gold must be at least 99% pure. All 999 and 999.9 bars qualify; a 22K "bar" sold as a pendant does not.
  • It must be tradable on the bullion market, meaning a recognised refinery form such as a bar, wafer or bullion coin. A 24K ornament is not a bar.
  • The invoice should show the item as investment gold with the weight, purity and serial. Keep it: it is your proof of zero-rating and your proof of ownership on resale.
  • Decorative packaging, gift boxes or a framed bar can tip a product into the taxable category at some sellers, so ask before paying.

Businesses trading gold between each other use a reverse-charge mechanism, which is why wholesale invoices look different from retail ones. Tourists do not need to claim a refund on a zero-rated bar because no VAT was charged, but they should keep the invoice for customs at home. The full picture is on the VAT and refund page, and anyone carrying bars to India should read the customs page first, since bars are treated more strictly than worn jewellery.

Which size to buy: 1 g, 10 g, 100 g or 1 kg

Size is a trade-off between flexibility and cost. Think about how you will sell before you decide how to buy.

1 g to 5 g

Gift sizes. The premium per gram is the highest of any bar, so they are poor value for saving but ideal for a birth, a graduation or an Eidi envelope. If you are buying ten of them for yourself, buy one 10 g bar instead.

10 g and 20 g

The sweet spot for most salaried residents: affordable in a single purchase, a sensible step down in premium, and small enough to sell one at a time when a bill arrives. The 10 g bar, often called a gold biscuit by South Asian buyers, is the most traded retail size in Dubai.

50 g and 100 g

Where the premium becomes almost negligible and cast bars become an option. Suits people who have accumulated a few smaller bars and want to consolidate, or who receive an annual bonus in one lump.

250 g, 500 g and 1 kg

Trade sizes. A 1 kg bar is the cheapest gold per gram you can buy in Dubai, but it is also indivisible: to raise part of its value you must sell the whole thing. Buyers at this level usually arrange vault storage and sometimes buy through a bank. The coins page is the better route if you want the same total in pieces you can sell separately.

Storing gold bars safely in the UAE

Once you hold more than a few bars, storage becomes the real cost of ownership and the main risk. The options residents use, roughly in order of cost:

  • Home safe: bolted down, in a bedroom cupboard rather than the obvious wardrobe. Fine for a handful of 10 g and 20 g bars. Check whether your home contents insurance covers gold and up to what limit; many policies cap valuables.
  • Bank safe deposit box: an annual fee, access during branch hours, and your own key. The usual answer for 100 g to a few hundred grams.
  • Dealer or vault storage: some bullion dealers and specialised vaults in Dubai store allocated bars in your name for a yearly percentage fee, with insurance included and buyback on request. Convenient for larger holdings but adds a counterparty.
  • Bank gold accounts: you own grams on a statement rather than a specific bar; cheapest to hold, but you depend on the bank and may pay a fee to take delivery.

Keep the sealed packaging intact wherever the bars live, and store invoices separately from the gold. A photograph of each serial number on your phone takes a minute and settles any later dispute.

Selling a gold bar back in Dubai

Liquidity is the strongest argument for bars over jewellery. Any gold shop in the UAE will buy a sealed, serial-numbered bar at a small discount to the day's 24K rate, usually within minutes. The process is short: the dealer checks the pack and serial, weighs it, may run an XRF scan if the seal is broken, and pays cash or transfer. Bars that match the dealer's own stock or come from well-known refineries get the best price; obscure brands or opened packs get tested and discounted.

Timing a sale is not possible, but choosing where to sell is. Bullion desks pay closer to spot than mall counters, which treat buyback as a service for their own customers. If you bought from a chain, their own branch may offer the best price on their own branded bar. Compare two quotes on the same morning before parting with a 100 g bar; the difference can be noticeable.

If you need cash for a short period and expect to want the gold back, a gold loan against the bar may cost less than the round-trip spread of selling and rebuying. The gold loan page explains how lenders value bars, and how to sell gold in Dubai walks through the paperwork and the questions to ask at the counter.

Questions people ask

What is the price of a 10 gram gold bar in Dubai today?

The metal value is ten times the live 24K per-gram rate, which you can see in the bar table above. Dealers then add a premium for minting, packaging and margin, which is smallest at bullion desks in Deira and larger at mall jewellers. Ask for the total and work back to the premium per gram so you can compare two shops fairly. No VAT applies on a 999.9 bar.

Is a PAMP gold bar worth the extra premium?

If you may sell the bar outside the UAE, a Swiss pack with a serial and assay card is the most widely recognised and easiest to sell. If you will sell in Dubai, a sealed bar from a reputable local refinery is accepted everywhere and often costs a little less. Either way, the sealed packaging and matching invoice matter more than the logo.

How much is a 1 kg gold bar in Dubai?

A kilobar is simply one thousand times the live 24K per-gram rate plus the smallest premium of any size, often a fraction of a percent at a trade counter. It is the cheapest way to own gold per gram, but it cannot be sold in parts, large transactions attract identity checks, and most buyers arrange vault storage. Check the live bar table for today's metal value.

Is there VAT on gold bars in the UAE?

No, as long as the bar is at least 99% pure and in a tradable bullion form, it is zero-rated. Jewellery and ornamental items are charged 5%. Make sure the invoice describes the bar as investment gold with its weight, purity and serial number, and keep it, because that invoice is what proves the zero-rating and your ownership when you sell.

Which is better, a gold bar or gold jewellery, for investment?

For pure investment the bar wins: no making charge, no VAT, 24K purity and the best buyback price. Jewellery carries making charges you never recover plus 5% VAT, and is bought back at metal value only. Jewellery makes sense when you want to wear it; bars make sense when you want the metal and nothing else.

Can I carry gold bars from Dubai to India or Pakistan?

You can, but bars are treated as dutiable gold on arrival, and the duty-free jewellery allowance for returning Indian residents does not cover bars. Declare them, carry the Dubai invoice, and check the latest customs notice before travelling because rates change. Pakistan and other home countries have their own declaration rules. Our customs page covers the Indian case in detail.
