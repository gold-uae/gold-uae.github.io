Gold Earrings in Dubai: Studs, Hoops, Jhumkas and Nose Rings Priced Honestly

Earrings are the cheapest way into real gold jewellery in the UAE, which is exactly why they are the first thing most residents buy and the piece they get wrong most often. A pair of studs that looks identical in two Deira shop windows can differ by a third in price because one is hollow and the other is solid, or because one shop quotes a flat charge and the other a percentage. This page walks through how ladies gold earrings are actually priced here, what each style typically weighs, and how to read a receipt for studs, hoops, danglers and a gold nose ring.

Quick answer

  • A pair of gold earrings in Dubai costs the metal weight at the day's karat rate plus a making charge.
  • Small studs weigh 1-3 g; everyday hoops 3-8 g; a bridal jhumka pair can pass 20 g.
  • Gold nose rings and pins are usually 18K or 22K and weigh well under a gram.
  • Ask for the weight, the karat and the making charge separately before any discount talk.
  • Lightweight pieces carry a higher making charge per gram than heavy ones.

How the price of gold earrings is built in Dubai

Every honest gold shop in the UAE prices earrings the same way: the net gold weight in grams multiplied by today's per-gram rate for the karat, plus a making charge, plus 5% VAT on the whole amount. The rate part is public and identical in every emirate, so the only things a shop controls are the weight it puts into the design and the charge it adds for the work.

Earrings have two features that push the making charge up compared with a chain or a bangle. First, they are small, so the labour of soldering posts, hinges and screw backs is spread over very few grams. Second, most pairs carry a second component: screw backs, butterfly clips or wire hooks, which may be a different karat from the main piece. A good shop weighs the finished pair with the backs on and tells you if the backs are 18K while the fronts are 22K.

Check what today's rate looks like before you walk in, so the first number on the receipt means something to you.

Hollow hoops are the thing to watch. A 40 mm hoop can weigh 3 g if it is tube-formed or 9 g if solid, and both are sold as "22K hoops". Neither is wrong, but the hollow version dents easily and is harder to repair, so the lower price is buying you less durability as well as less gold.

Which karat suits which earring

Choosing the karat is about wear, not just purity. Earrings hang from a thin post or wire that takes stress every time you put them on, and softer gold bends faster.

  • 22K (916) is the default for Indian and Pakistani buyers in Meena Bazaar and Karama, and the right choice for jhumkas and heavier danglers where resale weight matters. Posts in 22K can bend if forced through a tight piercing, so many shops fit slightly harder backs.
  • 21K (875) is what most Emirati, Egyptian, Jordanian and Lebanese customers ask for in Deira and in Sharjah's Central Souk. It keeps a warm colour, holds its shape better than 22K and is priced a little lower per gram. Read more on the 21K rate page.
  • 18K (750) is the sensible choice for gold stud earrings with diamonds or coloured stones, because the prongs must grip. It is also the standard for white gold and rose gold hoops. The 18K page explains why its per-gram rate is lower.
  • 24K earrings exist but are rare and impractical: the metal is too soft for a working post.

Gold nose rings and pins: tiny weight, different rules

A gold nose ring is the one piece of jewellery where the gold value is almost irrelevant. A plain 22K nose pin can weigh a fifth of a gram, so the metal is a few dirhams and everything else is the making charge, the stone and the shop's minimum ticket. Expect to pay a flat amount rather than a per-gram figure, and do not expect a discount on it.

What to check on a nose pin

  • The wire gauge: a thick wire will not pass through a healed piercing and a thin one bends when you sleep on it.
  • The closure: L-shaped pins stay put; straight pins fall out; screw-type nath rings need a steady hand.
  • The stone setting: single CZ or a tiny diamond; ask whether it is bezel-set (safer) or prong-set.
  • The karat: 18K holds its shape better in such a thin wire, which is why many mall jewellers only stock 18K nose pins.

Bridal naths, the larger ring with a chain to the hair, are a different product again, usually 22K, 4-10 g, and sold with the same per-gram logic as danglers. For the wider set, pair it with items from the necklace and set guide.

Where to buy gold earrings in the UAE

Where you shop changes the mix of design choice, price and negotiation room more than for almost any other jewellery, because earrings are a volume item that every counter stocks.

  • Deira Gold Souk and the covered extension: the deepest range of 21K and 22K studs and hoops, with trays sorted by weight. The best place to compare three shops in ten minutes. See the souk guide.
  • Meena Bazaar and Karama: south Indian and Pakistani designs, jhumkas and temple-style danglers; shops here are used to price-per-gram bargaining.
  • Gold & Diamond Park on Sheikh Zayed Road: 18K diamond studs and custom work, with the workshop often behind the counter.
  • Mall branches of the big chains: fixed prices and clear receipts; good for gift-wrapped studs where you want a certificate and an easy exchange policy.
  • Sharjah Rolla and Abu Dhabi's Madinat Zayed: same rate, quieter counters, and many Arab-style 21K hoops that Dubai malls do not carry.
Ask the shop to put the pair on the scale in front of you and read the grams aloud before they print the bill. Then ask for the making charge as a separate line. If the total does not equal weight times rate plus charge plus VAT, something is bundled.

Reading the receipt and bargaining on the right line

The gold rate is fixed for the day, so negotiating on it is pointless. The making charge is where a shop has room, and the room is bigger on heavier, plainer pieces and on pairs it has held in stock for a while.

  1. Confirm the karat stamp on the post or the hoop closure, and that the receipt matches it.
  2. Confirm the net weight in grams including backs; if backs are a different karat, they should be weighed separately.
  3. Ask whether the making charge is per gram or a percentage of the gold value, and get it as a number.
  4. Ask whether stones are included in the gold weight (they should not be) and what the stone line costs.
  5. Check the VAT line is 5% of the subtotal, and keep the receipt for any future resale or tourist VAT refund.

On a plain 6 g pair of 22K hoops, a reasonable ask is a lower per-gram charge; on a 1.5 g pair of studs the shop will usually hold its flat minimum and offer a free pair of screw backs instead. Our making charges page goes deeper on what is negotiable.

Gold earrings design trends in the UAE right now

Gold earrings design in Dubai sits between three tastes: Gulf Arab (21K, polished, geometric hoops and drops), South Asian (22K, jhumkas, temple motifs, chandbalis) and international (18K, minimal studs, huggies, ear cuffs). Most souk shops stock all three, grouped by tray.

  • Huggies and small chunky hoops in 18K and 21K are the everyday choice for office wear; they are solid, so the price is honest per gram.
  • Ear stacks of two or three tiny studs are sold as sets; check each piece is weighed, not a single "set price".
  • Chandbali and jhumka remain the wedding staples, and prices rise with the Diwali and wedding season demand around November to February.
  • Two-tone and rose gold hoops in 18K suit buyers who want the look of a designer piece at souk prices; see the coloured gold page.
  • Coin and calligraphy drops in 21K are popular Eid gifts and often come in matched sets with a chain or pendant.

Fashion styles age faster than classic ones, which matters on resale: a plain hoop sells back at close to gold value anywhere, while an elaborate stone-set dangler is usually bought back for its metal weight only.

Caring for, repairing and reselling earrings

Earrings are lost more often than any other gold piece, so the care advice is mostly about the backs. Screw backs beat butterfly clips for anything over 2 g; many Deira shops will swap them for a small fee at purchase. Keep hoops in a separate pouch so they do not scratch softer 22K studs.

Repairs are cheap and quick in the souk: a snapped post, a loose hinge or a missing stone are usually fixed while you wait at a workshop behind the counter. In the Gold & Diamond Park the manufacturer is often on site and can re-tip prongs on 18K diamond studs.

When it is time to sell, a shop will weigh the pair, confirm the karat with an acid or XRF test, and pay the day's buyback rate for the metal; making charges are never refunded. Earrings with stones are valued on gold only, with the stones returned or ignored. The selling guide explains how buyback rates compare with the board rate, and the rings page covers the same logic for solitaires.

Questions people ask

How much does a pair of gold earrings cost in Dubai?

There is no single price because earrings are sold by weight. Take the pair's net grams, multiply by today's rate for its karat, add the shop's making charge and 5% VAT. A 2 g pair of 22K studs and a 20 g pair of jhumkas are ten times apart on the gold line alone. Use the live strip above and the calculator for the metal part, then budget the making charge on top.

Are gold stud earrings a good first purchase?

They are the lowest-ticket way into real gold and easy to resell, but the making charge per gram is the highest of any jewellery type because the labour is spread over so little metal. If resale value matters to you, plain 21K or 22K studs over 2 g are better value than very light 1 g pieces or stone-set studs.

What karat is a gold nose ring in the UAE?

Most nose pins sold in Dubai malls are 18K because the wire is so thin that softer karats bend. Souk shops in Meena Bazaar and Deira also carry 22K pins and bridal naths for South Asian customers. Either way the gold weight is tiny, so you are paying mainly for the making and the stone, not the metal.

Are hollow gold hoop earrings worth buying?

Hollow hoops give you a large look for a fraction of the weight, which keeps the price low, but they dent easily and cannot always be repaired. If you want a hoop to wear daily and to resell later, choose a solid one and accept the higher gold cost. For an occasional statement piece, hollow is a fair compromise, as long as the receipt states the true weight.

Can I get the VAT back on earrings bought in Dubai?

Tourists can reclaim the 5% VAT on jewellery through the Planet refund scheme at the airport, as long as the single purchase is at least AED 250, the shop is registered and the goods leave the UAE within 90 days. Residents cannot claim it. Keep the tax invoice and the tagged receipt the shop gives you at purchase.

Do shops in Dubai exchange earrings for a different design?

Most chains and many souk shops will exchange a pair bought from them for the gold value against a new piece, sometimes with a deduction. The making charge you paid is lost in the exchange, which is why it pays to keep it low on pieces you may want to swap later. Ask about the exchange policy before paying and get it on the receipt.
