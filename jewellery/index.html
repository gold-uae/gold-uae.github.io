Gold Jewellery in Dubai: Prices, Karats and Where to Buy

Gold jewellery is the reason most people walk into a UAE gold shop, and the pricing is simpler than it looks once you know the three parts: the metal, the workmanship and the tax. The metal is the weight in grams multiplied by today's rate for that karat, the same figure on every board from Deira to Madinat Zayed. The workmanship is the making charge, which varies by design and by shop. The tax is 5% VAT on the total. This hub explains each part, shows how karat choice changes the bill, and routes you to our detailed guides on rings, chains, necklace sets, bangles and earrings.

Quick answer

  • Gold jewellery in Dubai is priced as today's per-gram rate for the karat plus a making charge plus 5% VAT.
  • Every shop in the UAE works from the same daily rate; the making charge is where prices differ.
  • 22K is the default for Indian-style pieces, 21K for Gulf designs, 18K for diamond and Italian work.
  • Weigh the piece, ask for the rate and the making charge separately, and keep the invoice for resale.
  • Tourists can reclaim VAT on jewellery at departure through the Planet refund desks.

How gold jewellery price in Dubai is calculated

Pick up any piece and ask the salesperson for three numbers: the weight, the per-gram rate for its karat, and the making charge. Multiply weight by rate to get the metal value. Add the making charge, which is quoted either as a flat amount per gram or as a percentage of the metal value. Add 5% VAT on the total and you have the price on the receipt.

Because the per-gram rate is published daily and used across all seven emirates, the metal part of the bill is identical whether you buy in Sharjah's Central Souk or a mall in Abu Dhabi. The making charge is where shops compete and where you negotiate. A plain machine-made chain might carry a small charge per gram; a hand-set bridal necklace can carry several times that.

One habit worth keeping: write down the rate shown on the board the moment you enter, because it is updated during the day and the shop will bill you at the figure current when you pay, not when you started browsing.

Which karat for which piece

Karat is purity, and purity decides both the colour and how well a piece survives daily wear. The UAE market stocks four karats side by side, which is unusual and useful.

KaratPurityTypical use in the UAE
24K999Bars, coins, a few Chinese-style pendants; too soft for everyday jewellery
22K916Indian and Pakistani bridal sets, chains, bangles; the Kerala and Mumbai standard
21K875Gulf, Egyptian and Levantine designs; the local favourite
18K750Italian chains, diamond settings, white and rose gold, branded pieces

A higher karat costs more per gram and holds more resale value per gram, but 22K bends and scratches more easily than 18K. For a ring you wear every day, many jewellers steer you towards 18K or 21K; for a bangle that sits in a locker between weddings, 22K keeps more of its value. Making charges: the number that actually varies

Since the metal price is fixed by the daily rate, the making charge is the only line you can really shop around on. In the souk it is quoted per gram and falls as weight rises; in branded stores it is more often a percentage, sometimes with a minimum per piece. Intricate filigree, stone-setting and antique finishes push it up; plain polished bangles and machine chains keep it down.

Three things worth knowing. First, "zero making charge" promotions during the Dubai Shopping Festival and around Diwali are genuine but usually limited to selected designs, and the metal is still billed at the full rate. Second, when you sell a piece back, the making charge is not returned; you get the metal value minus a small deduction, so a low making charge protects your resale. Third, VAT applies to the making charge as well as the metal.

Our dedicated page on making charges in Dubai has a worked comparison across chain, ring and set styles.

Ask for the making charge "per gram, before VAT" in every shop you visit, write it next to the shop name, and compare only that number. The rate and the tax are the same everywhere.

Where to buy gold jewellery in the UAE

Dubai concentrates the trade, but every emirate has a gold row. The classic destination is the Deira Gold Souk at Al Ras, where a few hundred small shops sit shoulder to shoulder and the design range is widest, especially for 21K and 22K. Meena Bazaar in Bur Dubai and the shops around Karama lean towards South Indian and Pakistani tastes. The Gold and Diamond Park on Sheikh Zayed Road is calmer, air-conditioned and better for custom 18K work.

Outside Dubai, the Madinat Zayed Gold Centre in Abu Dhabi is the capital's equivalent of the souk, the Central Souk and Rolla in Sharjah are strong on 21K, and Ajman and Al Ain have compact souks with friendly pricing. Mall jewellers in every emirate give you fixed prices, air conditioning and easy returns in exchange for slightly higher making charges.

Online shopping has grown: the large chains sell 22K jewellery through their own UAE sites with delivery and in-store collection. The same three-part pricing applies, and the rate is locked at checkout. The jewellers and brands page compares the big names, and gold shops near me lists the rows in each neighbourhood.

Season, hallmark and the questions to ask

Timing matters more for stock than for price. Before Eid, Diwali and the winter wedding season, shops fill their windows with new sets and run making-charge offers; after the season, the best designs are sold out but the floor is quieter and salespeople have more time to negotiate. The international rate, not the calendar, drives the metal price, so check our price history chart if you are deciding whether to wait.

Every piece sold in the UAE should carry a purity stamp, and reputable shops will test on an electronic tester or an XRF machine in front of you if you ask. The hallmark page explains what the stamps look like and how to read them.

At the counter, ask these in order: What is the exact weight on the scale? What rate are you billing, and for which karat? What is the making charge per gram? Is any part of this piece hollow or stone-set, and is stone weight deducted from the gold weight? Does the invoice show weight, karat and rate separately? A shop that answers all five without hesitation is a shop you can buy from.

Caring for gold jewellery in the Gulf climate

Heat, humidity, sunscreen and chlorine are the four enemies of jewellery in the UAE. High-karat gold does not tarnish, but perfume and lotion dull its shine and pool and sea water loosen stone settings. Put jewellery on last when dressing and take it off first; rinse 18K and stone-set pieces in lukewarm water with a drop of dish soap and dry with a soft cloth.

Soft 22K bangles should be stored separately so they do not scratch each other, ideally in cloth pouches rather than loose in a box. Most souk shops offer free ultrasonic cleaning and polishing for pieces bought from them; branded stores usually do too, so keep the invoice.

Finally, keep a simple record for each piece: weight, karat, rate on the day and the making charge. If you ever need a gold loan or decide to sell, that record is exactly what the buyer will ask for, and it settles any dispute about what you paid for.

Questions people ask

Is gold jewellery cheaper in Dubai than in India or the UK?

The metal costs the same worldwide, but Dubai has no import duty, only 5% VAT that tourists can reclaim, and fierce competition that keeps making charges low. For plain 22K pieces the saving is real; for heavily worked or branded 18K items it is smaller. Our comparison page walks through the arithmetic.

Can I buy 22K gold jewellery online in Dubai?

Yes. The large UAE jewellers run their own online stores with 22K and 21K ranges, home delivery across the emirates and in-store collection. The price is built the same way as in the shop, metal at the locked rate plus making charge plus VAT, and the invoice shows weight and karat. Check the return window before paying, since gold is usually exchange-only.

What is the difference between a gold set and buying pieces separately?

A set is a matched necklace, earrings and often a ring or bangles sold together with one making charge. Buying separately lets you mix karats and spread spending, but matching a pattern later is hard. For weddings the set is usually better value per gram; for everyday wear separate pieces give more flexibility.

Does the price change if I pay by card instead of cash?

Some souk shops quote a slightly better making charge for cash because card fees come out of their margin, and most will accept bank transfer for larger purchases. Mall and branded stores price the same either way. Whatever you pay with, insist on a VAT invoice that lists weight, karat and rate, because the tourist refund and any future resale depend on it.

How much gold jewellery can I carry out of the UAE?

The UAE itself does not limit what you take out, but your destination does. India allows a small duty-free allowance for returning residents and taxes the rest; other countries have their own thresholds and declaration rules. Read our Dubai to India customs guide and check your own customs authority before you buy a heavy set.
