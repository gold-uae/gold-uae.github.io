Gold Necklace Price in Dubai: 22K Necklace Sets, Chokers and Pendants

Necklaces are where the UAE gold trade shows its range. At one end sit delicate 18K pendants on fine chains, bought as birthday and graduation gifts; at the other sit 22K bridal sets of a hundred grams or more, chosen over several visits by whole families before a wedding in Kerala, Lahore or Sharjah. The pricing logic is the same for both, but the numbers scale fast with weight. This guide explains how a gold necklace price in Dubai is calculated, what a 22K necklace set with price actually looks like on an invoice, the main styles sold in the UAE from chokers to long haars, and the questions that separate a good bridal purchase from an expensive mistake.

Quick answer

  • A gold necklace set in Dubai is priced on total weight times the karat rate, plus making charge and 5% VAT.
  • Sets are heavy: a 22K bridal set often runs 40 to 120 grams, so the metal rate dominates the price.
  • Chokers sit at 15 to 40 grams; long haars and layered necklaces go well beyond that.
  • Stone-set necklaces should show stone weight separately on the invoice, or you pay gold rate for stones.
  • Buy sets off-season and compare making charge per gram; the per-gram rate is identical in every shop.

Gold necklace price in Dubai: the three lines on the invoice

Every necklace or set sold in the UAE should be billed in three visible parts. Metal: total gold weight in grams multiplied by today's per-gram rate for the karat stamped on the piece. Making charge: a percentage of the metal value or a per-gram figure, higher for hand-worked and stone-set designs. VAT: 5% on the sum of the two. On a set, the necklace and the matching earrings (and sometimes a ring or tikka) are weighed together and billed under one making charge.

Because the set is heavy, small differences in the rate matter. A set of 60 grams moves in price by sixty times any change in the per-gram rate, which is why bridal buyers watch the board for weeks. Our price history chart shows how the rate has moved so you can decide whether waiting makes sense, and the calculator prices any weight at today's figure.

What should not be on the invoice: a single lump sum with no weight. If a shop refuses to break the price into metal and making, walk to the next counter.

22K necklace sets with price: how the weight is built up

A "22K gold necklace set" in the Indian and Pakistani shops of Bur Dubai, Karama and Deira usually means a necklace plus matching earrings, sometimes with a maang tikka or a ring. The table below shows the weight bands you will meet, so you can estimate the metal value from the live rate before you shop.

Two things inflate a set's price beyond the metal. First, hand-finished work such as temple motifs, filigree or meenakari enamel can carry a making charge several times that of a plain chain. Second, stones: kundan, polki and coloured glass add weight that is not gold. The 22K rate page gives today's per-gram figure for the metal part.

Gold necklace designs sold in the UAE

The emirates sell necklaces to buyers from every gold-loving culture, and each lane of the souk has its own accent.

Gulf and Arab designs

21K is the rule. Expect wide, flat collars with hanging coins or tassels, Bahraini-style bib necklaces, and lighter everyday pendants with Quranic calligraphy or the name of the wearer. Sharjah's Rolla and the Deira arcades are the deepest sources.

Indian and Pakistani designs

22K dominates. South Indian temple jewellery, Kerala-style palakka and manga malai, North Indian kundan and polki chokers, Pakistani rani haar and jadau sets all appear in Meena Bazaar and Karama, with stock peaking before Diwali and the winter wedding season.

Western and branded

18K yellow, white and rose gold pendants, tennis-style necklaces and diamond-set pieces live in the mall jewellers and the Gold and Diamond Park. These carry fixed prices and higher making charges but are easier to return.

Across all three, a gold choker necklace is the fastest-growing category: it suits both saris and abayas, photographs well, and sits at a weight most families can afford for an engagement.

Gold pendants and pendant sets

A gold pendant is the entry point for many buyers: a small 18K or 21K piece of 1 to 5 grams on a fine chain, or a heavier 22K pendant of 5 to 15 grams carrying a religious symbol, a zodiac sign, an initial or a locket. Pendants are priced by weight exactly like chains, with a per-piece making charge if there is stone or enamel work.

A "pendant set" in UAE shop language is a pendant with matching earrings, usually sold without the chain so you can choose the chain weight and length separately. That is a sensible way to buy: pair a 22K pendant set with a sturdier 21K or 22K chain for daily wear, or with an 18K chain if you want strength on a thin link. Our chain guide covers which chain holds which pendant.

Watch for hollow pendants: domed or puffed designs are often hollow to keep weight down. That is fine for a light look but lowers resale value relative to appearance, so confirm on the invoice. For pendants with a single stone, ask whether the gold weight is quoted net of the stone; good shops weigh the mount separately.

Buying a bridal gold set in the UAE: a step-by-step

Wedding gold is the largest single jewellery purchase most families make. The process below is the one experienced buyers in Dubai follow.

  1. Fix a gram budget, not a dirham budget. The rate moves daily; deciding "about 70 grams across the set" keeps you anchored whatever the board says on the day.
  2. Decide the karat with the destination in mind. For a wedding in India or Pakistan, 22K matches what the family expects and resells best there. For a Gulf wedding, 21K is the norm.
  3. Shortlist designs on a quiet weekday. Visit two or three shops, photograph pieces with the shop's permission, note weight and making charge per gram for each.
  4. Confirm stone weights. Ask for the net gold weight on any kundan, polki or stone-set piece.
  5. Negotiate the making charge, not the rate. On a heavy set the per-gram making charge is where a meaningful saving sits.
  6. Check customs before you commit. If the set is travelling, read our Dubai to India customs guide, since duty on a heavy set can erase the saving.
  7. Pay on a day you are happy with the rate, and insist on a VAT invoice listing each piece's weight, karat and making charge.
Ask whether the shop will hold a design for a deposit at an agreed making charge while you watch the rate for a few days. Many souk shops will, and it separates the design decision from the timing decision.

Where to buy gold necklaces and sets

For 22K bridal sets, Bur Dubai's Meena Bazaar lanes and the shops around Karama are the natural first stop; the ranges are deep, the staff know Indian and Pakistani regional styles, and several shops will make a set to order from a photograph. The Deira Gold Souk has the widest Gulf 21K selection and plenty of 22K too, with the keenest making charges when you compare across counters. Branded 18K and diamond necklaces are strongest in the mall stores and the Gold and Diamond Park.

Abu Dhabi families shop the Madinat Zayed Gold Centre, where sets are priced on the same rate as Dubai, and the big chains' branches on Hamdan Street. Sharjah's Central Souk and Rolla are good for 21K Arab collars; Ajman and Al Ain souks are smaller but worth a look for plainer sets.

The large UAE jewellers also sell sets online with delivery, which is practical for residents who know the exact design they want. Compare the big names on our jewellers and brands page.

Making charges, offers and the resale reality

On a necklace set the making charge is the lever. Plain machine-made necklaces sit at the low end; hand-set temple and kundan work at the high end, sometimes approaching a large share of the metal value. Seasonal "zero making charge" and "flat making charge" offers around the Dubai Shopping Festival, Eid and Diwali are real and worth waiting for if your dates are flexible, but they usually apply to selected designs and the metal is still billed at the full rate.

Resale is where the design premium disappears. A shop buying back a set weighs the gold, tests the karat, deducts stone weight and pays metal value minus a small cut. The making charge, however beautiful the work, is not returned. That is why many families split their wedding gold: a heavy plain 22K piece that holds value, and a lighter worked piece for the ceremony. Read our page on making charges in Dubai for what is reasonable, and selling gold in Dubai for the buy-back process.

Looking after a gold necklace

Heavy 22K sets are soft. Store each piece flat in its own cloth pouch or the shop's box, never tangled with chains, and keep kundan and enamel work away from water, perfume and the humidity of a bathroom cupboard. Clean plain gold with warm soapy water and a soft brush; leave stone-set and enamel pieces to the jeweller, who will usually clean them free of charge if you bought from them.

Chokers and collars with hinged links should be checked once a year for worn pins. Pendant bails and clasps on daily-wear necklaces wear fastest; a souk goldsmith can replace a clasp while you wait. If a set will sit in a bank locker between weddings, keep the invoice with it. The invoice, with weights and karats listed per piece, is also the document you need for a gold loan or an insurance claim.

Questions people ask

How much does a 22K gold necklace set cost in Dubai?

Take the set's total gold weight, multiply by today's 22K per-gram rate on the board, add the shop's making charge and 5% VAT. Weights range from under 20 grams for a light set to well over 100 grams for heavy bridal work. The live strip on this page shows the current rate; the making charge is what differs between shops.

Is it cheaper to buy a gold set in Dubai than in India?

For the same weight and karat, usually yes, because the UAE charges only 5% VAT (refundable for tourists), has no gold import duty and intense competition keeps making charges down. The saving shrinks if India's customs duty applies on arrival, so check the allowance for your situation before buying a heavy set to carry home.

What is the difference between a choker and a necklace?

A choker sits high and close around the neck, usually 30 to 40 cm, and is broad rather than long. A necklace falls lower, to the collarbone or chest, and a long haar can reach the waist. Chokers weigh less for the same visual impact, which is why they are popular engagement and reception pieces in the UAE.

Does the stone weight count as gold in a necklace price?

It should not. A reputable UAE shop weighs the gold net of stones or states the stone weight separately on the invoice, and bills gold rate only on the gold. If a kundan or polki piece is quoted on gross weight at the gold rate, you are paying gold price for glass and stone, so ask for the breakdown.

Can I order a custom gold necklace in Dubai?

Yes. Many shops in Deira, Meena Bazaar and the Gold and Diamond Park make to order from a sketch or photograph, usually within one to three weeks depending on complexity. You agree the weight range, karat and making charge up front and pay a deposit; the metal is billed at the rate on the day you collect or on the day you lock it.
