White Gold, Rose Gold, Black Gold and Champagne Gold: Colours, Prices and Karats in Dubai

White gold, rose gold, black gold and the newer champagne gold are the same metal as the yellow bangles in the Deira souk, just mixed with different partners. The karat still decides how much gold is in the piece and therefore its metal price; the alloy decides what colour you see. That single idea answers most of the questions people bring to a counter in Dubai Mall or the Gold & Diamond Park: whether white gold is "real", whether rose gold is cheaper, whether black gold is a precious metal at all.

This page walks through how each colour is made, how the white gold price in Dubai compares with yellow, which colour suits which use, and what each one is worth when you come to sell.

Quick answer

  • Colour comes from the alloy mixed into the gold, not from the karat; 18K white and 18K yellow contain the same gold.
  • White gold is usually rhodium-plated for brightness and needs re-plating every few years.
  • Rose gold gets its colour from extra copper and is the most durable of the three.
  • Black gold is almost always a surface treatment over ordinary white or yellow gold.
  • The gram rate for any colour is the standard karat rate; the difference sits in making charges.

Gold colour is a recipe, not a karat

Pure gold is a warm, almost orange yellow. Every other colour is produced by the metals mixed in to reach a given karat. An 18K piece is always 75% gold; the remaining 25% is the colour recipe. Use silver and copper in balance and you get classic yellow. Swap in palladium, nickel or zinc and the mix turns pale. Lean heavily on copper and it blushes pink.

Because colour depends on alloy, it is rare above 18K. There is not enough non-gold metal in 21K or 22K to shift the colour convincingly, which is why the Arabic and Indian counters are yellow from end to end and the colours live in the diamond and designer shops. White gold: how it is made and why it is plated

White gold is yellow gold whitened with palladium, nickel or a silver-zinc blend. Even the best white alloys come out of the furnace a faint warm grey rather than true white, so almost every white gold piece sold in the UAE is electroplated with rhodium, a platinum-group metal that is brilliantly white and hard. That plating is what you see in the window.

Rhodium wears. On a ring worn daily the plating thins over two to four years and the warmer alloy beneath starts to show, usually first on the underside of the band. Re-plating is a routine service at any jeweller in Karama, Deira or the malls and is inexpensive. It is not a defect; it is maintenance, like polishing silver.

Nickel and sensitive skin

Nickel-based white gold can irritate sensitive skin. Palladium-based white gold costs a little more because palladium is dearer than nickel, but it is hypoallergenic and holds a better colour as the plating fades. If you react to cheap earrings, ask specifically for palladium white gold or consider platinum, which is naturally white and never needs plating.

For white gold rings in particular, the gold rings guide compares settings and widths.

White gold price in Dubai versus yellow gold

The metal in an 18K white gold ring is priced at exactly the 18K rate on the shop board; there is no separate white gold rate. Two things can still make the white version of a design cost a little more at the till.

  • Alloy cost. Palladium is far more expensive than copper or silver, so a palladium white gold piece carries a slightly higher making charge to cover the alloy.
  • Plating. Rhodium plating is an extra production step, and rhodium itself is a costly metal, so a few dirhams per piece go on that.

Set against the cost of the diamonds or the brand margin on a typical white gold piece, these differences are minor. What you should compare between a white and yellow version is the making charge line, not the gold line, which will be identical for the same weight. The making charges guide shows how to read that line, and the 18K rate page gives the live gram rate that both colours use.

Where white gold genuinely costs more is on resale, in reverse: a gold buyer pays the 750 metal value and nothing for the plating or the palladium, so the small premium you paid is not recovered.

Rose gold: copper, colour and durability

Rose gold is the simplest colour to make: increase the copper share of the alloy and reduce the silver. At 18K, roughly a fifth of the piece by weight is copper and the metal takes on a soft pink; push the copper further and jewellers call it red gold. 14K rose is pinker still because there is more alloy to colour it.

Copper is hard and cheap, which gives rose gold two practical advantages. It is the most scratch-resistant of the three colours at a given karat, and it carries no plating to wear off, so a rose gold bracelet looks the same at year ten as at year one. Some wearers find it deepens very slightly in tone with age as the copper reacts with air and skin, an effect most people like.

In Dubai, rose gold price per gram is the ordinary karat rate: an 18K rose gold piece is priced on the 18K line, and a 14K piece on the 14K line where a shop lists it. Making charges are usually in line with yellow gold, since the alloy is inexpensive and there is no plating step. Rose gold is popular for watches, stacking bands and modern Arabic-calligraphy pendants, and the Gold & Diamond Park workshops will make a design in rose on request if the showroom only displays it in white.

Black gold: what it is and what it is not

There is no black gold alloy sold in jewellery that stays black through and through. "Black gold" in the trade means one of three things, and it is worth asking which before you pay.

  1. Black rhodium or ruthenium plating over white or yellow gold. This is the common method. The plating is thin, scratches reveal the lighter metal beneath, and it needs renewing like white rhodium, only more visibly.
  2. Surface oxidation of a cobalt- or chromium-bearing gold alloy, which forms a dark skin. Rare, hard to repair, mostly seen in designer pieces.
  3. Laser or electron treatment that darkens the surface. Also uncommon and found in high-end work.

In every case the piece's metal value is its karat on the scale, usually 18K or 14K, and the black finish adds nothing on resale. It can also disappear into pawn and gold-buying tests, since an XRF reading looks through the plating to the alloy. Treat black gold as a fashion finish on real gold, enjoy it, and expect to have it re-blackened periodically.

Ask the shop to write the base metal and karat on the receipt for any plated colour: for example "18K white gold, rhodium plated" or "14K yellow gold, black rhodium". That line protects you if the finish wears and is what a buyer will price when you sell.

Champagne gold and other in-between shades

Champagne gold is a marketing name for a pale, warm yellow between white and classic yellow, achieved with a low-copper alloy and sometimes a light champagne-tinted plating. It flatters diamonds that are slightly warm in colour and has become a fashionable choice for bridal sets in the mall boutiques. Treat it like white gold: the karat sets the price, and if it is plated the shade will need refreshing.

You may also meet green gold (a silver-heavy alloy, rarely stocked), grey gold (palladium-rich, unplated, matte) and two-tone pieces that combine white and yellow or white and rose. Two-tone is the easiest to price: the whole piece is one karat, so the gold line is simply weight times rate; the making charge is a little higher because the parts are made separately and joined.

Whatever the shade, the three questions to ask are the same: what karat, is it plated, and what is the net gold weight. The hallmark and testing guide explains the stamps to look for and how a shop verifies an unmarked piece.

White gold versus yellow gold: choosing for real life

The choice is personal, but a few practical points help.

  • Skin tone and stones. White gold makes colourless diamonds look larger and icier; yellow and rose flatter warmer stones and most skin tones under Gulf sun.
  • Maintenance. Yellow and rose need polishing only. White needs re-plating every few years to keep its look. Black needs re-plating most often.
  • Durability. Rose is hardest, then white, then yellow at the same karat. All 18K pieces are fine for daily wear.
  • Allergies. Palladium white gold or platinum if nickel bothers you; yellow and rose rarely cause a reaction at 18K.
  • Tradition and gifting. For Arab, Indian and Pakistani family occasions, yellow 21K or 22K is still the expected gift. Coloured gold is for personal pieces, engagement rings and Western-style sets.
  • Resale. Yellow 21K and 22K resell fastest in the UAE; coloured 18K returns metal value only. See the selling guide for how buyers assess plated pieces.

A 6 gram 18K wedding band in white, rose or yellow carries the same 4.5 grams of fine gold and the same gold line on the receipt; everything else about the three is a matter of taste and upkeep.

Where to buy coloured gold in Dubai and the UAE

Because coloured gold lives at 18K and below, you will find the widest choice where diamonds and design dominate rather than where weight dominates.

  • Gold & Diamond Park, Sheikh Zayed Road: custom white and rose gold engagement rings, two-tone sets and quick re-plating by the manufacturers themselves. The park guide explains how custom orders work.
  • Dubai Mall, Mall of the Emirates and Yas Mall boutiques: branded white, rose and black gold collections, certified stones, fixed prices.
  • Deira's Italian chain specialists: fine rose and white gold chains and bracelets sold by weight at sensible making charges, a good route to coloured gold without paying a brand premium.
  • Bur Dubai and Sharjah independents: value white gold rings and small diamond pieces; ask whether the white alloy is palladium or nickel.

Tourists can reclaim VAT on any of these purchases at departure if the goods leave within 90 days, exactly as with yellow gold. And whichever colour you choose, run the weight through the gold calculator first so you know what the metal alone is worth before the conversation turns to design.

Questions people ask

Is white gold real gold?

Yes. White gold is ordinary gold alloyed with white metals such as palladium, nickel or silver and zinc, then usually plated with rhodium for a bright finish. An 18K white gold ring contains exactly as much gold as an 18K yellow one, 75% by weight, and is stamped 750 in the same way. Only the colour recipe and the plating differ.

Is rose gold cheaper than yellow gold in Dubai?

Not per gram. Rose gold is priced on the same karat line as yellow, so an 18K rose piece and an 18K yellow piece of equal weight have the same metal cost. Making charges are also similar because copper is cheap and there is no plating. Any price difference between two pieces comes from design, stones or brand, not from the colour.

Does black gold hold its value?

Black gold holds the value of the gold underneath it and nothing more. The black finish is plating or a surface treatment on 18K or 14K gold, and buyers pay for the karat and weight only. The finish also wears and needs renewing. Buy black gold because you like the look, not as a store of value, and keep the receipt that states the base karat.

How often does white gold need re-plating?

A ring worn every day typically needs new rhodium every two to four years; earrings and pendants that see less contact can go much longer. You will notice a warmer tint appearing on the parts that rub most. Re-plating is a quick, inexpensive service at almost any jeweller in the UAE, and some shops include the first re-plate free when you buy.

White gold or platinum for an engagement ring?

Platinum is naturally white, denser, hypoallergenic and never needs plating, but it costs more per piece and scratches into a soft patina. 18K white gold is lighter, cheaper and brighter when freshly plated, but needs rhodium every few years. In the UAE heat and daily wear both hold up well; many buyers choose white gold for budget and platinum for a ring they never want to maintain.
