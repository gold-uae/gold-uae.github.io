Gold Karats Explained: 24K, 22K, 21K and 18K (999, 916, 875, 750)

Every gold shop in the UAE prices its stock by karat, every receipt records it and every buyer who sells later is paid by it, yet most people could not say what the number means beyond "higher is purer". This page is the plain explanation: what 24 carat gold, 22K gold, 21K and 18 carat gold actually contain, what the 999, 916, 875 and 750 stamps are, how to convert between the two systems in your head, and how the karat decides the price per gram on the board.

Read it once and the difference between 22K and 24K gold, or the question of what 21K gold is for, will never confuse you at a counter again.

Quick answer

  • Karat measures purity in 24ths: 24K is pure gold, 18K is 18 parts gold and 6 parts alloy.
  • Fineness says the same thing per thousand: 999 is 24K, 916 is 22K, 875 is 21K, 750 is 18K.
  • Each karat's gram price is simply the 24K price multiplied by its purity.
  • Lower karats are harder and better for stones and daily wear; higher karats hold more metal value.
  • A karat is purity; a carat is a diamond's weight. Jewellers in Dubai use both words.

Karat versus carat: two words that sound the same

In gold, a karat (K or kt) is a unit of purity. Pure gold is 24 karat, so the karat number tells you how many parts out of 24 are gold and how many are something else. 22K is 22 parts gold and 2 parts alloy; 18K is 18 parts gold and 6 parts alloy. The word comes from the carob seed once used as a small weight in the markets of the Mediterranean and the Gulf.

In gemstones, a carat (ct) is a unit of weight, one fifth of a gram, used for diamonds and coloured stones. The two words share an origin but measure completely different things. A "1 carat 18K ring" is a ring made of 75% pure gold holding a stone weighing 0.2 grams.

British and Indian English spell both as "carat", which is why you will see "22 carat gold" on UAE shop signs and in the newspapers. American English keeps "karat" for gold. On this site the two spellings mean the same thing when applied to gold.

The karat scale and the fineness stamps

There are two ways to write purity, and UAE jewellers use both, often on the same piece. The karat system counts in 24ths. The fineness system counts parts per thousand, and it is what gets stamped inside rings and on clasps.

To convert, divide the karat by 24 and multiply by 1000: 22 divided by 24 is 0.9167, so 22K is 916 (rounded down, by convention). Going the other way, 750 divided by 1000 times 24 gives 18. A 999.9 stamp, sometimes called "four nines", is the refinery grade used on investment bars; 999 and 995 also count as 24K in everyday trade.

Marks and how to verify them are covered in the hallmark and purity test guide.

Why gold is alloyed at all

If pure gold is the most valuable, why mix it with anything? Because 999 gold is soft. A 24K ring will scratch, dent and slowly lose its shape with daily wear; a thin 24K chain can stretch under its own weight. Adding a small amount of copper, silver, zinc, palladium or nickel does three useful things.

  • Hardness. Even the 8% of alloy in 22K roughly doubles the metal's resistance to wear. The 25% in 18K lets it grip a diamond with four thin prongs for a lifetime.
  • Colour. Copper pulls the colour towards rose, silver and palladium towards white, and a mix keeps the classic yellow. The white, rose and black gold page goes into the recipes.
  • Workability. Fine filigree, sharp engraving and machine-made chain all need an alloy that holds a crisp edge.

The trade-off is simple: every point of karat you give up is metal value you are not paying for, in exchange for a tougher, more versatile piece. That is why bars are 999 and rings are 750.

The relationship is fixed arithmetic. The 24K rate comes from the international ounce price (divide by 31.1035 for a gram, multiply by the fixed 3.6725 peg for dirhams). Every other karat is that number times its purity:

  1. 22K rate = 24K rate × 0.916
  2. 21K rate = 24K rate × 0.875
  3. 18K rate = 24K rate × 0.750
  4. 14K rate = 24K rate × 0.585

Two consequences follow. First, the spreads never change: 22K is always about 8.4% below 24K, 18K always a quarter below. Second, a lower karat is never a bargain. A 10 gram 18K chain contains 7.5 grams of gold and is priced for 7.5 grams of gold; a 10 gram 22K chain contains 9.16 grams and costs correspondingly more. You pay for exactly the metal you get. The who sets the Dubai gold rate page explains where the published 24K figure comes from, and the calculator does the multiplication for any weight and karat.

Difference between 22K and 24K gold

This is the comparison most UAE buyers care about, because it is the choice between jewellery and bullion.

Purity and price

24K is 999 fine; 22K is 916. The 22K gram rate is 91.6% of the 24K rate. A 50 gram 24K bar holds about 49.95 grams of gold; a 50 gram 22K necklace holds 45.8 grams.

Form and tax

24K is sold mainly as bars and coins, which are zero-rated for VAT if they are investment grade, with low premiums. 22K is sold as jewellery, with making charges and 5% VAT on top. Some shops sell 24K bridal pieces, but they are soft and priced with heavy charges.

Wear

22K survives daily life; 24K marks easily. If a piece will be worn, 22K or lower is the practical choice.

Resale

Both resell at close to metal value in the UAE. 24K bars recover the most because there was little premium to lose; plain 22K pieces come next; ornate 22K loses its making charge. The 24K rate page and 22K rate page cover each side in detail.

What is 21K gold, and why the Gulf uses it

21K is 21 parts gold in 24, stamped 875, and it is the house purity of Arab jewellery. Visitors from India or Europe are often surprised to find it, because it barely exists in their home markets, yet in the UAE it fills the Arabic-design counters in Deira, most of the Central Souk in Sharjah and the gold centre in Abu Dhabi's Madinat Zayed.

The logic is a compromise between colour and strength. At 87.5% purity the metal still looks like deep yellow gold, but the extra alloy over 22K makes it hard enough for the hollow bangles, wide chokers and coin-set pieces that Gulf, Egyptian and Levantine designs favour, which are meant to be worn rather than stored. It is also priced about 4.5% below 22K per gram, which matters when a wedding set runs to 100 grams or more.

If your family is from the Arab world, 21K is what resale buyers at home expect; if from South Asia, 22K is. The 21K rate page and the karat hub help you decide if you sit between the two.

18 carat gold and the 750 stamp

18K gold, marked 750, is three-quarters gold and one-quarter alloy. It is the standard metal for diamond jewellery, designer and branded pieces, watches, and almost all white and rose gold, because the higher alloy share holds stones securely and takes colour well.

On an 18 carat piece the gold is often the smaller part of the price. A 2.5 gram 18K ring contains under 2 grams of pure gold; a stone on it can easily be worth more than the metal, and the labour to set it is charged per piece rather than per gram. For that reason 18K is the karat to buy for design and durability, not as a store of value. When sold, it returns 75% of the 24K rate on the net gold weight, with nothing for the labour and often little for small stones.

The 18K rate page explains how to read an 18 carat quote line by line, and the Gold & Diamond Park guide shows where most of Dubai's 750 work is made.

Which karat should you buy? A quick decision guide

Match the karat to the job, not to the idea that higher is always better.

  • You want to hold wealth: 24K bars or coins. Pure metal, zero-rated VAT on investment grade, lowest premium, easiest resale.
  • You are buying for an Indian, Pakistani or Sri Lankan wedding or to take home: 22K. Expected by families and by resale buyers in those countries.
  • You are buying Gulf or Arab designs, a shabka or everyday Arab pieces: 21K. More designs, slightly tougher, lower gram price.
  • You are buying diamonds, a designer brand, white or rose gold, or a piece for hard daily use: 18K.
  • You are buying imported Western fashion jewellery or a watch case: 14K may appear; it is fine for wear but weak for resale in the UAE.
Whatever the karat, ask the shop to write three lines on the receipt: net gold weight, karat (with fineness) and the rate applied. Those three numbers are the whole of your gold's resale value, and any shop that hesitates to put them in writing is telling you something.

Myths about karats that cost people money

A few persistent misunderstandings are worth clearing up.

  • "Dubai gold is purer." No. 22K is 916 everywhere. What Dubai offers is price transparency, competition and no import duty, not extra purity.
  • "18K is cheaper, so it is better value." It is cheaper per gram because it contains less gold. Per gram of gold, the price is identical across karats.
  • "Higher karat jewellery is always a better investment." A 24K piece with a high making charge can lose more on resale than a plain 22K bangle. Charges and form matter as much as purity.
  • "White gold is a different metal." It is the same gold at the same karat, alloyed white and usually rhodium-plated.
  • "The stamp is enough." Stamps can be faked. Reputable UAE shops test on an XRF machine on request; use the testing guide if a piece has no paperwork.

Understanding these saves far more than haggling a few fils off the rate ever will, and it makes conversations with a jeweller in Karama, Rolla or the Gold Souk a great deal shorter.

Questions people ask

What is the difference between 22K and 24K gold?

24K is essentially pure gold (999 fine) and 22K is 91.6% gold (916 fine) alloyed with copper and silver. 24K is softer, brighter yellow and sold mostly as bars and coins; 22K is tougher and is the standard for Indian-style jewellery. The 22K gram rate is 91.6% of the 24K rate, so the price difference is purity, not a discount.

What does 916 gold mean?

916 is the fineness stamp for 22 karat gold: 916 parts gold per thousand, or 91.6% pure. It is the mark you will find inside most Indian, Pakistani and Sri Lankan jewellery sold in the UAE. The remaining 84 parts per thousand are an alloy that gives the metal hardness and a slightly deeper yellow colour than pure gold.

What is 750 gold and is it real gold?

750 gold is 18 karat gold: 750 parts gold per thousand, or 75% pure, with the rest being silver, copper, palladium or zinc. It is entirely real gold, just alloyed for strength and colour. It is the standard for diamond settings, designer brands and most white and rose gold jewellery sold in Dubai and across the UAE.

Is 999 gold the same as 24K?

Yes. 999 fineness (99.9% pure) is what jewellers mean by 24K, and 999.9 is the refinery grade used on investment bars and coins. Some trade bars are 995, which also counts as 24K in practice. 24K is the reference price on every UAE shop board; all other karats are priced as a fixed fraction of it.

What is 21K gold used for?

21K (875 fine, 87.5% gold) is the traditional purity of Gulf, Egyptian and Levantine jewellery. It is used for the shabka and mahr sets, hollow bangles, chokers and coin pieces sold on the Arabic counters in Deira, Sharjah and Abu Dhabi. It is slightly harder and about 4.5% cheaper per gram than 22K, and resells easily throughout the Arab world.

Which karat is best for daily wear in the UAE heat?

Heat itself does not harm gold at any karat, but sweat, perfume and knocks do affect softer alloys. For rings and bracelets worn every day, 18K holds its shape and polish best, 21K is a good compromise with more gold content, and 22K needs more care in thin designs. Keep any karat away from chlorine and store pieces separately.
