24K Gold Rate in Dubai Today: 24 Carat Price per Gram in the UAE

The 24K figure at the top of every shop board in the UAE is the purest and simplest number in the gold trade: what one gram of 999 fine gold costs in dirhams right now. Everything else on the board is a fraction of it. This page shows the live 24 carat rate, explains why it tracks the international ounce price to the tick, and then gets practical: how bars and coins are priced over that rate, why investment gold escapes VAT while a 24K bangle does not, where in Dubai and Abu Dhabi people buy pure gold, and when a lower karat is honestly the better buy.

Quick answer

  • The 24K rate is the price of pure gold per gram in dirhams and is the base from which 22K, 21K and 18K are calculated.
  • The same 24K gold rate applies in Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Sharjah and every other emirate on the same day.
  • Investment-grade 24K bars and coins are zero-rated for VAT in the UAE; 24K jewellery still carries 5%.
  • You pay the rate plus a small premium for bars; the premium per gram falls as the bar gets heavier.
  • 24K is too soft for everyday jewellery, which is why most UAE pieces are 22K, 21K or 18K.

This is the rate for the metal itself, as published for the trade in Dubai and used from Deira to Ras Al Khaimah. The number comes from a short chain of arithmetic: the dollar price of a troy ounce on the world market, divided by 31.1035 to reach one gram, multiplied by the fixed 3.6725 dirham-to-dollar peg, with a thin margin for the local market. The spot price page shows that ounce figure if you want to check the maths.

Because the dirham does not float, a UAE resident watching the 24K rate is effectively watching the world gold price in slow, readable units. When the ounce rises one percent, the gram rate rises one percent, nothing more and nothing less.

What 999 purity actually means

24K means 24 parts gold out of 24, and the stamp 999 means 999 parts per thousand. In practice there are a few grades within "pure":

  • 999.9 (four nines): the standard for refinery bars and most modern coins sold in Dubai.
  • 999 (three nines): common on smaller bars, traditional Gulf jewellery and many Chinese-style pieces.
  • 995: the minimum fineness accepted for large wholesale bars in the international market; you will rarely meet it at retail.

The difference between these grades is tiny in value and is reflected in the bar's price. What matters more is that pure gold is soft. A 24K ring can be marked with a fingernail, loses sharp detail quickly and bends under daily wear. That softness is why jewellers alloy gold down to 22K, 21K or 18K, and why the karats explained page describes 24K as a material for storing value rather than for wearing.

There is also a colour difference: 24K is a deep, almost orange yellow that some Gulf and East Asian buyers specifically want in heritage pieces, which keeps a small market for 24K jewellery alive in Deira.

Bars, coins and the premium over the 24K rate

Nobody buys pure gold at exactly the board rate. A bar or coin costs the rate multiplied by the weight, plus a premium that covers refining, minting, packaging, the dealer's margin and, for branded bars, the brand itself.

VAT on 24K gold: when it is zero and when it is 5%

The UAE charges 5% VAT on gold jewellery, but gold that qualifies as investment-grade is zero-rated. To qualify, the item must be at least 99% pure and traded on the basis of its metal content, which describes bars and most bullion coins. So a 50 g 999 bar bought in Deira carries no VAT, while a 50 g 24K chain, even at the same purity, is jewellery and carries 5% on the whole invoice including making charges.

Three practical consequences follow:

  1. For pure saving, bars beat 24K jewellery before you even consider making charges.
  2. Tourists cannot reclaim VAT on bars because there is none to reclaim; the refund scheme matters for jewellery.
  3. Between businesses, gold trade uses a reverse-charge mechanism, which is why wholesale invoices look different from retail ones.

The full picture, including the tourist refund process at the airport, is on the VAT and tax refund page.

Where people buy 24K gold in the UAE

Pure gold is sold in more places than jewellery, and the right one depends on how much you are buying.

Deira and the Gold Souk Extension

The bar dealers clustered around the Gold Souk and inside the covered extension sell everything from 1 g to 1 kg, usually with tight premiums and no-nonsense buy-back. Metro to Gold Souq station and walk.

Jewellery chains

Large retailers in Bur Dubai, Karama, Madinat Zayed in Abu Dhabi and the malls stock branded bars and coins alongside jewellery. Premiums are a little higher, but the packaging, certificate and easy exchange suit gift buyers.

Refiners and bullion houses

For kilo bars and regular large purchases, the refineries and bullion dealers in Dubai sell closer to the wholesale price. This is the route for serious savers and for anyone who later wants the simplest resale.

Whatever you buy, keep the bar in its sealed assay card. A 24K bar with an intact seal and certificate sells back in minutes at near the board rate; a loose bar must be tested first and may be bought at a discount.

For a tour of the physical market, start with the Dubai Gold Souk guide.

24K versus 22K and 21K: which gives better value?

The question comes up at every counter, and the honest answer depends on whether you will wear the gold or store it.

  • Per gram of actual gold, all karats cost the same; a 22K gram simply contains 0.916 g of gold priced at the 24K rate. Nobody gets a discount on the metal by choosing a lower karat.
  • On resale, a 24K bar recovers almost everything because there is no making charge to lose and the buyer barely needs to test it. A 22K or 21K bangle recovers the metal value minus a spread, and the craftsmanship is gone.
  • For wearing, 24K is impractical. A bride in Sharjah or Al Ain wears 21K or 22K because it holds its shape; 24K would dent at the first wedding.
  • For gifting, a small 24K coin or bar is often the better present: no VAT, no making charge and no question of taste.

The 22K rate page and the 21K rate page cover the jewellery side; this page's advice is simply to keep the two purposes separate in your head and in your budget.

Checking that your 24K gold is really 24K

Pure gold is the easiest grade to verify and the one most often faked in the crudest ways, so a few checks are worth the minute they take.

  • Read the stamp and card. A legitimate bar states the weight, the fineness (999 or 999.9), the refiner and a serial number that matches the certificate.
  • Weigh it. Shops weigh on calibrated scales in front of you; a 10 g bar reading noticeably light is a red flag.
  • Ask for an electronic test. Every serious dealer in Deira has an XRF analyser and will show the reading. Pure gold should read in the high 990s per thousand.
  • Be wary of colour alone. Plated tungsten or brass can look identical; weight and density tell the truth.

The hallmark and purity test page goes through each method, including simple home checks. For a quick value estimate of any 24K piece by weight, use the gold calculator with the karat set to 24.

Questions people ask

What is the 24K gold rate in Dubai today?

The live 24K rate in dirhams per gram is shown in the card near the top of this page. It is the rate published for the Dubai market and used across the UAE, and it moves with the international ounce price during trading hours. The same rate applies in Abu Dhabi, Sharjah, Ajman and the other emirates.

Is 24K gold the same as 999 gold?

Yes. 24K means 24 parts gold out of 24, and 999 means 999 parts per thousand, which is the practical standard for pure gold. Many refinery bars are stamped 999.9, a slightly higher fineness that commands a tiny extra premium. All of these are sold at the 24K rate plus the dealer's premium.

Do I pay VAT on 24K gold in the UAE?

Not on investment bars and coins of 99% purity or higher that are traded for their metal value; those are zero-rated. You do pay 5% VAT on 24K jewellery such as chains or bangles, because they are sold as jewellery with making charges. Tourists can reclaim the VAT on jewellery at departure but have none to reclaim on bars.

Why is 24K jewellery rare in Dubai?

Pure gold is too soft to hold fine detail or survive daily wear, so jewellers alloy it down to 22K for Indian designs, 21K for Gulf and Arab designs and 18K for stone-set pieces. The 24K jewellery you do see is usually chunky, traditional and sold to buyers who value the deep colour and easy resale over durability.

Is it better to buy 24K bars or 22K jewellery as an investment?

For investment alone, bars usually win: no VAT, a lower premium and almost full recovery on resale. Jewellery carries making charges and VAT that you will not get back. Many families still buy 22K or 21K because it doubles as something to wear and to gift, which is a reasonable choice as long as the resale difference is understood.
