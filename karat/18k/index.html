The 18K gold rate today sits at the bottom of most UAE shop boards, and for good reason: 18 carat is the working metal of the diamond trade, the fashion houses and the Italian chain makers, not of the bullion counter. If you are looking at a solitaire in the Gold & Diamond Park, a tennis bracelet in Dubai Mall or a slim rose gold chain in a Jumeirah boutique, the metal in it is almost certainly stamped 750.

Below is the live 18K gold price in Dubai per gram in dirhams. This page then explains what the rate does and does not tell you about an 18K piece, why the same gram price can produce wildly different shop prices, and how to buy and sell 18 carat gold in the UAE without being surprised.

  • 18K gold is 75% pure (750 fineness) and its gram rate is 75% of the 24K rate.
  • It is the karat of diamond jewellery, designer brands, Italian chains and most white and rose gold.
  • On 18K pieces the design and stones usually cost more than the gold itself.

What 18 carat gold is for

18K gold is 18 parts gold and 6 parts other metals in 24, which is 750 parts per thousand. That 25% of alloy does three jobs the higher karats cannot. It makes the metal hard enough to hold a diamond in four thin prongs for decades. It takes a crisp polish and a sharp edge, which is why engraved and machine-finished designs look better in 18K. And it accepts colour: swap copper for palladium or nickel and you get white gold, lean on copper and you get rose gold, both of which are nearly always 18K or 14K rather than 21K or 22K.

In the UAE this makes 18K the karat of a specific set of counters: the diamond showrooms in the Gold & Diamond Park on Sheikh Zayed Road, the international brands in the malls, the Italian and Turkish chain specialists in Deira, and the watch and pen makers. Walk the Gold Souk looking for a plain 18K bangle and you will find few; walk it looking for a diamond ring and almost every one will be 750.

The white, rose and black gold page explains how the alloys change colour without changing the karat.

Notice how far below the 24K line it sits: exactly a quarter lower, because a quarter of the alloy is not gold. The 18K rate is arrived at the same way as every other karat. The ounce price in dollars is divided by 31.1035 for a gram, multiplied by the fixed 3.6725 dirham peg for the 24K gram rate, then multiplied by 0.75. The spot price page shows the dollar figure the chain starts from.

The practical point: because the rate is derived, the 18K line on every board in the country moves together with the others. There is no shop or emirate where 18 carat gold is "cheap"; there are only shops where the charges on top of the metal are lower.

Why the gram rate explains so little of an 18K price

On a 22K bangle the metal might be nine-tenths of the receipt. On an 18K diamond ring it can be less than a third. Three things sit between the 18K rate and the number on the tag.

  • Stones. Diamonds, sapphires and even good cubic zirconia are priced separately and often dwarf the gold. A 3 gram 18K ring holds a little over 2 grams of pure gold; the solitaire on top can be worth many times that.
  • Making charges. 18K work is fine, slow and often done by hand or on precision machines, so the charge per gram is far higher than on a plain 21K bangle. Brands add their margin on top. The making charges guide shows how to separate the two.
  • VAT at 5% on the whole thing: metal, stones and labour.

This is why two 18K rings of the same weight in the same mall can differ in price by a factor of ten. When you compare 18 carat quotes, compare the gold line, the stone line and the labour line separately, and ignore the "per gram" figure a brand sometimes prints, which usually bundles labour into it.

Ask for the net gold weight with stones excluded and the 18K rate applied to it, written on the receipt. That one line is what any buyer will pay you for later, and seeing it next to the full price tells you instantly how much of the money is going into design and stones.

Against 21K and 22K, 18K wins on durability and finish and loses on gold content and resale simplicity. Against 14K, 18K has a richer colour and more metal value, which is why UAE jewellers stock far more 18K than 14K even though both are legal and hallmarked. Readers coming from the United States or Europe, where 14K and even 9K are common, often find that the UAE market treats 18K as the minimum for serious jewellery.

Where to buy 18 carat gold in Dubai and the UAE

Because 18K is the diamond karat, the best places to buy it are not always the famous gold streets.

  1. Gold & Diamond Park, Sheikh Zayed Road. Air-conditioned, manufacturers on site, and the largest concentration of diamond and 18K specialists in the city. Good for custom engagement rings made to your stone and budget. The Gold & Diamond Park guide covers timings and how custom orders work.
  2. Mall boutiques in Dubai Mall, Mall of the Emirates and Abu Dhabi's big centres. International brands, certified diamonds, fixed prices, generous exchange policies, and the highest charges per gram.
  3. Deira's Italian and Turkish chain shops. Lightweight 18K chains, bracelets and hollow designs sold by weight with reasonable making charges; a good place for everyday 18K without stones.
  4. Independent jewellers in Bur Dubai, Karama and Sharjah. Often the best value for simple 18K rings and small diamond pieces if you are prepared to ask about stone certificates.

Wherever you buy, the 18K rate will be identical. Use the gold calculator to see what the metal alone should cost for a given weight, then judge whether the rest of the price is fair for the stones and work you are getting.

Selling 18K gold: what to expect

Selling 18 carat jewellery in the UAE is straightforward but rarely rewarding, and it helps to know why before you buy. A gold buyer in Deira or Sharjah will test the piece, remove or ignore the stones, weigh the net gold, apply the 750 fineness to the day's rate and deduct a melting margin. Everything you paid for design, brand and labour is gone, and most buyers will offer little or nothing for small stones because they cannot resell them easily.

Diamonds above a certain size with a laboratory certificate are a separate conversation and are better sold through a diamond dealer or back to the brand than at a gold counter. Branded pieces from well-known houses sometimes fetch a premium on the second-hand market, but that is a different trade from gold buying.

For plain 18K chains and bracelets without stones, resale is quick and the recovery is simply the metal value, roughly three-quarters of what the same weight of 24K would fetch. The selling guide explains how to get competing quotes, and the gold loan page covers using 18K jewellery as collateral instead of selling it.

Caring for 18K and reading the 750 stamp

Every piece of 18K sold by a reputable UAE jeweller carries a 750 or 18K mark, usually inside a ring band, on a clasp tag or on the back of a pendant. White gold is often rhodium-plated for brightness and may carry an additional plating note; the plating wears over a few years and is re-applied cheaply by any jeweller.

Day to day, 18K is the easiest karat to live with. It resists scratches better than 21K or 22K, holds its shape in thin designs and tolerates knocks. Keep it away from chlorine and harsh cleaners, store pieces separately, and have prongs checked yearly if the piece holds stones. Rose gold can deepen slightly in colour with age as the copper reacts; most owners like the effect.

If you want to confirm a stamp or test an unmarked piece, the hallmark and purity test guide explains XRF testing at shops and the simple checks you can do yourself. For first-time buyers, the how to buy gold in Dubai guide puts the whole process in order.

Questions people ask

What does 750 mean on gold jewellery?

750 is the fineness mark for 18K gold: 750 parts gold per thousand, or 75% pure. The other 25% is an alloy of silver, copper, palladium or zinc chosen to give the piece its colour and strength. A 750 stamp is the standard hallmark on diamond jewellery, designer pieces and most white and rose gold sold in the UAE.

Is 18K gold worth buying in Dubai?

For jewellery you will wear, yes: 18K is durable, holds stones securely and comes in colours the higher karats cannot offer. As a way of storing value it is weak, because labour and stones form most of the price and resale returns only the metal at 75% fineness. Many residents buy 18K for diamonds and design, and 21K, 22K or bars when the goal is savings.

Why is 18K gold cheaper per gram than 22K?

Because a gram of 18K contains only 0.75 grams of gold against 0.916 grams in 22K. The rate per gram of actual gold is identical; the board simply reflects how much metal is in the alloy. That is also why an 18K piece can still cost more than a 22K piece of the same weight once stones and fine workmanship are added.

Does 18K white gold cost more than 18K yellow gold?

The gold rate is the same, since both are 750 fine. White gold sometimes costs a little more at the counter because palladium in the alloy is pricier than copper, and because the rhodium plating adds a step. The difference is in making charges, not in the metal rate, and it is usually small compared with the cost of any stones in the piece.

Can I get the VAT back on 18K jewellery bought in Dubai?

Tourists can. All jewellery, including 18K diamond pieces, carries 5% VAT, and visitors reclaim most of it through the tax refund scheme at the airport provided the goods leave the UAE within 90 days and the purchase meets the minimum. Ask the shop for a tax-free tag at the time of sale; it cannot be issued afterwards. Residents cannot reclaim VAT.
