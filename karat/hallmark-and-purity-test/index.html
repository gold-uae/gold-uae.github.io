Gold Hallmark in the UAE: How to Check Gold Purity and Spot Fake Gold

Every licensed jeweller in the UAE must stamp or certify the fineness of the gold it sells, so the first place to look is inside the band, on the clasp or on the back of the pendant. A 916 mark means 22-karat gold, 875 means 21-karat and 750 means 18-karat. The stamp is reassuring, but a stamp can be forged, so a careful buyer pairs it with a proper invoice and, for larger purchases, an XRF test at an assay office.

This guide explains the hallmarks you will meet in Deira, Sharjah and Abu Dhabi, which home checks actually mean something, and where to get gold tested professionally before you buy or sell.

Quick answer

  • A UAE hallmark is a tiny fineness stamp: 999 is 24K, 916 is 22K, 875 is 21K, 750 is 18K.
  • Home tests (magnet, ceramic streak, vinegar) only rule out obvious fakes; they cannot confirm purity.
  • Gold testing in Dubai is done with XRF machines at assay offices and many souk shops, usually in minutes.
  • Always ask for an itemised invoice showing karat, weight and making charge; it is your proof of purity.
  • Fake gold in Dubai is rare in licensed shops; most problems come from street sellers and unverified online deals.

What a UAE gold hallmark actually tells you

A hallmark is a fineness mark expressed in parts per thousand. The number tells you how much of the piece is pure gold by weight; the rest is alloy such as copper, silver, zinc or palladium. Because karat is simply a different scale for the same thing, the two always line up as in the table below.

999 / 999.924K99.9%+Bars, coins, some bridal sets
91622K91.6%Indian-style jewellery, bangles, chains
87521K87.5%Gulf and Levantine designs, everyday pieces
75018K75%Diamond settings, watches, Italian designs
58514K58.5%Rare locally; imported fashion jewellery

Beside the fineness you will often see a maker's mark (a small logo or letters identifying the factory or brand) and sometimes a country or assay symbol. Pieces sold in the UAE should carry the fineness stamp as a minimum; the rest varies with where the item was made.

If you want the background on why these percentages matter for price and durability, the karat and purity explainer covers it in detail, and the karat hub helps you pick between them.

916 hallmark meaning and why it dominates the souk

Walk through the Gold Souk in Deira and the stamp you will see most is 916. It is the fineness of 22K, the standard for South Asian jewellery, and because a large share of UAE buyers send or carry gold to India, Pakistan and Sri Lanka, shops stock it heavily. In India the same 916 mark is what the BIS hallmark scheme certifies, which is why Indian buyers in Dubai look for it by reflex.

The 875 stamp is the Gulf equivalent. Emirati, Saudi, Egyptian and Lebanese families traditionally buy 21K, which is a little harder than 22K and sits at a slightly lower per-gram metal cost. The 21K page explains the trade-offs and shows the live rate.

Reading a worn or partial stamp

Hallmarks on old bangles wear down. If you can only make out two digits, the remaining shape usually gives it away: 91x is 22K, 87x is 21K, 75x is 18K. A stamp that reads 22K rather than 916 is not wrong, just a different convention; many pieces carry both. What should make you pause is a stamp that looks freshly punched into an old piece, or a number such as 925, which is sterling silver, on a yellow item that is being sold as gold.

Gold purity test at home: what works and what does not

Home checks are useful for one thing: catching obvious fakes before you waste a trip to an assay office. None of them can tell 21K from 22K, and none should be the basis for paying real money. In rough order of usefulness:

  • Magnet test. Gold is not magnetic. If a strong magnet grabs the piece, it is plated steel or a base alloy. If it does not, you have learned very little, because brass and copper are also non-magnetic.
  • Weight and feel. Gold is dense. A 22K bangle feels noticeably heavier than a brass copy of the same size. With a kitchen scale and a measuring cup you can estimate density, but the method is clumsy on hollow or stone-set jewellery.
  • Ceramic streak. Drag the piece lightly across an unglazed ceramic tile. A gold streak is gold-coloured; a black or grey streak means plating over base metal. This scratches the item, so use a hidden edge.
  • Skin and discolouration. Green or black marks on skin after wearing usually point to plating or a very low-karat alloy, though some people react to the copper in 18K too.
  • Vinegar or nitric acid. Pure gold does not react. Acid kits sold online can approximate karat, but they damage the piece and are easy to misread; leave them to the trade.

If a piece passes all of these, it may still be 14K sold as 22K, or a thick gold shell over tungsten. That is where professional testing comes in.

Gold testing in Dubai: XRF, fire assay and what they cost

Professional gold testing in Dubai is quick and inexpensive, and most reputable shops do not mind if you ask for it. The common methods are:

  1. XRF (X-ray fluorescence). The piece is placed in a desktop machine for under a minute and the screen shows the metal composition. It is non-destructive and accurate to a fraction of a percent on the surface. Many souk shops, bullion dealers and all assay offices have one. Its weakness is depth: a thick plating or a gold shell over a different core can fool it, which is why bars and heavy pieces are sometimes also checked by weight and dimensions.
  2. Electronic conductivity testers. Handheld pens that read the electrical response of the metal. Fast, cheap and fine for a sanity check, but less precise than XRF.
  3. Fire assay. The reference method for bullion. A sample is melted and separated chemically. Destructive and slow, so it is used for refining and for disputes, not for a bangle.

For a small fee an assay office or testing lab will issue a certificate with the fineness and weight. If you are about to sell gold, this certificate also stops a buyer from quoting you a lower karat than you own. If you are buying a bar, see the bar guide for how sealed assay cards work.

Ask the shop to run its own XRF in front of you before you pay for any piece over a few grams. A licensed jeweller in Deira or at the Gold and Diamond Park will usually do it without fuss; a refusal tells you something.

Fake gold in Dubai: where the real risk is

The UAE gold trade is tightly regulated and licensed shops have strong incentives to protect their reputation, so outright fake gold in Dubai is uncommon inside the souks and malls. The cases that do surface follow a few patterns:

  • Street and car-park approaches. Someone near a metro station or hotel offers gold at a discount with a story about needing cash. Walk away. Legitimate gold is never sold below the published rate by a stranger.
  • Online classifieds and social media sellers. No shop, no licence, no invoice, often a demand for a transfer first. Treat every such offer as a scam until proven otherwise.
  • Karat substitution. A piece stamped 916 that assays at 18K or 20K. This is a problem of unlicensed back-room workshops, not of established retailers. An XRF test catches it instantly.
  • Heavy plating on silver or copper. Common in costume jewellery sold honestly as such; a problem only when someone represents it as solid gold.
  • Tungsten or lead-filled bars. A known trick in the international bullion world. Buying bars only from recognised refiners with tamper-evident packaging avoids it.

The practical rule: buy inside a licensed shop, get an itemised invoice, and keep the receipt. Our buying guide walks through the full checklist.

How to know if gold is real before you pay: a buyer's checklist

Combine the signals rather than relying on one. In a shop in Karama, Bur Dubai or Madinat Zayed, this takes five minutes:

  1. Find the fineness stamp with a loupe (shops keep them on the counter) and confirm it matches the karat on the price tag.
  2. Weigh the piece on the shop scale and watch the reading; the invoice must carry the same gram figure to one decimal.
  3. Check that the per-gram rate on the tag matches the board rate for that karat; the Dubai rate page shows what the board should say right now.
  4. Ask for the making charge as a separate line, so you can see metal value and labour separately.
  5. For anything above a few grams, ask for an XRF reading or buy from a shop that offers one.
  6. Keep the invoice; it is what a buyer will ask for when you resell, and what customs may ask for when you travel.

Stone-set pieces need one extra step: the invoice should state the net gold weight after deducting stones, not the gross weight with stones included. Paying gold price for a ruby is a classic mistake.

Hallmarks on bars, coins and imported jewellery

Bars and coins follow a different convention from jewellery. A refinery bar is stamped with the refiner's logo, the fineness (usually 999.9), the weight and a serial number, and small bars ship sealed in a card with an assay certificate printed on it. The seal matters as much as the stamp; a bar out of its card is normally bought back at a slightly lower price because it has to be re-tested.

Coins carry their purity and weight in the design itself, so a sovereign, a maple leaf or an Emirati commemorative coin is identified by its pattern rather than a separate stamp. The coin page lists the ones you will find locally.

Imported jewellery brings its own marks: Italian pieces are often 750 with a star-and-number maker code, Turkish pieces frequently 585 or 750 with a small crescent, Singapore and Hong Kong pieces usually 916 or 999 with a Chinese character. None of this changes the UAE rule: the shop selling it to you is responsible for the fineness on the invoice, whatever the origin.

Gold purity checker tools and when to trust them

Online "gold purity checker" apps and calculators cannot test metal; they convert between karat, fineness and price. They are still handy for working out what a 21K piece should be worth against the 24K rate, or what weight of 22K you get for a fixed budget. The Gold UAE calculator does exactly that with the live rate.

Where a figure matters, trust the hierarchy: a fire assay certificate from a refinery outranks an XRF printout, an XRF printout outranks a shop's hallmark, and a hallmark outranks a home test. Buy and sell at the level appropriate to the money involved. For a 2-gram pendant the stamp and invoice are enough; for a kilo bar you want the sealed refinery packaging and a recognised dealer.

Finally, remember that purity and price are separate questions. A genuine 18K piece is real gold, just less of it; whether it is good value depends on the making charge and the design, which is covered on the making charges page.

Questions people ask

What does the 916 hallmark mean on gold?

916 is the fineness of 22-karat gold: 916 parts per thousand, or 91.6%, are pure gold and the remaining 8.4% is alloy. It is the most common stamp in UAE souks because 22K is the standard for South Asian jewellery. A piece stamped 916 should be priced at the 22K board rate per gram plus making charges.

How can I check gold purity at home without damaging it?

The least damaging checks are the magnet test (real gold is not attracted), the weight test (gold is much denser than brass or plated steel) and simply looking for the fineness stamp with a magnifier. These only screen out crude fakes. They cannot distinguish 18K from 22K, so for anything valuable use an XRF test at a shop or assay office.

Where can I get gold tested in Dubai?

Most licensed jewellers in the Deira Gold Souk, the Gold and Diamond Park and the big mall chains have XRF machines and will test a piece on request, often free if you are buying or selling with them. Independent assay offices and testing labs in Deira and around the DMCC area issue formal certificates for a small fee, which is useful when selling or for insurance.

Is fake gold common in Dubai?

Not in licensed shops. The trade is regulated, shops carry licences and reputations, and invoices are standard, so substitution is rare and easy to prove. The fakes that do appear almost always come from street sellers, unverified social-media sellers or deals that skip the invoice. Staying inside licensed shops and keeping receipts removes nearly all of the risk.

Does gold bought in Dubai carry a hallmark recognised in India?

Dubai jewellery is stamped with the same fineness numbers (916, 750 and so on) but not with the Indian BIS hallmark, which is applied by Indian assay centres. The gold is equally real, and Indian jewellers will test and accept it, but if you later want a BIS stamp you can have the piece hallmarked at an Indian centre. Keep the Dubai invoice for customs and resale.

What is the difference between a hallmark and a certificate?

A hallmark is a physical stamp on the piece showing its fineness and often its maker. A certificate is a document, usually from an XRF or fire assay, stating purity and weight for that specific item. Jewellery normally carries only a hallmark and an invoice; bars and coins come with a certificate or a sealed assay card. For a valuable piece, having both is ideal.
