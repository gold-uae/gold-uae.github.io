Gold Karat Guide for the UAE: Purity, Rates and Which Karat to Buy in Dubai

Ask three shopkeepers in Deira which karat you should buy and you will get three confident, different answers, because the right karat depends on what the gold is for. This hub is the map of the karat section. It explains in plain terms what the numbers 999, 916, 875 and 750 mean, shows how purity turns into a per-gram rate, and then routes you to the right page: the live rate for each karat, the deeper purity explainer, the guide to white, rose and black gold, and the page on hallmarks and testing. Start with the decision path below if you simply want to know what to buy.

Quick answer

  • Karat measures purity out of 24: 24K is 999 fine, 22K is 916, 21K is 875 and 18K is 750.
  • The per-gram rate of each karat is the 24K rate multiplied by its purity share, so 21K is 87.5% of 24K.
  • Buy 24K for bars and coins, 22K for Indian-style jewellery, 21K for Gulf designs and 18K for stone-set pieces.
  • Every karat sold in the UAE should carry a purity stamp, and reputable shops will test a piece in front of you.
  • Lower karat does not mean worse gold; it means a harder alloy that suits daily wear and fine settings.

The four karats sold in the UAE at a glance

Gold karat is a fraction out of twenty-four. Pure gold is 24 parts out of 24; 18K is 18 parts gold and 6 parts other metals such as copper, silver, zinc or palladium. The UAE market concentrates on four grades, each with a three-digit fineness stamp you will find inside rings and on bangle clasps.

Because the same board rate applies across all seven emirates, the difference between a 22K bangle in Sharjah and one in Abu Dhabi is only the making charge, never the purity. The purity scale is the same whether you shop at the Gold Souk or a mall showroom.

Which karat should you buy? A short decision path

Match the karat to the job and most of the choice makes itself.

  • You want to store value and sell later. Go to 24K bars or coins. They are zero-rated for VAT, carry the smallest premium over the metal price and are easiest to sell back. Read the 24K gold rate page first.
  • You are buying for an Indian or South Asian wedding. 22K is the standard the family will expect and the easiest to resell or exchange in Kerala, Chennai or Karachi. Start with the 22K gold rate page.
  • You want Gulf or Arabic designs for everyday wear. 21K is the heart of the Emirati and wider Arab market: rich colour, slightly harder than 22K and a wide choice in Deira and Madinat Zayed. See the 21K gold rate page.
  • You are buying a diamond ring, a watch or a designer piece. 18K is the right alloy for secure stone settings and for white or rose shades. The 18K gold rate page explains why its price per gram is lower.
  • You are not sure what the numbers mean. Read the karats explained page before spending anything.

Colour is not purity: white, rose and black gold

A surprising number of shoppers assume white gold is a different metal or that rose gold is a lower grade. Neither is true. Colour comes from the alloy mixed into the gold, while karat tells you how much gold is in the mix. An 18K white ring and an 18K yellow ring contain exactly the same amount of gold.

The white, rose and black gold page explains how each shade is made, why white gold is usually rhodium-plated and needs re-plating after a few years, how rose gold gets its tint from copper, and what "black gold" really is in the trade. It also covers the pricing question: coloured gold is almost always 18K or 14K, so you are paying a higher share of making charges and a lower share of metal than you would for a 22K yellow piece of the same weight.

If you are weighing a white gold wedding band against a yellow 21K one, that page and the rings guide together give you the full picture.

How karat changes the bill and the buy-back

Purity affects two lines on a receipt. The metal line is simple: multiply the day's 24K rate by the karat share and by the weight. The making charge line is where karat has a less obvious effect. Softer, higher-karat gold is easier to work into heavy traditional pieces, so 22K and 21K making charges are often quoted per gram, while 18K pieces with stones are priced per piece or as a high percentage.

  1. Weigh the piece and note the karat stamp.
  2. Multiply weight by the karat's per-gram rate for the metal value.
  3. Add the making charge; ask whether it is per gram or a percentage.
  4. Add 5% VAT on the whole amount for jewellery.

On resale the order reverses. A shop pays for the metal content at the day's rate for that karat, minus a small spread, and pays nothing for the craftsmanship. That is why 24K bars and plain 22K or 21K bangles keep the highest share of their price, and why an elaborate 18K piece loses the most. Where each karat lives in the UAE

Karat preference maps neatly onto the geography of the gold trade, which is useful when you want the widest choice.

  • Deira Gold Souk and the Gold Souk Extension: everything, but the deepest stock of 22K Indian designs and 21K Arabic sets, plus bar dealers for 24K.
  • Meena Bazaar and Karama: South Indian and Pakistani jewellers, almost entirely 22K and 21K, strong on bridal sets.
  • Gold & Diamond Park on Sheikh Zayed Road: 18K dominates because the manufacturers there specialise in diamond settings and custom work.
  • Madinat Zayed in Abu Dhabi and Sharjah's Central Souk: 21K Gulf designs are the core, with 22K for the Indian community.
  • Mall showrooms: branded 18K and 21K collections with fixed, higher making charges.
Take a photo of the stamp inside any piece before you pay and ask the shop to write the karat on the invoice in the same words. A receipt that says "21K 875" is your protection if there is ever a dispute about purity at resale.

Common karat mistakes to avoid

A few errors come up again and again at the counters of Bur Dubai and Al Ain, and each costs money.

  • Comparing rates across karats. A cheap-looking per-gram figure may be 18K; always compare the same karat, or convert everything to 24K-equivalent with the calculator.
  • Paying 22K metal prices for 21K pieces. Check the stamp and the invoice agree.
  • Buying 24K for daily wear. Pure gold scratches and bends; a 24K chain worn every day will show it within months.
  • Assuming colour equals purity. White gold is usually 18K, however bright it looks.
  • Ignoring the buy-back difference. If resale matters, favour plain high-karat pieces over intricate low-karat ones.

Questions people ask

What is the best karat of gold to buy in Dubai?

There is no single best karat, only the best one for the purpose. For investment, 24K bars and coins carry the lowest premium and no VAT. For jewellery that will be resold or gifted within South Asian families, 22K is the standard. For Gulf and Arabic designs worn daily, 21K is the usual choice. For rings with stones and coloured gold, 18K is the right alloy.

Is 22K gold better than 21K gold?

Neither is better; 22K contains slightly more gold (91.6% versus 87.5%) and therefore costs more per gram and has a deeper yellow. 21K is marginally harder, which suits heavier everyday pieces. Preference is cultural: Indian buyers overwhelmingly choose 22K, while Emirati, Saudi, Egyptian and Levantine buyers favour 21K.

How is the gold rate for each karat calculated?

Every karat rate is a share of the 24K rate. Multiply the 24K per-gram figure by 0.916 for 22K, 0.875 for 21K or 0.750 for 18K. The board published daily in Dubai does this for you and rounds the result; the same rates apply in every emirate, and the live strip on this page shows all four at once.

Why is 18K gold so much cheaper per gram?

Because only three quarters of its weight is gold; the rest is alloy metals that cost very little. An 18K piece is therefore priced mostly on design and making charges rather than metal. It is the right choice for diamond settings and white or rose shades, but it recovers less of its purchase price on resale than 21K or 22K.

Can I trust the karat stamp on gold bought in the UAE?

Established shops in the UAE stamp purity and will test a piece in front of you on request, and the market's reputation rests on that reliability. For extra assurance keep the invoice stating the karat and ask for a test at an independent shop. The hallmark and purity test page in this section explains what each test shows.
