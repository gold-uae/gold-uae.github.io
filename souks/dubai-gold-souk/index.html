Dubai Gold Souk in Deira: Layout, Opening Hours, Shops and How to Bargain

The Dubai Gold Souk is the covered market in Al Ras, Deira, where more gold changes hands in a day than in some countries in a month. It is part tourist attraction, part serious trading floor, and both halves are real: the window displays weigh several kilos each, but the families filling the lanes at nine in the evening are there to buy wedding sets, not to take photographs. This guide explains how the souk is laid out, when it opens, what the different zones sell, how pricing works at the counter and how to walk out with a fair deal and a proper invoice.

Quick answer

  • The Dubai Gold Souk is in Al Ras, Deira, beside Gold Souq metro station and the bus station.
  • Shops open roughly 10:00 to 22:00 on most days and start in the afternoon on Friday.
  • The old wooden-roofed lane and the newer covered extension are one market; wholesalers trade upstairs.
  • Every shop sells at the same published rate; you negotiate the making charge, not the gold.
  • Bring cash or a card, your passport for a VAT refund and the patience to compare three shops.

Where the Deira Gold Souk is and how it is laid out

The souk occupies a wedge of old Deira between the creek and the road that carries the Green Line metro. Three parts make up the market.

The old lane

The original pedestrian street runs under a carved wooden roof that keeps the sun off but not the heat. This is the photogenic part, lined with the largest displays and the shops most used to tourists. The famous oversized rings and the heaviest necklaces hang here.

The Gold Souk Extension

The newer covered annex, which many locals still call the new gold souq, continues the market in a more orderly, air-conditioned building with wider corridors. Prices and shops are the same kind; the comfort is better, especially in summer.

The side alleys and upper floors

Turn off the main lanes and you reach the small specialists: shops that sell only chains by the metre, counters that buy old gold, workshops that resize and repair, and above them the wholesale floors where retailers from across the Gulf restock. Residents who know the souk shop in these alleys. For a wider look at the area and the purpose-built trading towers next door, see Gold Center and Gold Land.

Dubai Gold Souk opening hours and timings

There is no single gate that opens and closes; each shop keeps its own hours within a predictable pattern.

DayTypical patternWhat it means for you
Saturday to ThursdayAbout 10:00 to 22:00, some shops pausing in mid-afternoonMornings are quiet; evenings are lively and fully staffed
FridayLater start, usually mid-afternoon, open into the nightDo not arrive before Friday prayers expecting open doors
RamadanShorter daytime hours, much longer after iftarLate evening becomes the main trading time

The best moment to visit for serious buying is the first hour after opening, when the day's rate has just been posted and staff are fresh. The best moment for atmosphere is after sunset, when the lights are on and the lanes fill. Always confirm hours on the day if you are travelling across the city, because individual shops shut for stock-taking without notice.

What the Dubai Gold Souk shops actually sell

The windows look identical from a distance, but the souk is sorted by customer more than by product.

  • Indian and South Asian jewellers dominate the main lane with 22K bridal sets, bangles sold by the dozen, temple-style necklaces and chains sized for every budget. Diwali and the winter wedding season are their peak.
  • Arab and Gulf jewellers stock 21K and 18K, with broader bangles, Emirati-style sets and plenty of white and rose gold. They are busiest before Eid.
  • Bullion and coin counters sell 24K bars from the familiar refineries and minted coins, usually in sealed packaging, at a small premium over the rate. These are the shops bar buyers look for.
  • Diamond and stone specialists cluster in a few corridors and sell certified stones alongside 18K settings.
  • Buyback and exchange shops take old gold for cash or credit against new pieces, which is covered on the selling page.

How pricing works at the counter

A gold souk price is built from four lines, and understanding them is the whole secret to shopping well.

  1. Metal value. Net weight in grams multiplied by the day's rate for that karat. Not negotiable; it comes straight from the published board.
  2. Making charge. Either a per-gram figure or a percentage of the metal value. Entirely negotiable, and it varies enormously between a plain bangle and a filigree choker.
  3. Stones and extras. Priced separately if the piece has them; ask for the stone weight to be deducted from the gold weight.
  4. VAT at 5% on the total. Refundable for tourists through the airport scheme explained on the VAT page.

Ask for each line separately and in writing before you agree. A shop that will only give you a round total is a shop to leave. The making charges page gives typical ranges by piece type so you know when a quote is high.

Pick two or three pieces you like, ask for the making charge on each, then say you will buy the one with the best charge. Shops compete with themselves more readily than with a rival.

Bargaining in the souk without the drama

Negotiation here is expected but it has rules. You are bargaining the making charge and, sometimes, the price of stones; you are not bargaining the gold, so opening with 'what is your best price per gram' marks you as a beginner and wastes everyone's time.

A calm approach works. Ask the making charge, say that another shop quoted lower on a similar piece (if true), and name the figure you would pay. Be ready to walk; the next window is five steps away and the salesman knows it. Buying several items together, paying cash and shopping on a quiet weekday morning all strengthen your hand. Being rude, pretending to be an expert or quoting rates from another country does not.

One more thing: the giant display pieces are not for bargaining practice. They are showpieces, and asking a price for the eleven-kilogram ring is a photo opportunity, not a negotiation. Read the buying guide for the full checklist before your first serious purchase.

Checking purity and avoiding problems

The souk is strictly regulated and shops are inspected, so fakes behind the counter are rare. The real risks are smaller and more ordinary.

  • Check the stamp. Look for 916 on 22K, 875 on 21K, 750 on 18K and 999 on bars, and make sure the invoice says the same. The hallmark guide shows what to look for.
  • Watch the scale. Ask for the piece to be weighed in front of you with the display visible.
  • Ignore the touts. Men on the approach roads offering 'copy watches' and 'good gold shop' are selling neither gold nor anything you want. Walk past.
  • Keep the invoice. It should show weight, karat, rate, making charge and VAT. It is your proof for a VAT refund, an exchange, a resale or an insurance claim.
  • Use a card when the sum is large. Carrying thousands in cash through a crowd is unnecessary; almost every shop accepts cards, sometimes with a small surcharge that you can negotiate away.

Making a visit of it: what is around the souk

The gold souk sits in the oldest commercial quarter of Dubai, and the walk around it is half the pleasure. The spice souk begins almost at the gold market's edge, with sacks of saffron, dried limes and frankincense. A few minutes further the abra stations on the creek offer a crossing to Bur Dubai for a single coin, landing you near the textile souk and the Al Fahidi heritage lanes. Grand Souk Deira, restored around the old covered market, is good for a shaded walk, and small cafeterias serve tea and parathas late into the night.

If you are planning the practical side, including which metro station to use, where the bus station is, parking and the abra route, the getting there page handles all of it. And if the souk's crowds are not your style, the Gold & Diamond Park on Sheikh Zayed Road is the air-conditioned alternative with the same rate and quieter counters.

Souk, mall or online: where the souk still wins

Every big jeweller now has mall branches and a website, so is the souk worth the trip? For some purchases, clearly yes.

The souk wins on choice, on making charges for plain pieces and on anything needing a workshop. Malls win on branding and peace of mind. Many residents do their research online, check the Dubai rate, then buy in Deira where the counter price can be argued down.

Questions people ask

What are the Dubai Gold Souk timings today?

Most shops open around ten in the morning and close around ten at night from Saturday to Thursday, with some taking a mid-afternoon break. On Friday the souk comes alive in the afternoon and stays open late. During Ramadan hours shift to the evening. Confirm with a specific shop before travelling for a particular purchase.

Is the gold souk in Deira cheaper than shops elsewhere in Dubai?

The per-gram rate is identical across the city because it follows the published Dubai rate. The souk is usually cheaper on making charges, especially for plain 22K and 21K pieces, and it gives you dozens of shops to compare within a few metres. On branded designer jewellery the saving is small.

Which metro station is closest to the gold souk?

Gold Souq station on the Green Line, formerly called Palm Deira, is the nearest, with Al Ras station a short walk on the other side of the market. The Gold Souq Bus Station is beside the souk, and abras from Bur Dubai land a few minutes' walk away. Our getting there page has the full routes.

Can tourists get the VAT back on gold bought at the souk?

Yes. Ask the shop to issue a tax-free invoice under the Planet refund scheme at the time of purchase, keep the jewellery with you, and validate the refund at the airport before you fly within 90 days. The minimum spend is AED 250 on one invoice and the refund is paid to your card or in cash.

Can I pay by card at the Dubai Gold Souk?

Almost every shop accepts major cards, and many take mobile wallets. A few add a small surcharge for card payments, which you can usually negotiate away on a sizeable purchase. Cash is still welcome and can earn a slightly better making charge, but there is no need to carry large sums.

Is the new Gold Souq extension a different market?

No. The covered extension is part of the same Deira gold souk, built to add more orderly, air-conditioned space beside the old wooden-roofed lane. The shops, the rate and the trading rules are the same. Walk through both; the extension is more comfortable in summer, the old lane has more atmosphere.
