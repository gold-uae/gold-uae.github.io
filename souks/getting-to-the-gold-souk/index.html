How to Reach the Dubai Gold Souk: Metro Station, Bus Station, Abra and Parking

Reaching the gold souk in Dubai is simple once you know one thing: it sits in Al Ras, Deira, on the Green Line of the metro, not the Red Line that most visitors ride. Arrive by train and you step out a few minutes' walk from the first shop window; arrive by car and you spend those minutes circling for a parking bay. This page sets out every route, from the two metro stations and the bus station to the abra crossing and the intercity buses from Sharjah and Abu Dhabi, with honest notes on which is worth your time.

Quick answer

  • The nearest metro to the gold souk is Gold Souq station on the Green Line, with Al Ras one stop away.
  • There is no gold line on the Dubai Metro; Gold Souq is a Green Line stop, so change from the Red Line at Union or BurJuman.
  • The Gold Souq Bus Station sits beside the market and is the easiest taxi drop-off point.
  • From Bur Dubai, an abra across the creek lands you a short walk from the shops.
  • Parking is scarce and metered; leave the car and ride the metro if you can.

Gold Souk metro station: which stop to use

Two Green Line stations serve the market, and both are elevated stops a short distance apart.

StationLineWalk to the soukBest for
Gold Souq (formerly Palm Deira)GreenRoughly five to ten minutesFirst-time visitors; the bus station and the covered extension are close
Al RasGreenRoughly five minutesThe old wooden-roofed lane, the spice souk and the creek side

The station was renamed from Palm Deira to Gold Souq in 2020, so an older map or a friend's directions may still use the old name; they mean the same platform. Coming from the Union direction, Gold Souq is the stop after Baniyas Square; Al Ras is the next one; Al Ghubaiba, across the creek, follows. Either station works, and many people arrive at one and leave from the other after walking the length of the market. The souk guide shows what lies between them.

Buy a Nol card or a day pass at the first station you enter. It covers metro, bus and abra, and saves you fumbling for coins at the creek.

Is there a Dubai Metro gold line? Changing from the Red Line

People search for a gold line because the station is called Gold Souq, but Dubai's metro has only a Red Line and a Green Line, and the souk is on the green one. If you are staying in Downtown, the Marina, Jumeirah Lakes Towers or near the airport, you will start on the Red Line and change once.

  1. Ride the Red Line to Union or BurJuman, the two interchange stations.
  2. Follow the signs to the Green Line platform; it is in the same station building, with no extra ticket.
  3. Take the train towards Creek if you are at Union, which brings you to Gold Souq after Baniyas Square. From BurJuman, take the train towards Etisalat, passing Al Ghubaiba and Al Ras before Gold Souq.

From Dubai International Airport, Terminals 1 and 3 have their own Red Line stations; change at Union. Total travel time from the Marina is close to an hour, from Downtown about half that. Along the way, the Red Line also passes the Gold & Diamond Park, so a day covering both markets is easy to plan; see the Gold & Diamond Park page.

Gold Souq bus station: buses and taxis

The Gold Souq Bus Station is a large terminus wedged against the market. It handles local Deira routes, several cross-city lines and a stream of taxis, which makes it the simplest landmark to give a driver: say 'Gold Souq bus station' and you will be set down at the market's edge.

Buses run from early morning until late at night, with frequencies that vary by route and day; the RTA journey planner on your phone is the reliable way to check timings for the exact line you need. Intercity buses from Sharjah and the northern emirates mostly arrive at Union or Al Ghubaiba, both Green Line stations one or two stops from the souk, though a few routes serve the Gold Souq station directly.

Taxis are plentiful on the way in and harder to find on the way out at peak evening hours. If you leave the souk with a purchase after nine at night, walk to the bus station rank rather than waiting at the kerb, or take the metro from Al Ras. The gold shops near me page lists other gold areas you can reach from the same bus station if Deira does not have what you want.

By abra from Bur Dubai

The oldest route to the gold souk is still the most enjoyable. Traditional wooden abras shuttle across the creek all day between the Bur Dubai side, near the textile souk and the Al Fahidi district, and the Deira Old Souk landing beside the spice market. The crossing takes a few minutes, costs about a dirham paid to the boatman or by Nol card, and lands you a short walk from the gold shops.

  • From Al Fahidi or Al Ghubaiba metro, walk to the Bur Dubai abra station on the creek promenade.
  • Board the next boat; they leave when full, which rarely takes long.
  • On the Deira side, walk inland through the spice souk and the gold market opens up ahead of you.

This route pairs well with a half-day of old Dubai: textiles and the museum district on one bank, spices and gold on the other. It is also the quickest way back to a Bur Dubai hotel after shopping, because you skip the road traffic around the creek bridges entirely. Visitors who want to understand why this stretch of water made Dubai the City of Gold will enjoy the history page on the crossing.

Driving and gold souk parking

You can drive to the gold souk, and plenty of residents do, but go in with realistic expectations. The streets of Al Ras are narrow, one-way and shared with delivery vans; kerbside bays are metered RTA zones that fill by late morning and stay full until the shops close; and the evening rush around the bus station is slow.

If you must drive:

  • Aim for a weekday morning, when bays near the covered extension are still free.
  • Pay for parking by SMS or the RTA app rather than hunting for a meter, and set a reminder before it expires; fines are routine here.
  • Look for paid multi-storey parking close to the bus station rather than circling the market lanes.
  • Do not leave purchases visible in the car, and do not leave the car on a yellow line even for a minute.

A middle path many families use is to park at a Red Line station with a car park, then ride the metro in. It avoids the Deira traffic altogether and the walk from Al Ras station is shorter than the walk from most parking bays.

Reaching the souk from Sharjah, Ajman and Abu Dhabi

The gold souk draws buyers from every emirate, and each has a sensible route.

  • From Sharjah and Ajman. Driving is quick outside rush hour but painful between four and eight in the evening. Intercity buses to Union or Al Ghubaiba followed by one or two metro stops are the stress-free option. Before you make the trip, consider whether Sharjah's own gold souks already stock what you want at the same rate.
  • From Abu Dhabi and Al Ain. Intercity buses arrive at Al Ghubaiba, which is a Green Line station two stops from Al Ras. By car, allow around ninety minutes to Deira plus parking time, and consider the metro park-and-ride at a Red Line station near the highway.
  • From the northern emirates. Buses to Dubai generally terminate at Union or Al Ghubaiba; both connect to the souk by metro in minutes.

Whichever emirate you start from, the per-gram price is the same at home and in Deira; the trip is about range and bargaining, not a cheaper rate. The Dubai rate page lets you check the day's figure before you leave.

On foot: the walking route and accessibility

From either metro station, exit on the market side and follow the flow of people; signage points to the souk and the lanes are impossible to miss once you reach the covered area. The streets are flat but busy, with occasional steps at shop entrances and uneven paving in the oldest sections. Wheelchair and pushchair users will find the covered extension easier than the old lane, and both metro stations have lifts.

Summer heat is the main obstacle. Between May and September the walk from the platform to the first shaded lane can be uncomfortable in the afternoon; go in the morning or after dark, carry water and plan to spend the hottest hours inside the air-conditioned extension or in the nearby gold buildings. Comfortable shoes matter more than smart ones; you will walk further than you expect once you start comparing shops.

Leaving the souk with your gold

The journey home deserves a moment's thought when you are carrying something valuable. Keep purchases in the shop's bag inside a plain tote rather than in a branded carrier; sit inside a metro carriage rather than standing by the doors; and if you have spent a large sum, consider a taxi from the bus station rank to your door instead of a long walk from the station at the other end. Tourists who plan to claim the VAT refund should keep the tax-free invoice with the jewellery, because airport staff may ask to see both, as explained on the VAT refund page. None of this is cause for anxiety; Deira is a safe and heavily policed district. It is simply the same sense you would use anywhere after a big purchase.

Questions people ask

What is the nearest metro station to the gold souk?

Gold Souq station on the Green Line is the nearest, a short walk from the covered extension and the bus station. Al Ras, the next station, is equally convenient for the old lane and the spice souk. Both are elevated stations with lifts and escalators, and either makes a good starting point.

Is the gold souk on the Red Line or the Green Line?

The Green Line. There is no gold line on the Dubai Metro, despite the station name. Red Line passengers change at Union or BurJuman, where the two lines meet inside the same station, and ride the Green Line one to three stops to Gold Souq or Al Ras.

Where do I get the abra to the gold souk?

From the Bur Dubai abra station on the creek promenade near the textile souk and the Al Fahidi area. Boats cross continuously during the day and evening to the Deira Old Souk landing, next to the spice souk. From there the gold souk is a few minutes' walk inland. The fare is about a dirham.

Is there parking at the Dubai Gold Souk?

There is metered street parking and some paid car parks near the Gold Souq bus station, but spaces are scarce from late morning onwards and the surrounding streets are narrow and congested. Most regular visitors use the metro instead, or park at a Red Line station and ride in.

What time does the Gold Souq bus station operate?

Buses run from early morning until late at night, with frequency depending on the route and the day of the week. Use the RTA journey planner app to confirm the timetable for your specific line before you travel, especially on Fridays and public holidays when schedules change.
