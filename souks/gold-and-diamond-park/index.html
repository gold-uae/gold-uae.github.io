Gold and Diamond Park Dubai: shops, timings and how it compares with the Gold Souk

The Gold and Diamond Park is the calm, chilled alternative to the crowded lanes of Deira. It sits on the Al Quoz side of Sheikh Zayed Road, a short taxi ride from Mall of the Emirates, and it was built from the start as a jewellery destination: showrooms on the public floors and workshops behind them, where goldsmiths and diamond setters actually make what you see in the windows.

This guide explains what the shops sell, the timings, how pricing works and, most importantly, when you should come here instead of the Dubai Gold Souk.

Quick answer

  • Gold and Diamond Park is an indoor, air-conditioned jewellery mall on Sheikh Zayed Road, Al Quoz side.
  • Showrooms and manufacturing workshops share the building, so custom designs are its real strength.
  • Shops open roughly 10:00 to 22:00, with a later start on Friday; confirm with the shop first.
  • Gold costs the same per gram as in Deira; you pay for craft and stones, not metal.
  • Choose it for diamonds, 18K engagement rings and bespoke work; choose Deira for 22K bulk buying.

What the Gold and Diamond Park actually is

Think of it as a purpose-built jewellery complex rather than a souk. A low-rise building with a central arcade holds dozens of retail showrooms, while the surrounding units are licensed manufacturing workshops. That mix is unusual in the UAE: in Deira most shops buy finished pieces from wholesalers, whereas here many of the shops are selling what their own benches produced upstairs or next door.

The public part is fully air-conditioned, with marble floors, cafés and free parking, which is why so many Jumeirah and Al Barsha residents treat it as their default jeweller. There is none of the shoulder-to-shoulder bustle of the Al Ras lanes, and nobody calls out to you from a doorway.

Because manufacturers are on site, the Park also has a quiet trade side. Smaller jewellers from Sharjah or Abu Dhabi source castings, findings and diamond setting here, and if you ask the right questions you can tap into that same workshop pricing for a one-off piece.

Gold and Diamond Park timings and how to get there

Most showrooms keep mall-style hours: around 10:00 in the morning until about 22:00 at night, seven days a week, with Friday opening pushed to the afternoon in many shops. Workshops run office-style hours and often close earlier, so if you are collecting a custom order or need a resize, aim for a weekday afternoon. Always check the individual shop's hours before a special trip, especially during Ramadan when evening trade starts late.

By car

The Park sits beside Sheikh Zayed Road between the Al Quoz interchanges, with its own surface parking that rarely fills up outside Diwali and Dubai Shopping Festival weekends. Coming from Abu Dhabi, take the service road after Mall of the Emirates; from Deira or Bur Dubai, stay on Sheikh Zayed Road southbound and follow the Al Quoz exit signs.

By metro or taxi

The Red Line runs parallel to the road, but in summer the walk from the nearest station is unpleasant, so most visitors take the metro to Mall of the Emirates and finish with a short taxi hop. A taxi from Downtown or the Marina is a straightforward ten to twenty minute ride outside rush hour.

What the Gold and Diamond Park shops sell

The name is accurate: the balance tilts toward diamonds and finer gold rather than heavy 22K sets. Walking the arcade you will find three broad types of shop.

Diamond and bridal specialists

Solitaires, halo rings, tennis bracelets and eternity bands, usually in 18K yellow, white or rose gold or platinum. Many shops hand you a loupe and a grading certificate without being asked, which is the expected standard here.

Contemporary gold boutiques

Lighter chains, Italian-style bracelets, pendants and coloured-gold pieces aimed at the European and Arab market. Weights are lower, finishes are more varied, and white and rose gold are everywhere.

Manufacturers with a front counter

These are the units where a goldsmith works at the back and a small showcase sits at the front. They are the best place for repairs, stone replacement, re-plating of white gold, remakes of old jewellery and genuinely bespoke designs drawn from a sketch or a photograph.

You will still find some 21K and 22K, particularly from the Indian jewellers who have a presence here, but if your shopping list is wedding bangles by the dozen, the Deira souk and the Gold Land and Gold Center buildings carry far deeper stock.

The per-gram gold rate is identical in both places, because every shop in the UAE works from the same daily board. The real difference is what sits on top of the metal: stones, design and labour. If you are buying gold mainly as a store of value, Deira is cheaper per gram once making charges are counted. If you are buying a piece you will wear every day and want it made properly, the Park usually wins.

On top of the metal the shop adds making charges, which here are more often a percentage of the gold value or a fixed design fee than the per-gram figures used in Deira. For diamond pieces the stones are priced separately by carat weight, colour, clarity and cut, and the gold mounting is a minor part of the bill.

Jewellery carries 5% VAT in the UAE. Tourists can reclaim it at the airport through the tax-free refund scheme as long as the shop issues a tax-free form at the counter, so ask before you pay. Residents cannot reclaim VAT, which is one more reason to negotiate the making charge rather than the metal.

A simple habit: ask the salesperson to write the gold weight, the karat, the making charge and the stone cost on four separate lines. Shops that make their own pieces are rarely shy about this breakdown.

Ordering a custom piece: the workshop route step by step

The Park's best trick is turning an idea into a finished piece without a middleman. A typical bespoke order, say a 7 gram 18K signet ring with a family crest or a 15 gram 21K bangle copied from a grandmother's design, goes like this.

  1. Bring a reference. A photo, a sketch or the original piece. Workshops are used to working from a phone screen.
  2. Agree the specification. Karat, colour, approximate weight, stone sizes and finish. Ask for an estimated weight range rather than a single figure, because casting always varies slightly.
  3. Get a written quote. Gold at the daily rate on the day of delivery or the day of order; clarify which one, because the two can differ by the time the piece is ready.
  4. Pay a deposit. Usually a fraction of the total, with the balance on collection after you have weighed and inspected the piece.
  5. Check a wax or CAD model. Many workshops will show a 3D render or a wax before casting, which is the moment to change proportions.
  6. Collect and verify. Weigh it on the shop scale, check the hallmark and the stone certificate if any, and keep the invoice for resale or insurance.

Lead times run from a few days for a simple band to a few weeks for a stone-set piece in a busy season such as the weeks before Eid or Diwali.

Who should shop here, and who should go elsewhere

Three quick scenarios show where the Park fits.

The couple choosing an engagement ring. This is the Park's home turf. You can compare certified diamonds in several shops within a few minutes' walk, set the stone into an 18K or platinum mount made on site, and come back for free resizing. Read our ring buying guide first so you know what the certificate should say.

The Al Barsha family buying Eid gifts. If the gifts are small 21K pendants or light bracelets, the Park is convenient and pleasant, and the parking alone saves an hour. If the plan is a heavy 22K set for a wedding, drive to Deira; the choice there is many times wider and the per-gram making charges lower.

The tourist with one afternoon. Honestly, go to the Deira souk for the experience and the photos, then come to the Park only if you are after a diamond. Doing both in one day is possible but tiring.

Visit on a weekday afternoon and ask any showroom whether its workshop is in the building. Shops that manufacture on site can usually adjust a design or quote a remake on the spot, and they tend to be the most flexible on labour charges.

Mistakes visitors make at the Gold and Diamond Park

A few patterns come up again and again from readers who felt they overpaid.

  • Comparing a diamond ring with a plain gold ring by price per gram. The metal is a small part of a stone-set piece; compare the stone specification and the total.
  • Skipping the hallmark check. Even in a polished showroom, look for the karat stamp and ask for the purity on the invoice. Our guide to hallmarks and purity testing covers what the marks mean.
  • Assuming white gold is pure white. It is rhodium-plated and will need re-plating every year or two; the workshops here do it quickly, but budget for it.
  • Forgetting the tax-free form. Tourists lose the VAT refund if the shop does not register the sale at the counter.
  • Paying the metal at an old rate. If the price was quoted days earlier, the shop should update the gold portion to today's board. Use the gold calculator on your phone to check the weight times rate before you tap your card.

None of this makes the Park a risky place to shop. It is one of the most transparent jewellery destinations in the city; you simply need to bring the same basic discipline you would use anywhere else.

Questions people ask

What are the Gold and Diamond Park timings?

Showrooms generally open at about 10:00 and trade until roughly 22:00, with many shops starting in the afternoon on Friday. Manufacturing workshops keep shorter, weekday-style hours. Timings shift during Ramadan and public holidays, so call the specific shop before a long drive, particularly if you are collecting a custom order.

Is the Gold and Diamond Park cheaper than the Gold Souk?

For the gold itself, no: both use the same daily UAE rate per gram. For labour, Deira is usually cheaper on plain 22K pieces because volumes are higher and bargaining is routine. For diamonds and bespoke 18K work the Park is often better value, because you are dealing directly with the manufacturer rather than a shop that bought the piece in.

Can I get jewellery made to my own design there?

Yes, and that is the main reason to go. Bring a photo or sketch, agree karat, weight and stones, pay a deposit and the workshop casts and finishes the piece on site. Simple bands take days; stone-set pieces take longer, especially before Eid or Diwali. Ask whether the gold is priced at the order date or the delivery date.

Do the shops sell 22K and 21K gold or only diamonds?

Both exist, but the emphasis is on 18K and diamond jewellery. A number of Indian and Arab jewellers stock 21K and 22K chains, bangles and sets, yet the range is thin compared with Deira. If high-karat weight is the goal, the Gold Souk and the nearby Gold Land and Gold Center buildings are the better hunting ground.

Is there parking and is it free?

Yes. The complex has its own surface parking directly off the Sheikh Zayed Road service road, and it is free for visitors. It only gets tight on the busiest shopping evenings of the year. This convenience, plus full air-conditioning, is why residents from Jumeirah, Al Barsha and the Springs favour it over driving into Deira.
