Why is Dubai called the City of Gold? The history, the rules and what it means for buyers

Dubai is called the City of Gold because, for most of the last eighty years, more physical gold has passed through its creek, airport and free zones than through almost any other city of its size. The nickname is partly marketing, polished yearly by the Dubai Shopping Festival, but it rests on real trading history and on tax rules that still make this one of the simplest places anywhere to buy and sell the metal.

This page tells the story in order: how it started, which rules keep it going, who sets the daily rate, and what it means when you walk into a Deira souk shop.

Quick answer

  • Dubai earned the name City of Gold through decades of re-exporting gold across the Indian Ocean.
  • No import duty, 5% VAT only on jewellery and zero tax on bullion keep the trade here.
  • A single daily Dubai gold rate, set from the world spot price, is used across the UAE.
  • The Deira souk, the Gold and Diamond Park and the DMCC free zone are the three pillars.
  • For buyers it means the metal costs the same as anywhere, but the markup is lower.

The short answer to "why is Dubai called the City of Gold"

Three things came together. First, geography: Dubai sits between the gold-consuming markets of South Asia and the gold-producing and refining centres of Africa, Europe and the former Soviet space, with a natural harbour that merchants used long before there was an airport. Second, policy: the rulers of Dubai chose free trade early, charged no duty on gold, and later created free zones built around commodities. Third, people: a resident population drawn largely from India, Pakistan, the Gulf and the Levant, where gold is savings, dowry and celebration all at once.

Put those together and you get a city where gold is not a luxury niche but an everyday commodity, priced in the open on shop boards, traded by the kilo upstairs and worn by the gram downstairs. The phrase "City of Gold" was adopted by the trade itself and became a slogan, but a slogan that merchants from Mumbai to Nairobi recognise as fair.

Every other section on this page is a longer version of that paragraph.

From pearls to gold: how the creek trade began

Before oil, Dubai lived on the creek. Pearling and the re-export of goods landed from dhows paid for the town. When the pearl market collapsed in the 1930s, the merchant families of Deira and Bur Dubai needed something else to carry, and over the following decades gold became that cargo.

The decisive push came from India. When India tightened its gold import rules in the middle of the twentieth century, demand there did not fall; it simply had to be met from outside. Dubai's traders, already connected to Bombay and the Malabar coast by family and by sea, became the regional entrepôt. Gold arrived from London and Switzerland, was stored in the lanes behind the creek, and left again on wooden boats bound east. The old Deira souk grew up around this flow, and the trade buildings that now stand beside it are its direct descendants.

As India gradually liberalised and the Gulf grew wealthy, the re-export business turned into a retail and refining one. The dhows gave way to air freight, the family offices became licensed refineries and bullion houses, and the city kept the name it had earned.

The rules that keep the gold in Dubai

Trading history fades unless the rules stay friendly. Dubai's have, and they are simple enough to list.

  • No import duty on gold. Metal enters the UAE at the world price. That is the single biggest reason jewellery here is cheaper than in India or Pakistan, where duty is layered on top.
  • VAT at 5%, and only on jewellery. Since 2018 the UAE has charged 5% VAT on gold jewellery, while investment-grade bullion of 99% purity or more is zero-rated and business-to-business trade uses a reverse charge. Our VAT and tax refund page has the detail, including how tourists claim the 5% back at the airport.
  • A fixed dirham. With the dirham pegged to the US dollar since 1997, the local gold price moves one-to-one with the international spot price, with no currency noise in between.
  • Free zones built for commodities. The DMCC free zone in Jumeirah Lakes Towers was created in 2002 to house gold, diamond and commodity firms under one regulator, with its own vaults and quality standards.
  • Open competition. Thousands of traders, most of them small, pricing from one public rate. Margins stay thin because the shop next door is always visible.

None of these rules make the gold itself cheaper. They make the cost of getting it into your hands lower, which is the honest definition of the "Dubai discount".

The Dubai City of Gold rate: who sets it and how to read it

Ask for the "Dubai city of gold rate" and what you are really asking for is the retail gold rate published every day by the Dubai Gold and Jewellery Group and displayed on the boards of every souk and mall shop. It is quoted in dirhams per gram for 24K, 22K, 21K and 18K, follows the international spot price through the day, and is used by shops in all seven emirates.

Reading it is straightforward once you know the purities: 24K is 999 fine, 22K is 916, 21K is 875 and 18K is 750. The lower karats are the 24K rate multiplied by those fractions, give or take a few fils of rounding. Shops add making charges on top, which is where the real negotiation lives.

Behind the retail board sit the wholesale institutions that make Dubai more than a shopping street: the DMCC's good-delivery standard for refined bars, the Dubai Gold and Commodities Exchange for futures contracts, the refineries that turn scrap and doré into stamped kilobars, and the airport cargo village through which the metal moves. The souk and the shopping calendar

The physical heart of the City of Gold is still the Deira souk: covered lanes in Al Ras where hundreds of small shops show bangles stacked like bread and necklaces the size of breastplates. It is a tourist attraction, a working wholesale hub and a neighbourhood market at the same time, with the Gold Souk Extension, the Gold Land and Gold Center buildings and the Green Line metro station all feeding into it.

Around it the city built a calendar. The Dubai Shopping Festival, launched in the mid-1990s, made gold promotions and raffles a yearly ritual every winter. Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha bring Gulf and Arab families in for gifts and bridal sets. Diwali and the Indian wedding season bring the subcontinent's buyers for 22K. In between, the Gold and Diamond Park on Sheikh Zayed Road handles the diamond and bespoke trade in air-conditioned calm, and the malls carry the big brand showrooms.

That rhythm matters for the buyer. The weeks before a festival are when stock is widest and making-charge offers most aggressive; the quiet months of summer are when a patient buyer can negotiate labour hardest. Who buys gold in the City of Gold, and why

The demand side explains the nickname as much as the supply side does.

Residents sending value home

Millions of expatriates from India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, Sri Lanka and the Philippines earn in dirhams and think in their home currency. For many, a few grams of 22K bought from a monthly salary is a savings plan that travels, which is why the customs rules for carrying gold to India are one of our most-read pages.

Gulf families marking life events

Weddings, births and Eid all call for gold, usually 21K in Emirati, Saudi and Omani designs, bought by weight and kept for decades.

Tourists and investors

Visitors come for the souk experience and the VAT refund; investors from around the world come for bars and coins at thin premiums, for vaulting in the free zone, and for the online and futures markets described on our gold trading page.

Three very different buyers, one shop board, one price. That shared transparency is why trust in Dubai gold has held up for generations.

Is the City of Gold actually cheaper?

Honest answer: the metal is not, the total bill usually is. A gram of pure gold costs the same in Dubai, Mumbai, London and Lagos on any given minute, because it is one global market priced in dollars. What differs is everything stacked on top of the metal.

What the nickname means for you today

History is pleasant, but you came here to buy or sell. These are the practical consequences of living in, or visiting, the City of Gold.

  1. Trust the board, negotiate the rest. The per-gram rate is public and identical everywhere; spend your energy on making charges and stone pricing.
  2. Use karat to your advantage. 22K for Indian resale, 21K for Gulf designs, 18K for daily wear and stones, 24K for bars. Our 22K guide explains why that karat dominates the souk.
  3. Buy bullion tax-free. Bars and coins of 99% purity carry no VAT, which makes the UAE one of the cheapest places to hold physical gold; see the gold bars guide for sizes and premiums.
  4. Keep paperwork. An invoice with weight, karat and VAT is what makes resale, customs and insurance painless.
  5. Time the calendar. Festival weeks for choice, quiet months for bargaining power.
  6. Sell where you bought. The same transparency that lets you buy by weight lets you sell by weight, usually within minutes.
Whatever you buy in the City of Gold, ask for the gold weight and the making charge on two separate lines of the invoice. Every honest shop in Dubai does this without hesitation, and the habit protects you from the only markup that can hide.

Questions people ask

Why is Dubai called the City of Gold?

Because for decades Dubai has been one of the world's main hubs for physical gold: first as a creek port re-exporting to India, then as a retail, refining and free-zone centre. No import duty, low tax, a dollar-pegged currency and a population that buys gold for savings and celebrations keep the trade here, and the Dubai Shopping Festival turned the phrase into a brand.

Is gold really cheaper in the City of Gold?

The metal costs the same everywhere in the world at any given moment. What is lower in Dubai is the markup: no import duty, only 5% VAT on jewellery that tourists can reclaim, no VAT at all on investment bullion, and competitive making charges. The saving is largest on plain high-karat jewellery and bars, smallest on diamond and designer pieces.

What is the Dubai City of Gold rate?

It is simply the daily retail gold rate for Dubai, published by the Dubai Gold and Jewellery Group in dirhams per gram for 24K, 22K, 21K and 18K. It tracks the international spot price in US dollars, converted at the fixed dirham peg, and the same rate is used by shops across the UAE. The live figures are on the board on this page.

Where is the City of Gold's main market?

The Dubai Gold Souk in Deira, in the Al Ras district beside the creek, reached by the Gold Souq and Al Ras metro stations on the Green Line. Around it are the Gold Souk Extension and the Gold Land and Gold Center trade buildings. The Gold and Diamond Park on Sheikh Zayed Road is the modern, air-conditioned counterpart for diamonds and custom work.

When did Dubai's gold trade start?

Gold became Dubai's main re-export cargo in the decades after the pearl trade collapsed in the 1930s, especially once India restricted gold imports in the mid-twentieth century and demand had to be met from abroad. The modern phase began with the free-trade policies of the 1970s and the creation of the DMCC free zone in 2002 and the Dubai Gold and Commodities Exchange soon after.
