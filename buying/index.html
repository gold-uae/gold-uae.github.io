Buying Gold in Dubai: The Complete Guide for Residents and Visitors

Gold is one of the few things in the UAE where the price is genuinely transparent. The metal rate is public, printed on a board in every shop from Deira to Madinat Zayed, and it is the same in all seven emirates. What changes from counter to counter is the making charge, the hallmark, the design and how much the salesperson is prepared to negotiate. This hub walks you through the whole process, then points you to the detailed guide for each step.

Whether you are an expat putting part of a salary into bangles, a tourist with one afternoon at the souk, or a trader comparing wholesale rows, the pages below are organised around what you need to decide first.

Quick answer

  • Every shop in the UAE prices gold from the same daily per-gram rate; only making charges differ.
  • Compare three shops on the same day and ask for the making charge in dirhams per gram.
  • Tourists can claim back 5% VAT on jewellery through the Planet refund desk at departure.
  • First purchase? Start with the how-to-buy page, then read about making charges.
  • Investment-grade bars and coins are zero-rated for VAT; jewellery is not.

How buying gold in Dubai actually works

Walk into any jewellery shop in the UAE and you will see a lit board with four or five numbers on it: the per-gram rate for 24K, 22K, 21K and 18K, sometimes 14K too. That board is not the shop's own price. It is the retail rate published by the Dubai Gold & Jewellery Group, which tracks the international spot price in US dollars and converts it at the fixed peg of 3.6725 dirhams. Because the peg does not move, the dirham rate rises and falls exactly as the dollar price does.

The price of a piece is built in three layers. First, the weight in grams multiplied by the board rate for its karat. Second, the making charge, which is the shop's fee for labour, design and overhead. Third, 5% VAT on the whole amount for jewellery. A stone-set piece may add a separate charge for the stones. Nothing about the metal layer is negotiable; it is the same in Sharjah's Blue Souk as in Dubai Mall. Everything about the making layer is. That single fact drives most of the advice on this site.

Where to buy: souks, malls, brands and factories

The second decision is the venue, and each type of seller has a distinct personality.

Traditional gold rows

The Deira Gold Souk, Meena Bazaar in Bur Dubai, Rolla in Sharjah and the Madinat Zayed centre in Abu Dhabi are where bargaining still happens and where small family shops compete side by side. If you simply want to know which row is closest to your flat or hotel, the gold shops near me guide maps the main clusters in every emirate, including Karama, Satwa and the Dubai Mall gold area.

Branded chains

Malabar, Joyalukkas, Damas, Pure Gold and the other large retailers run fixed-price counters with certificates, exchange policies and frequent promotions. The best jewellers and brands page compares what each chain is known for and when a brand premium is worth paying.

Wholesale and manufacturers

Above a few hundred grams, or if you are buying stock to resell, the economics change. The wholesalers and factories page explains the Gold & Diamond Park, the trading floors around Al Ras and what "wholesale rate" really means when the metal price is public anyway.

Gold buying tips UAE shoppers learn the hard way

Ask anyone who has bought gold here for a decade and the same lessons come up.

  1. Weigh it in front of you. Every counter has a calibrated scale. Watch the display, note the grams on the receipt, and make sure stones are either deducted or priced separately.
  2. Ask for the making charge in dirhams per gram, not just a total. A total hides the arithmetic. A per-gram figure can be compared across shops in seconds.
  3. Check the stamp. 916 for 22K, 875 for 21K, 750 for 18K. The hallmark guide shows where to find it and what a shop assay looks like.
  4. Shop late in the week for bargaining, early in the day for attention. Weekend evenings in Deira are crowded; a Monday morning gives you the owner's full time.
  5. Keep the receipt forever. It states weight, karat, rate and making charge, and it is what a buyer will ask for when you sell.
Before you leave the flat, check the live rate on your phone and write it down. If the board in the shop shows a higher number, you are either looking at a different karat or the rate has moved during the day; either way, you are now equipped to ask.

Timing your purchase: seasons, festivals and the daily rate

Two clocks matter. The first is the international one: the spot price trades almost around the clock from Monday morning in Asia to Friday evening in New York, so the UAE board can change several times a day. Many residents simply check the live chart and buy on a dip rather than trying to predict the week.

The second clock is local demand. Making charges and promotions move with the calendar even when the metal does not. The run-up to Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha, the Diwali and Onam weeks, the Dubai Shopping Festival in winter and the wedding season from November to March all bring "zero making charge" banners and free-gold-coin offers. These promotions are real but conditional; read the small print on minimum weights and exchange terms.

Conversely, the quiet summer months, when many families travel, are when small souk shops are most willing to cut a deal on labour. If you are flexible, a July purchase of a plain 21K chain can cost noticeably less in making charge than the same chain in December, even if the gram rate is identical.

What to check at the counter, line by line

A good receipt in the UAE reads like a short invoice, and every line should match what you agreed.

  • Karat and purity stamp: 22K/916, 21K/875, 18K/750. The number on the receipt must match the number on the piece.
  • Net gold weight in grams, to two decimals. For stone-set pieces, gross weight and stone weight should be listed separately.
  • Rate per gram applied, which should equal the board rate at the time of sale. Compare it with the Dubai gold rate on your phone.
  • Making charge, either per gram or as a percentage, shown as its own line.
  • VAT at 5% on the total, with the shop's tax registration number printed on the invoice. Tourists need this invoice for the Planet refund.
  • Exchange and buy-back terms, usually a printed sentence at the bottom. Many shops exchange old gold at full metal value against new pieces, which is useful if you upgrade later.

If any line is missing, ask for it to be added before you pay. Reputable shops do this without hesitation; a shop that refuses to put the making charge in writing is telling you something.

Selling it back later: plan the exit on the way in

The best buying decision takes the eventual sale into account. A plain 22K bangle from a souk shop with a low per-gram making charge will return close to its metal value when you sell. A heavily stone-set 18K designer piece bought at a mall with a high percentage charge may return half of what you paid, because buyers pay for metal, not for brand or craft.

Three habits protect your exit. Keep the original invoice; it proves karat and weight. Prefer pieces whose weight is mostly gold rather than stones or hollow sections. And know in advance that most shops will exchange old gold against new at the board rate, while a cash sale usually carries a small deduction. The selling guide covers the process, and gold loans are an alternative if you need cash but want to keep the jewellery.

Finally, use the gold calculator before you walk in: enter the grams and karat and you will know the metal value to the dirham, which turns the negotiation into a conversation purely about the making charge.

Questions people ask

What is the best place to buy gold in Dubai?

It depends on what you want. The Deira Gold Souk and Meena Bazaar give the widest choice and the lowest making charges if you are willing to compare and negotiate. Branded chains in malls give certificates, exchange policies and consistency. The Gold & Diamond Park suits custom orders and larger quantities. For plain 21K or 22K pieces, a small souk shop usually wins on price; for a certified diamond-set piece, a brand is safer.

Is the gold rate the same in every emirate?

Yes. The daily retail rate per gram is the same in Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Sharjah, Ajman, Al Ain, Fujairah and Ras Al Khaimah, because it is derived from the same international spot price and the fixed dollar peg. What differs from shop to shop and city to city is the making charge, the level of competition and how willing a seller is to discount labour on a quiet day.

Can I pay by card, and does it change the price?

Almost every shop accepts cards, and most accept bank transfer for larger amounts. Some smaller souk shops quote a slightly better making charge for cash because card fees eat into their margin, and a few add a small percentage for card payments; ask before you decide. For bars and coins, where the margin over spot is thin, a bank transfer is often the only way to get the best quoted premium.

How much gold can a resident buy at once?

There is no retail purchase limit for residents, but anti-money-laundering rules mean shops ask for an Emirates ID or passport copy on larger transactions and may require a bank transfer rather than cash above a certain amount. What limits most buyers is the customs allowance at their destination, which for India is 20 g for men and 40 g for women after one year abroad, with duty above that.

Should I buy 22K or 21K jewellery?

Both are high purity and both resell well in the UAE. 22K (916) is the standard for Indian, Pakistani and Sri Lankan designs and is what most South Asian buyers expect to inherit or sell at home. 21K (875) is the Gulf and Levantine preference, slightly harder-wearing, and is priced lower per gram. Choose based on where you expect to sell or pass the piece on, then compare making charges within that karat.
