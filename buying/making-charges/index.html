Gold Making Charges in Dubai: How They Are Calculated and How to Pay Less

Every gram of gold in the UAE is priced from the same public rate, so the making charge is the only part of a jewellery bill that a shop truly controls. It pays for the goldsmith's time, the design, the finishing, the rent and the retailer's margin, and it is where the difference between a fair deal and an expensive one lives. Understanding how making charges on gold are calculated turns you from a price-taker into a negotiator.

This guide explains the two ways shops quote the charge, shows the arithmetic with a worked example, decodes the famous zero making charge promotions and gives a practical sequence for getting the number down without souring the conversation.

Quick answer

  • Making charges are the shop's fee for labour and design, added on top of the metal value.
  • They are quoted per gram or as a percentage of the gold value; always convert to per gram.
  • Zero making charge offers are real but usually tied to minimum weights or selected designs.
  • Plain machine-made pieces carry the lowest charge; handmade and stone-set pieces the highest.
  • Making charges are rarely recovered when you sell, so a lower charge protects your resale.

What a making charge actually covers

A finished bangle is not just metal. Someone alloyed the gold to 21K or 22K, cast or drew it, shaped it, soldered the joints, polished it, set any stones, stamped the hallmark and displayed it under lights in a shop with air conditioning and security. The making charge, also called labour, wastage or workmanship on some invoices, is the fee for all of that.

It sits on top of the metal value, which is simply weight in grams multiplied by the board rate for the karat. The four-karat strip below is the metal layer that no shop can change:

Three things follow from this structure. First, two pieces of identical weight and karat can carry very different making charges depending on complexity and brand. Second, the charge is a cost you pay once and usually never see again, because a buyer of second-hand gold pays for metal, not craft. Third, since the metal layer is public, any shop that claims a special "low rate" is almost always just moving part of the making charge into the per-gram figure. Learn to read the two lines separately and the trick stops working.

How are making charges calculated? Two methods, one answer

Shops in the UAE quote making charges in one of two ways, and both are legitimate as long as the method is written on the invoice.

Flat fee per gram

The charge is a fixed number of dirhams for every gram of gold in the piece. A 20 g chain at a per-gram charge of AED 15 adds AED 300 to the metal value regardless of where the gold rate is that day. This method is common in the Deira souk, in Sharjah's Rolla and in most Indian and Pakistani family shops.

Percentage of the gold value

The charge is a percentage of the metal value. The same 20 g chain at 8% adds 8% of whatever the gold is worth today, so the charge rises and falls with the rate. Branded chains and mall jewellers often prefer this, and it is standard on designer and diamond-set pieces.

Converting between them

  1. Take the board rate per gram for the karat of the piece.
  2. Multiply by the percentage to get the per-gram equivalent.
  3. Compare that number with the flat per-gram quote from the other shop.

When the gold rate is high, a percentage charge becomes expensive in dirhams while a flat per-gram fee stays put; when the rate is low, the reverse. Always ask for both quotes to be expressed as dirhams per gram on the day, then the comparison is honest. The calculator gives you the metal value instantly so the conversion takes seconds.

Zero making charges on gold in Dubai: what the offer really means

Banners promising zero making charge gold jewellery in Dubai appear before Eid, during the Dubai Shopping Festival, around Diwali and whenever a chain opens a new branch. They are genuine, in the sense that the invoice will show no making line, but the economics have to work somehow, and they usually do in one of these ways.

  • Selected designs only. The offer applies to a defined range, often older stock or simpler machine-made pieces.
  • Minimum weight or spend. The charge is waived above a threshold, so the shop still earns on the volume.
  • A slightly higher per-gram rate. Some promotions quietly price the gold above the board rate. Compare the rate line with the live rate before celebrating.
  • Stones priced generously. On stone-set pieces the margin moves into the stone line.
  • Exchange terms. Old gold brought in exchange may be valued below the board rate during the offer.

None of this makes the offers bad. A plain 22K chain bought at a true zero making charge with the correct metal rate is as good as gold buying gets. The point is to verify the rate line and the weight, and to check whether the piece you want is actually inside the promotion rather than next to it.

During a zero making charge promotion, photograph the shop's rate board and the live rate on your phone at the same moment. If the invoice rate is higher than both, the "free" labour has simply been renamed.

Negotiating making charges without losing the shop's goodwill

In the souks of Deira, Bur Dubai, Sharjah and Ajman, negotiating the making charge is expected and even respected. In a branded mall store the room is smaller but rarely zero. A sequence that works in both:

  1. Know the metal value before you speak. Weight times karat rate, on your phone. Everything above it is the conversation.
  2. Ask for the charge per gram, in writing or on the calculator the salesperson is holding. A vague total is the end of a negotiation, not the start.
  3. Compare at least two similar pieces elsewhere. In the souk this takes ten minutes. Tell the second shop the first shop's figure; tell the third shop the best of the two.
  4. Buy more than one piece from the same counter. Shops cut the per-gram charge for a set or for two bangles far more readily than for one ring.
  5. Offer to pay by transfer or cash. Card fees come out of the making charge, so a small discount is often available.
  6. Ask about exchange terms as part of the deal. Full metal value on future exchange is worth more than a few dirhams off today.

Be polite, be specific and be willing to walk away slowly. The shops that compete hardest are the ones with the lowest rent and the highest volume, which is why the Deira souk and the Sharjah souk remain the benchmark for plain pieces.

Making charges and resale: the cost you never get back

When you sell gold jewellery in the UAE, the buyer weighs it, confirms the karat and pays for the metal, usually at or slightly below the board rate. The making charge you paid is gone. This is not a Dubai quirk; it is true everywhere, but it matters more here because so many residents buy gold specifically as a store of value.

The arithmetic is simple. A piece bought with a making charge equal to a small fraction of its metal value returns almost everything on resale once the rate has moved up a little. A piece bought with a charge equal to a third of its metal value needs a much larger rise in the gold price before you break even. That is why experienced buyers who want both wearability and value gravitate to plain 21K and 22K pieces with low per-gram charges, and keep bars and coins for pure savings.

One exception softens the rule: exchange. Many shops will take back a piece they sold you and credit the full metal value, and some credit part of the original making charge, against a new purchase. Keep the invoice, because that policy is only honoured with it. Where making charges are lowest: souk, mall, factory and emirate

Since the metal price is fixed across the UAE, geography only affects the making charge, and the pattern is consistent.

Traditional souks in Deira, Meena Bazaar, Rolla in Sharjah and the Ajman souk have the lowest charges on plain pieces. Rents are modest, shops are small and side by side, and competition is visible from the doorway.

The Gold & Diamond Park on Sheikh Zayed Road houses manufacturers who sell directly, which can undercut retail on custom orders, diamond work and larger quantities, though single plain pieces are not always cheaper than the souk.

Mall jewellers and brands carry the highest standard charges but run the most aggressive promotions, so their effective price swings with the calendar.

By emirate, Sharjah, Ajman and Al Ain often match or beat Dubai on labour for plain gold because overheads are lower; Abu Dhabi's Madinat Zayed centre is competitive for Gulf designs. If you live in the Northern Emirates, there is no need to travel to Deira for a better metal price, only for a wider choice. Questions people ask

What is a reasonable making charge for gold in Dubai?

There is no official figure, and it varies with design, karat, weight and season. As a guide, plain machine-made 21K and 22K chains and bangles in the souks carry the lowest per-gram charges, handmade bridal pieces sit in the middle, and branded or diamond-set 18K pieces at the top. The only reliable test is to ask three shops for a per-gram quote on a comparable piece on the same day and treat the lowest as the market.

Is a percentage or a per-gram making charge better?

Neither is better in itself; what matters is the dirham-per-gram equivalent on the day. A percentage charge rises when the gold rate rises, so in a high-rate period a flat per-gram fee is often cheaper. In a low-rate period the percentage can be the better deal. Convert the percentage by multiplying it by the board rate for your karat, then compare like with like.

Are zero making charge offers a scam?

No, they are usually genuine, but they come with conditions: selected designs, minimum weights, a slightly higher rate line or stones priced at the shop's discretion. Verify the per-gram rate on the invoice against the live rate, confirm the piece you want is inside the promotion and check the exchange terms. Done that way, a zero making charge purchase of a plain piece is excellent value.

Do I pay VAT on the making charge?

Yes. The 5% VAT on jewellery is charged on the full invoice, which means the metal value plus the making charge plus any stone charge. Tourists can reclaim that VAT at departure through the Planet scheme, which effectively refunds the tax on the making charge as well. Investment bars and coins of 99% purity or more are zero-rated and typically have no making charge, only a small premium.

Can I get the making charge back when I sell?

Almost never in a cash sale; buyers pay for the metal only. The exceptions are exchange schemes, where the original shop credits full metal value and sometimes part of the making charge against a new purchase, and certain brand buy-back guarantees. Keep the original invoice, because every such policy depends on it, and prefer low-charge plain pieces if resale value is important to you.

Why is the making charge higher on small pieces?

Because the goldsmith's time does not scale down with weight. Setting a clasp, polishing and hallmarking a 2 g pair of earrings takes nearly as long as doing the same on a 10 g pair, so the per-gram labour works out higher on the lighter piece. If you are buying for value rather than for a specific design, a single heavier piece will usually carry a lower per-gram charge than several light ones.
