How Much Gold Can I Carry from Dubai to India? Customs Limits and Duty Explained

Every flight from Dubai to Kochi, Mumbai or Delhi carries passengers wearing a little more gold than they left with. Indian customs expects that and has clear rules for it: a modest duty-free jewellery allowance for people who have genuinely lived abroad, a weight ceiling beyond which gold becomes a commercial import, and duty on everything in between. The rules are published, the arithmetic is simple, and the penalties for ignoring them are not.

This guide sets out who qualifies for the allowance, what counts, how the duty is calculated after the July 2024 cut, and how to walk through the red channel with your paperwork in order.

Quick answer

  • Returning residents abroad over a year can bring jewellery duty-free: 20 g up to ₹50,000 for men, 40 g up to ₹100,000 for women.
  • The allowance is for jewellery only; bars and coins always attract duty.
  • Above the free limit you pay basic customs duty, cut to 6% in July 2024, plus cess; check the latest CBIC notice.
  • Declare on the red channel with your Dubai invoice; undeclared gold can be seized and penalised.
  • Lower duty has narrowed the Dubai advantage, so run the sums before buying heavy sets to carry home.

Indian customs gold limit at a glance

The free allowance depends on who you are and how long you have been outside India, and it applies to jewellery you are bringing as personal baggage, not to bars or coins.

Two points trip people up. The limit is both a weight and a rupee value, and you must satisfy both; a 40-gram 22K necklace at today's prices is worth far more than ₹100,000, so in practice the value cap bites long before the weight does. And the allowance is per eligible adult, not per family, so a couple cannot pool it onto one person's bangle set.

What counts as jewellery, and who counts as a returning resident

Jewellery versus bars and coins

Customs treats ornaments meant to be worn, including bangles, chains, rings and earrings, as jewellery. Gold bars, biscuits, coins and raw gold are not jewellery, however small, and get no free allowance at all. A 10-gram bar from a souk counter is dutiable from the first gram, which is one reason our gold bars page suggests leaving investment metal in the UAE or buying it in India.

The one-year test

The free jewellery allowance is for Indian passengers returning after more than a year abroad. Customs can check your passport stamps and may ask for proof of residence abroad, such as a UAE residence visa or Emirates ID. A six-month stay opens the door to bringing a larger quantity of gold on payment of duty, but not to the free allowance.

Personal use ceiling

There is also an overall limit on how much gold a passenger may bring as baggage even with duty paid, historically expressed as one kilogram for eligible passengers. Above that, gold is a commercial import needing an importer's licence and the paperwork described on our wholesalers page. Rules are revised, so confirm the current figure with the Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs before you fly.

How custom duty on gold in India is calculated

After several years of increases, the Union Budget of July 2024 cut the basic customs duty on gold to 6%. On top of that sits a cess, and the two together make up the effective rate; the exact total changes when either component moves, so check the latest CBIC notification rather than relying on a figure you read last year. The notional value customs uses is not your Dubai invoice but a tariff value notified periodically, which tracks the international price.

In practice the process at the airport is:

  1. Customs weighs your declared gold and assesses its purity from the hallmark or a quick test.
  2. The assessable value is worked out from the notified tariff value for that purity.
  3. Basic duty and cess are applied to that value.
  4. You pay, receive a receipt, and the gold is released to you.

Duty is charged on the excess above your free allowance, not on the whole amount, if you qualify for the allowance. Keep the receipt with the Dubai invoice: together they prove the gold entered legally, which matters if you ever sell it or carry it out of India again.

Step by step: buying in Dubai, declaring in India

  1. Buy with an itemised tax invoice. Weight, karat, rate, making charge and VAT on separate lines, from a licensed shop. Our how to buy gold in Dubai guide lists what to check.
  2. Claim the UAE VAT refund if you are a tourist. Non-residents can reclaim the 5% through the Planet scheme at departure, provided the goods leave within 90 days; details on the VAT refund page. UAE residents cannot claim it.
  3. Carry the gold in hand baggage, never in checked luggage, and keep the invoice with your passport.
  4. Fill the customs declaration on the airline form or the arrival app where available.
  5. Go to the red channel if you have anything above the free allowance, or if you are unsure. Walking through green with dutiable gold is the offence; declaring and paying is routine.
  6. Pay the duty and keep the receipt.
Wearing jewellery through the green channel does not make it exempt. Customs officers at Kerala and Mumbai airports have seen every variation of "it is just my wedding set"; what they respond to is a resident's passport, a clean invoice and a declaration that matches the scale.

Is gold from Dubai still cheaper once duty is paid?

The famous Dubai discount comes from the absence of import duty, 5% VAT that tourists can reclaim, and fierce competition on making charges. The metal itself costs the same everywhere, as our page on whether gold is cheaper in Dubai explains. When India charged a high duty, buying in Deira and paying Indian duty at the airport could still leave a margin. With the basic duty cut to 6%, that margin is thin and sometimes negative once you add the Indian cess, your flight, and the risk of a valuation dispute.

A rough way to think about it without stale numbers: compare the Indian retail price of the piece you want, including Indian making charges and GST, against the Dubai price net of any VAT refund plus the Indian duty and cess on the excess over your allowance. For plain 22K within the free allowance the Dubai purchase still wins clearly. For a heavy bridal set far above the allowance the gap often disappears, and the real reason to buy in Dubai becomes design choice, hallmark confidence and lower making charges on intricate work.

Check the Dubai gold rate in Indian rupees on the day you shop, because the rupee moves against the dirham and shifts the comparison.

Mistakes that turn a wedding gift into a customs case

  • Splitting gold among non-eligible travellers. Handing bangles to a friend on a tourist visa to carry does not create an allowance; it creates two offences.
  • Hiding bars in luggage or on the body. Concealment is what converts a duty matter into a seizure and prosecution.
  • Relying on an old duty rate. The rate moved several times in recent years; a figure from a forum post may be wrong today.
  • No invoice. Without proof of purchase customs values the gold itself and you cannot show when or where it was bought.
  • Confusing the UAE VAT refund with Indian duty. One is reclaimed on departure from Dubai, the other is paid on arrival in India; they are unrelated.
  • Forgetting that coins are not jewellery. Souvenir coins from the gold coins counters are dutiable in full.

Penalties for undeclared gold can include confiscation, a fine that is a multiple of the duty, and in serious cases prosecution. The honest route costs a few minutes at the red channel.

Carrying gold from Dubai to Pakistan, Nepal, Sri Lanka and Bangladesh

Many Dubai residents fly home to other South Asian countries with the same question. Each has its own baggage rules: typically a small personal jewellery allowance, duty on anything beyond it, a cap on bars, and a declaration requirement. Pakistan and Bangladesh have changed their gold baggage rules more than once in recent years, Nepal is strict on quantity and favours declared jewellery over bars, and Sri Lanka assesses duty on declared gold at the airport. In every case the principles are the same as India's: jewellery is treated more kindly than bars, declaration is cheaper than concealment, and the receipt from a licensed UAE shop is your best friend.

Questions people ask

How much gold can I carry from Dubai to India without paying duty?

If you are an Indian passenger returning after more than a year abroad, you may bring jewellery duty-free up to 20 grams worth no more than ₹50,000 for a man, or 40 grams worth no more than ₹100,000 for a woman. Both the weight and the value cap apply. Bars and coins get no free allowance, and travellers on short trips get no gold allowance at all.

What is the custom duty on gold in India now?

The basic customs duty on gold was cut to 6% in the July 2024 Union Budget, and a cess is charged on top, so the effective rate is a little higher. Because either component can change in a budget or notification, check the latest CBIC notice before you travel rather than relying on a remembered figure. Duty applies only to gold above your free allowance.

Do I have to declare gold jewellery I am wearing?

If the jewellery you are bringing exceeds your free allowance, or you are not eligible for one, yes, regardless of whether it is in a box or on your wrist. Declare on the arrival form and use the red channel. Wearing gold through the green channel with dutiable quantities is treated as non-declaration and can lead to seizure and penalties.

Can I bring gold bars or coins from Dubai to India?

Yes, within the overall personal ceiling, but they are dutiable from the first gram because the free allowance covers jewellery only. Eligible passengers who have stayed abroad for the required period can bring bars on payment of duty; others may face restrictions. Declare them, carry the dealer's invoice, and confirm the current ceiling with Indian customs before buying.

Is it still worth buying gold in Dubai to take to India?

For jewellery within your free allowance, usually yes, because you avoid duty and benefit from lower making charges and the tourist VAT refund. For heavy sets far above the allowance, the 6% duty plus cess, the flight and the risk of a valuation dispute often cancel the saving. Compare the all-in Indian retail price with the all-in Dubai price before deciding.
